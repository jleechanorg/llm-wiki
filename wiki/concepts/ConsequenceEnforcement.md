# Consequence Enforcement

Engine behavior observed on the [[Voyage]] platform ([[2026-08-04-bg3-voyage-campaign-summary]]): the narrator **disrupts or degrades player commands based on ambient conditions in the fiction** rather than always complying. Two instances in the [[NocturneSosuke]] run:

- Nocturne's own ink cloud caused allied Shadow Stalkers to take friendly fire from Asharak and Zevlor — the player's obscurement worked against their own side.
- The "psychic static" from [[Shadowheart]]'s artifact garbled a telepathic assassination command aimed at Zevlor.

## Why it matters

This is the inverse failure mode of sycophantic AI DMing: instead of the narrator rubber-stamping every declared action, environmental state feeds back into command resolution. It complements [[DynamicSituationalModifiers]] (numeric context adjustment) with *qualitative* interference — commands can partially fail or misfire for in-fiction reasons. Distinct from [[NarratorBugCorrection]], which handles cases where the engine gets state wrong; here the engine gets state *right* against the player's interest. A useful benchmark dimension for any AI-DM platform, including [[WorldArchitectAI]]-style systems.
