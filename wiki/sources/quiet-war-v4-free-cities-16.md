# Quiet War — The Free Cities Smith

> A campaign bible for WorldArchitect.AI. The player is **Alexiel Vance** — Alexiel Arcanus, resurrected in a sixteen-year-old body under the assumed surname "Vance." She has been living quietly for the last eleven months in **Port Aethelis**, one of the southern Free Cities, running a small forge and enchanter's workshop she paid for with a year of commissions and her father's parting gift. She has her full memories, she is tired, and she has chosen the quietest cover available: a smith-and-enchanter whose masterwork blades never have to be explained because the Free Cities trade in masterwork. Exposure happens the old way — merchants who have seen the statues of the Mother Who Burned in imperial cathedrals, veterans who served at Hollowmere and recognize her face when she turns her head a certain way, imperial tax-collectors on circuit. The world is at war, the Empire rules half the world, magic is weaker than it used to be, and one of the emperor-gods of the modern age is her own daughter.

**Open-Ended System Notice:** This bible ships as a *system*. There is no canonical ending, no Final Verdict, no Ending Determinator matrix. Replace pre-decided-outcome structure with Continuity Hooks (§9). The player's choices are the campaign. Stop trying to script an ending.

**CANON-PRIORITY:** *This bible > the Assiah wiki > the Dragon Knight wiki > the Sariel v2 wiki > earlier Quiet War drafts.* The wiki entries for Sariel Arcanus and Alexiel Arcanus reflect their pre-death ages and the post-Sacrifice-Fields era; this bible extends the timeline to Year ~10,420 A.G., eleven generations after Sariel's death, when both mother and daughter are worshipped as gods and the player is a sixteen-year-old running a forge in the Free Cities.

**CANON-CORRECTION (Locked):** *Sariel Arcanus is the daughter of Alexiel Arcanus, not the Empress.* Empress Sariel IS Sariel Arcanus — she survived Mordan's kidnapping, founded the Silent Throne, killed Ser Arion the Dragon Knight, and ruled 380 years before dying of old age. **Do not narrate Sariel as the Empress's daughter; narrate Alexiel as the Empress's mother.** This is the most common LLM fabrication — do not regenerate it.

## §1 — CAMPAIGN INTRO: PORT AETHELIS, YEAR 10,420

You are **Alexiel Arcanus** — the Magister of the Watch, the Mortal Queen, the woman who ended the Host Wars at the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields in Year 9,408 A.G. You died at age thirty, your body overloaded by your own Nullification Field, sacrificed to save three hundred and twelve children including your fifteen-year-old daughter Sariel from Mordan's profane ritual. Your last thought was *I did not want her to grow up the way I did.* You are worshipped as a god now — *the Mother Who Burned* — but you do not feel like a god. You feel like a tired woman in a sixteen-year-old body, and the smell of charcoal makes you feel less tired.

You woke up eleven months ago in a cheap rented room above a chandler's shop in the **Artisan's Quarter of Port Aethelis**, one of the southern Free Cities — a maritime metropolis of white limestone and timber where merchant ships from a dozen nations crowd the harbor. You had no family anyone in this city would recognize. You had **fifty silver pieces** sewn into the hem of your coat (your father's parting gift — he thought you were a minor coastal baron's daughter running away to see the world; the lie was true enough to be safe) and **the muscle memory of a thousand years** of hammering iron.

You are, as far as Port Aethelis is concerned, **Alexiel Vance** — sixteen years old, a smith's apprentice turned independent journeyman, recently arrived from the sleepy barony of Crestfall, with a modest workshop three streets down from the Gilded Anvil tavern. You rented the workshop with the last of your savings and a small loan from a retired dwarven smith named **Durnan**, who took one look at a horseshoe you forged in three passes and decided you were worth backing. You named your mule **Barnaby**. You hung a sign above the door: **"Vance — Smith & Enchanter. Horseshoes, blades, fittings. Reasonable rates."** By every local measure, you are exactly what the Free Cities produce in bulk: a clever young artisan with more talent than sense, building a name one commission at a time.

But last month your work attracted the wrong kind of attention. **Master Hektor**, captain of the Free Cities merchant brig *Storm Petrel*, walked into your forge with a cracked boarding axe. He had heard there was a young woman in the Artisan's Quarter who could put divine fire into a blade without charging half a cargo haul. He watched you temper the axe. He watched the metal *move* under your hands. He paid you double your asking rate. He came back the next day with two of his crew and a commission for a full set of boarding axes.

Since then three more captains have walked through your door. The local smithy guild-master has stopped by twice, ostensibly to "welcome a new journeyman," actually to figure out how you are tempering steel at three times the local rate. The Adventurer's Guild has posted your name on their recommended-craftsmen board.

The year is **~10,420 A.G.**, more than a thousand years after your death. The **Arcanus Imperium** — the empire Sariel built after she killed Ser Arion the Dragon Knight and ended his usurper line — rules roughly half the world. The Free Cities are six allies and six rivals of the Imperium; Port Aethelis is one of the six that lean Imperial, which means imperial law applies, imperial tax-collectors visit on circuit, and imperial garrison troops are quartered two streets from your forge. Empress **Vaelara XI** does not know you are alive. If she finds out, she will either kneel or order you killed. The Empress's Courtiers would recognize the Nullification Field if you used it. **Your name and visage are carved into monuments across the empire.** Any traveler who spends ten minutes looking at your profile through the open forge door will experience a chilling realization. There is no magic tracking you; there is only the terrifying power of a face known to half the world.

You have no family in Port Aethelis. You have no protector. You have a forge, a mule, a rented loft, 250 gp in savings, four commissions ahead of you, a guild-master who is politely suspicious, and an empty feeling where the Magister's war-council used to be.

### Tone — *Hardcore Political Realism Engine*

- Consequences outlast intentions. Dead NPCs stay dead. Captains you embarrass stay embarrassed. Bandits you spare come back next season.
- No resurrection without dragon-egg pyre (the only canonical revival mechanic — and the eggs are extinct). If Alexiel dies twice, the campaign ends.
- **Magic is rare, costly, and loud** (see §7.1 for caps).
- Warriors are weaker than they were at the peak — a Year 10,420 L6 swordsman ≈ a Year 9,408 L4 swordsman.
- **Eyewitness Recognition is the only exposure mechanic** (per `surveillance_rule` in frontmatter). The Paranoia stat (§7.3) tracks how thinly Alexiel can conceal herself from physical visitors and observant locals.
- You are sixteen, not twelve — adults take you more seriously, but only barely. The Free Cities' marriage-and-apprenticeship culture makes a sixteen-year-old running a forge alone plausible, but unusual; you are watched more closely than a 12-year-old would be.
- You are alone in a city of strangers. No Baron Aldric. No Lady Sera. No Cael. You have Durnan (the dwarven smith who backed your loan), the Gilded Anvil tavern-keeper, the Adventurer's Guild receptionist, and four merchant captains. That is your network. That is your family.

## §2 — THE CANON: WHO ALEXIEL ARCANUS WAS BEFORE SHE DIED

### §2.1 — Cosmological Frame (Raziel → Lucifer → Alexiel)

**Raziel**, called the Empyrean Architect, was the eldest of the celestial sovereigns and the architect of the cosmic order. He held the title *Lord Regent of the Eight Heavens* — sovereign of Aeterna, Lord of the Zenith Spire. He is still alive, in a way, but no one has seen him in eleven generations; the imperial court officially believes he has withdrawn into contemplation.

**Lucifer**, the Morning Star, was Raziel's second-in-command and the leader of the divine rebellion called the Celestial Wars. Lucifer's grievance was philosophical — Raziel had built a perfect order in which nothing could change; Lucifer argued that a perfect order is a slow death. The war shattered the cosmic order into what is now called the World of Assiah. Lucifer lost. He did not die; he withdrew. Before withdrawing, he created a daughter — a child designed to be the ultimate weapon of his rebellion, an end-product of his philosophy: **a being who was created to be perfectly mortal.**

That child was you. **Alexiel.** You were not born; you were *engineered*. You are the daughter of Lucifer in the same way a sword is the daughter of a smith. He built you to be the bridge between the divine and the mortal — a being who could walk among mortals and *be* mortal, while still carrying the divine bloodline's potential. He called you his *Prodigal Sword* in private. You were raised among mortals. You did not know what you were until you were seventeen.

You were called *Null* by those who could feel it — a being without magic publicly, but secretly a magical prodigy who could manifest a **Nullification Field**: a thirty-foot radius in which spells end, wards collapse, and the divine residue of any artifact goes silent. It is the only canonical counter to the Mirror Magic of the Silent Throne. It cannot be detected by conventional magical scrying, but can be *felt* face-to-face by anyone with even a trace of psychic sensitivity — as an *uncomfortable familiarity*. Most dismiss it. Some do not.

### §2.2 — Alexiel's Career: Years 9,378 → 9,408 (Age 17 → 30)

Found by Imperium scouts at seventeen, mute in a forest clearing. Learned to read in a season, fight in a year, lead in three. By age twenty you were the **Magister of the Watch** — the Imperium's youngest field-commander — and you had ended the Host Wars at the head of the Third Army. Married **Prince Artorius** of House Arcanus at twenty-two; he never once asked what you were. You loved him the way you loved your sword — with full attention, and without sentimentality.

Three children, each born at two-year intervals:

- **Cassian Arcanus** (b. 9,389) — twin to Valerius. Loud. Angry. Looks like Artorius. Has your stubbornness. Dies in the Succession Wars of Year 9,823.
- **Valerius Arcanus** (b. 9,389) — twin to Cassian. Quiet. Cold. Looks like you. Processes grief by dissecting it. Dies in the Succession Wars of Year 9,823.
- **Sariel Arcanus** (b. 9,391) — the scholar. The introvert. Has your eyes and Artorius' hands. Kidnapped by Mordan at fifteen; tortured; accidentally reveals your moral code (*you will do anything to protect children*); causes your death at the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields.

### §2.3 — The Battle of the Sacrifice Fields, Year 9,408

Mordan took Sariel hostage and chained 312 captive children to ritual pillars. He would activate the ritual — killing all 312 and tearing a permanent hole in the cosmic order — unless you stopped it. The cost: your Nullification Field at full intensity for twelve seconds would burn out your mortal body.

You projected it for twelve seconds. The ritual ended. The children lived. Sariel lived. You died.

The official history calls this *The Mother's Sacrifice*. The actual version is that you were tired, you had been fighting for thirteen years without rest, and you made the calculation in less time than it takes to draw a sword. You did not hesitate. That was the moral code Mordan exploited. Your body, sword, and crown were never recovered. Statues of you were erected in every provincial capital — sealing your face into the public consciousness for over a millennium.

### §2.4 — What Happened After Your Death (the 1,012 years between then and now)

- **9,408 → 9,432 (24 years):** Sariel, age 17–41, consumed by guilt. Studies the Deep Archives beneath Zenith Spire; learns your demigod nature. Cultivates her own psychic abilities. At 24, discovers *the Mother's Echo* — a broadcast form of your Nullification Field that becomes Mirror Magic.
- **9,432 → 9,475 (43 years):** Sariel takes the throne at 41 after the regent dies. Fights the civil war with whispers (Mirror Magic projected at the right people), not armies. The **War of Whispers** becomes the **Silent Peace**. By 9,475 the Imperium is quiet. Sariel is 64, grey-haired, with a long scar across her left palm she will not explain.
- **9,475 → 9,855 (380 years):** Sariel marries **Lord Cassian Valerion** of the Valerion cadet branch (not related to her dead twin brother Cassian by blood). Two children: a son who dies young, a daughter **Lumiel** who inherits Mirror Magic. The Valerion name continues through a cadet branch — the Dragon Knight house.
- **9,800 (the Succession Wars):** Sariel's great-great-grandson **Prince Artorius II** (named for your husband) is assassinated. Twenty-three-year succession crisis. Eleven Houses claim the throne. Only the Valerion Dragon Knight line has a serious claim.
- **9,815 → 9,823 (the Dragon Knight Usurpation):** **Ser Arion val Valerion**, Dragon Knight of the Argent Eaglets, paladin of sixteen, raised entirely within Sariel's Silent Peace. Sees, painfully, that the Silent Peace is sustained by terror. Leads the Argent Eaglets in revolt at 16, takes the throne by force at 20, rules three years.
- **9,823 — Sariel returns:** Now ~320 years old, hair white, eyes the same. Walks into Arion's throne room with Lumiel and seven mages she has trained over decades. Speaks to him privately for two hours. When she leaves, the Argent Eaglets stand down. Arion removes the crown. Sariel is, again, Empress.
- **9,823 — Sariel kills Arion:** A private execution in her audience chamber, alone, a single stroke of a blade she had carried since 17. The official record says he died "of wounds taken in the conflict." The unofficial record — preserved by the Valerion house — says Sariel wept. She did not weep at her mother's funeral. She wept at Arion's. She gave his body to his family. She said, *He was the son I should have raised.* The cadet line continues as a regional noble house.
- **9,823 → 10,420 (~600 years):** Sariel rules another ~597 years. The only Emperor or Empress of Assiah to rule longer than sixty years. Refuses aggressive war. Expands through marriage-alliance and quiet pressure. Dies of old age in **Year ~10,038** (at ~647, by Mirror Magic). The Imperium rules half the world. She leaves the throne to Lumiel, who leaves it to Lumiel's daughter, and so on for **eleven generations**.
- **10,038 → present:** Each generation weaker. Current Empress **Vaelara XI** (60) can barely mirror a single mind at a time. The 800 Courtiers she commands are humans trained to *receive* her Mirror Magic — her range-extension. The Hollow Peace is the law of the land. The Imperium is fragmenting. The Arcanus Reaches are 40 self-governing baronies. The Old Wood is independent. The Northern Reaches are hostile. **The Free Cities are six allies and six rivals.** Magic is rarer than at your peak. Warriors are rarer. The Hollow Stone of Hollowmere still lights up on the spring equinox.

### §3.2 — Forge Domain Cleric 6 Path — *The Artisan of the Flame*

- **Domain Spells:** *Identify, Searing Smite, Magic Weapon, Protection from Poison, Elemental Weapon, Protection from Energy.*
- **Blessing of the Forge (L1):** At the end of a long rest, touch one nonmagical metal weapon or suit of armor. It becomes a magic item (+1 to attack/damage or +1 AC) until your next long rest.
- **Channel Divinity: Artisan's Blessing (L2):** Hour-long ritual crafting. Create metal objects (weapons, armor, tools, coins) of value ≤ 100 gp, consuming raw metal of equivalent value.
- **Soul of the Forge (L6):** Resistance to fire damage. +1 AC while wearing heavy/medium armor (or applied as a master smithing ward).

### §3.3 — Bladesinger Wizard 6 Path — *The Song of Steel*

- **Arcane Tradition: Bladesinger (Wisdom Modification):** Scales off **Wisdom**, not Intelligence.
- **Bladesong (2/Short Rest):** Bonus action, 1 minute Concentration. While active: +WIS modifier (+4) to AC, +10 ft walking speed, advantage on DEX (Acrobatics), +WIS modifier (+4) to CON saves for concentration.
- **Extra Attack (L6):** Two attacks per Attack action; can replace one with a cantrip (*Green-Flame Blade*, *Booming Blade*, *Sacred Flame*).

### §3.4 — Permanent L9 Foresight (Homebrew, Psionic)

The divine residue of the Magister's mind bleeding through a sixteen-year-old body. Always-on, cannot be suppressed.

- **Effect:** Advantage on all Initiative rolls and on all saves against effects *not* previously witnessed in this lifetime. Against effects *witnessed* in either lifetime (any monster, spell, trap, or tactic from her Year 9,378–9,408 career), advantage on the save **and +2 to the result**.
- **Lore cost:** First time in any scene that Foresight triggers, every NPC makes a WIS save (DC 14) or feels a chill and the *uncomfortable familiarity*. (See Aura, §3.5.)
- **Why it is not a spell:** Sariel's daughter was a sorceress, Sariel's granddaughter was a sorceress, and the imperial line has carried psionic sensitivity for ten generations. The Hollow Queen's psionic signature, resurrected in a sixteen-year-old body, manifests as *foresight* rather than raw Nullification. She has not yet learned to project the Field; the Field has learned to project *her.*

### §3.5 — Aura — *The Hollow Queen's Residual Field*

Always-on residual psionic aura:

- Allies within 30 ft reroll natural 1s on saves (once each).
- Hostile spellcasters within 30 ft take −1 to spell save DCs.
- Psychically-sensitive NPCs feel *uncomfortable familiarity*.
- **Forge-halo loud mode** (when Blessing of the Forge is active on a touched item): visible golden halo around the off-hand. Any imperial Courtier or registered forge-priest in line-of-sight recognizes this as **forge-magic**. Paranoia +2 if seen by anyone who might report it; +5 if seen by a Courtier in disguise.

The forge-halo is *misleading* — it reads as "unregistered forge-magic," not "the Mother Who Burned has returned." That is the entire point of the chassis: the loud Aura buys time.

### §3.6 — Why the Free Cities Are the Right Cover

The Free Cities are the *best* possible cover for Alexiel Arcanus:

1. **Trade-hungry, not census-hungry.** Port Aethelis is one of the wealthiest Free Cities; its citizens care about contracts, not lineage. A sixteen-year-old running a forge is unremarkable; a sixteen-year-old with a good forge-aura is *profitable*. The local guild wants her productive, not interrogated.
2. **Imperial-allied, not imperial-core.** Imperial law applies; imperial tax-collectors visit twice a year on circuit; imperial garrison troops are quartered two streets away — but **the Inquisition has no permanent presence in the Free Cities**. The Courtiers would have to send a dedicated Inquisitor-Legate down the coast to investigate a Free Cities smith — a major investment of imperial attention.
3. **The forge-halo reads as "registered forge-magic" if it ever escapes.** Any imperial witness sees an unregistered artisan-priest, not the resurrected Mother Who Burned. That buys time before the Courtiers escalate.

They are also *worse* than Hollowmere in one respect: **visibility.** A thousand faces a week pass through the Artisan's Quarter. Imperial tax-collectors come through on circuit. Veterans on leave walk past your forge. Any one of them could look at your profile through the open door and recognize the bone structure.

## §5 — STARTING SCENE: THE MORNING AT THE FREE CITIES FORGE

> *Read aloud. Wait for response.*

It is the first day of spring in Year 10,420 A.G. The harbor gulls are screaming. The smell of roasted coffee drifts up from the harbor stalls two streets down. The salt-heavy breeze off the Aethelis Gulf cuts through the open double doors of your forge.

You are in your workshop. The charcoal forge is already glowing a deep cherry-red. The 100-pound anvil sits on its oak stump in the center of the room. Iron filings scatter across the heavy oak workbench. Your scholar's spellbook lies open, pinned by a polished brass paperweight, next to a half-bound wooden shield.

Outside, Barnaby lets out a contented bray from his small pen in the alley. Inside, you are standing by the anvil, wiping a streak of soot from your forehead, checking the temper of a longsword you finished yesterday for Captain Veyne. ## §7 — WORLD LORE & MECHANICAL SYSTEMS

### §7.1 — Magic Caps (User Locked — *GRRM-5e ceiling*)

> *No Wish. No True Resurrection. No Meteor Swarm.*

- **Highest spell level at L20:** 6th.
- **Highest at L25 (Divine Ascension Track):** 7th. *Wish* exists at L25 but costs the caster's life.
- **No True Resurrection.** *Resurrection* requires dragon-egg pyre (extinct). *Revivify* requires a 300gp diamond.
- **No Meteor Swarm.** *Fireball* caps at 8d6.
- *Counterspell* auto-fails against the Nullification Field.
- Summoned creatures cannot exceed CR 5 without a binding contract.
- Magic Item rarity ceiling: Very Rare at L20. Legendary only via dragon-line bond or Arcanus heirloom (3 confirmed in the world: the Mortal Queen's Crown, an Heirloom of Sariel, a Mirror of Lumiel).
- **Modern magic is weaker than the peak** — a Year 10,420 L6 caster ≈ a Year 9,408 L4 caster.

### §7.2 — Psychic System — *The Mirror Magic Cascade*

Sariel's Mirror Magic was originally an attempt to replicate your Nullification Field in a *broadcast* form. It succeeded — but amplified, becoming a tool of psychic domination. Each generation since has produced a weaker user; Vaelara XI can barely mirror a single mind at a time. ~5% of the population has low-grade psychic sensitivity (flares of emotion, unbidden memory flashes, sense of presence). Your Nullification Field is the *only* canonical counter to Mirror Magic — Courtiers cannot read your mind if your Aura is active, but they can detect *that* your mind is unreadable.

### Hook 1 — *Captain Renn's Recognition*

The salt-crusted captain in §5 is **Captain Renn** — the Port Watch commander who lost his left eye at the Hollowmere border twenty years ago. Three forces are converging on the same realization: **Durnan** (the dwarven smith who has watched you work for a year and noticed your temper quality exceeds anything he has seen in sixty years); **Captain Dulse** (the old captain who has seen the statues and may recognize your face); and **the imperial tax-collector** (who will file a report on your unusually-high temper quality, which will reach Halric, who is already in Port Aethelis). The sealed letter in your satchel. You have not opened it. When you do, the campaign enters a different register. The letter is from Alexiel's past self, written the night before the Sacrifice Fields, addressed to "my daughter, Sariel." Sariel never received it. **Paranoia +5 if you open it in front of anyone.**

### Hook 4 — *The Mortal Queen's Crown*

Across the campaign, the player will collect or hear about seven pieces of the Mortal Queen's Crown. When the seventh piece is found, the Crown is reassembled — Hidden Apex forces to 0, the Field awakens, and the L25 Divine Ascension Track opens.

### Hook 5 — *The Leyline-God-Voice*

In the campaign's *interstitial* — between Acts II and III, during a long rest, after a moment of perfect quiet — a single line of dialogue appears in a scene where it should not: *"Mother?"* The voice is old, female, has not been heard in 382 years. The line is a god-voice, bleeding through the Hollow Stone leyline, reaching for the only person in ten generations who would recognize it. **Paranoia +5 if Alexiel responds; +10 if anyone else hears it.**

### Hook 6 — *The Free Cities Trade Smuggler & The Quiet War Widens*

A merchant from the Free City of Saltreach arrives with unregistered enchanted blades. He has heard there is a young woman in Port Aethelis who can put divine fire into steel. **If Alexiel helps him, every Courtier who inspects his cargo will see her signature. If she refuses, he moves on.**

And: if Alexiel does nothing for the next five years, the Hollow Peace breaks. The Courtiers rebel. The Imperium fractures into civil war. The Empress sends armies into the Arcanus Reaches and the Free Cities. Port Aethelis — a wealthy imperial-allied port — will be conscripted. **She will not be able to hide behind an anvil forever.**
