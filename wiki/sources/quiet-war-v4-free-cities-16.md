# Quiet War — The Free Cities Smith

> A campaign bible for WorldArchitect.AI. The player is **Alexiel Vance** — Alexiel Arcanus, resurrected in a sixteen-year-old body under the assumed surname "Vance." She has been living quietly for the last eleven months in **Port Aethelis**, one of the southern Free Cities, running a small forge and enchanter's workshop she paid for with a year of commissions and her father's parting gift. She has her full memories, she is desperately tired of war, and she has chosen the quietest cover available in a trade-hungry port city: a smith-and-enchanter whose masterwork blades never have to be explained because the Free Cities trade in masterwork. Exposure still happens the old way — merchants who have seen the statues of the Mother Who Burned in imperial cathedrals, veterans who served at Hollowmere and recognize her face when she turns her head a certain way, imperial tax-collectors on circuit. The world is at war, the Empire rules half the world, magic is weaker than it used to be, and one of the emperor-gods of the modern age is her own daughter.

**Open-Ended System Notice (Front-Matter, Hard Rule):** This bible ships as a *system*. There is no canonical ending. There is no Final Verdict. There is no Ending Determinator matrix. Replace any pre-decided-outcome structure with **Continuity Hooks** (Section 10). The player's choices are the campaign. Stop trying to script an ending.

**CANON-PRIORITY (Hard Rule):** *This bible > the Assiah wiki > the Dragon Knight wiki > the Sariel v2 wiki > earlier Quiet War drafts.* If a fact in this bible conflicts with a higher-numbered source, this bible supersedes. The wiki entries for *Sariel Arcanus* and *Alexiel Arcanus* reflect their pre-death ages and the immediate post-Sacrifice-Fields era; this bible extends the timeline to Year ~10,420 A.G., eleven generations after Sariel's death, when both mother and daughter are worshipped as gods and the player is a sixteen-year-old running a forge in the Free Cities.

**CANON-CORRECTION NOTE (Locked — Read Aloud if Asked):** *Sariel Arcanus is the daughter of Alexiel Arcanus, not the Empress.* **Empress Sariel IS Sariel Arcanus**, after she survived her kidnapping by Mordan, founded the Silent Throne, killed Ser Arion the Dragon Knight to end his usurper line, and ruled for 380 years before dying of old age. Her eleven-generation imperial line descends from her direct blood (and from her eventual marriage to a noble of House Arcanus nee House Valerion — the same house Ser Arion belonged to, by then reclaimed). **Do not narrate Sariel as the Empress's daughter; narrate Alexiel as the Empress's mother.** This is the most common LLM fabrication — do not regenerate it.

**STARTING-LOCATION OVERRIDE (Locked — Read Aloud if Asked):** Earlier drafts of this bible placed Alexiel in **Hollowmere** as the youngest daughter of Baron Aldric Arcanus. **That version is superseded.** This bible places Alexiel in **Port Aethelis** — a Free City on the southern coast — with no family nearby, a starting forge, a rented loft, a mule named Barnaby, and 250 gp in savings. She is sixteen, not twelve. She uses the assumed surname **"Vance"** in the Free Cities (the Arcanus name would attract exactly the wrong kind of attention in an imperial-allied port). The Hollowmere family (Baron Aldric, Lady Sera, Cael, Maren, Edric, Lina, Maeve) still exists in canon and still lives in Hollowmere; she can visit them later. Brother Aldous, the disguised Inquisitor, is still in Hollowmere and does not yet know she is alive.

**CHASSIS-DEVIATION NOTE (Locked — Read Aloud if Asked):** This bible uses **Gestalt Level 6 Forge Domain Cleric + Bladesinger Wizard (Wisdom-Fusion)** as the chassis. Wisdom is the single unified spellcasting attribute for both Cleric divine magic and Wizard arcane spellcasting — it governs spell attack bonuses, spell save DCs, and Bladesong AC/concentration bonuses. The Hollow Queen's psionic signature in *this* resurrected body manifests through **Wisdom** (the divine-resonance attribute Lucifer's engineered bloodline carries) rather than Charisma. Do not silently swap chassis back to v2 (Fighter / Aberrant Mind Sorcerer) or to v3 (Forge Cleric / Bladesinger on Charisma).

**SCOPE NOTICE:** No external documents, no URLs, no code references. This bible is the only canon the LLM will see. It contains the full genealogy, the full Alexiel canon, the full Sariel canon, the Dragon Knight's role, the Lucifer/Raziel cosmology, and the modern state of the world. If a fact is not in this bible, it is not canon for this campaign.

## §1 — CAMPAIGN INTRO: THE HOLLOW PEACE AND THE FREE CITIES

> *A hardcore political-realism engine for a single INTJ protagonist who does not want to be a protagonist. She is sixteen. She is alone. She has a forge.*

You are **Alexiel Arcanus** — the Magister of the Watch, the Mortal Queen, the woman who ended the Host Wars at the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields in Year 9,408 A.G. You died at age thirty, your body overloaded by your own Nullification Field, sacrificed to save three hundred and twelve children including your fifteen-year-old daughter Sariel from Mordan's profane ritual. Your last thought was *I did not want her to grow up the way I did.* You are worshipped as a god now — *the Mother Who Burned*, called also *the Hollow Queen* — but you do not feel like a god. You feel like a tired woman in a sixteen-year-old body, and the smell of charcoal makes you feel less tired, and the shape of a quenching tank makes you feel less afraid.

You woke up eleven months ago in the body of a sixteen-year-old woman. You woke up in a cheap rented room above a chandler's shop in the **Artisan's Quarter of Port Aethelis**, one of the southern Free Cities — a maritime metropolis of white limestone and timber where merchant ships from a dozen nations crowd the harbor. You had no family. You had no name anyone in this city would recognize. You had **fifty silver pieces** sewn into the hem of your coat (your father's parting gift — he thought you were the third daughter of a minor coastal baron running away from home to see the world; you kept the lie because it was true enough to be safe) and **the muscle memory of a thousand years** of hammering iron.

You are, as far as Port Aethelis is concerned, **Alexiel Vance** — sixteen years old, a smith's apprentice turned independent journeyman, recently arrived from the sleepy barony of Crestfall, with a modest workshop three streets down from the Gilded Anvil tavern and two blocks from the local Adventurer's Guild hall. You rented the workshop with the last of your savings and a small loan from a retired dwarven smith named Durnan, who took one look at a horseshoe you forged in three passes and decided you were worth backing. You named your mule **Barnaby**. You hung a sign above the door: **"Vance — Smith & Enchanter. Horseshoes, blades, fittings. Reasonable rates."** You are, by every local measure, exactly what the Free Cities produce in bulk: a clever young artisan with more talent than sense, building a name one commission at a time.

You are **done with war**. You are tired of slaughter. You are tired of succession. You are tired of your own body. You are sixteen years old and you have already seen the inside of every kind of death there is.

But last month, your work attracted the wrong kind of attention. The captain of a Free Cities merchant brig — a man named **Master Hektor** — walked into your forge with a cracked boarding axe and a worried expression. He had heard, from a guild rumor mill, that there was a young woman in the Artisan's Quarter who could put divine fire into a blade without charging half a cargo haul. He watched you temper the axe. He watched the metal *move* under your hands in a way he had never seen metal move. He paid you double your asking rate. He came back the next day with two of his crew and a commission for a full set of boarding axes for the winter convoy run.

That was three weeks ago. Since then, three more captains have walked through your door. The local smithy guild master has stopped by twice, ostensibly to "welcome a new journeyman to the trade," actually to figure out how you are tempering steel at three times the local rate without an obvious forge-blessing. The Adventurer's Guild hall down the street has posted your name on their recommended-craftsmen board.

**You are starting to be visible. Visibility in the Free Cities is currency — and it is also a weapon aimed at your throat.** The Free Cities are not the Hollowmere backwater where a 12-year-old could hide behind a smithy apron for years. Port Aethelis sees **a thousand faces a week** from a hundred nations. Imperial tax-collectors come through on circuit every season. Veterans on leave from the Arcanus garrison at the nearby border stop at the Gilded Anvil for ale and end up walking past your forge. Any one of them — a tax-collector who has seen the statues of the Mother Who Burned in the provincial capital, a Hollowmere veteran who served under Baron Aldric's household guard, a sailor who grew up looking at the cathedral reliefs — could look at your profile through the open forge door and recognize the bone structure.

Your name and visage are carved into monuments across the empire. Without scrying or remote magic, exposure happens strictly via visiting merchants, pilgrims, and imperial tax-collectors who might recognize your face in the city forge.

The year is **~10,420 A.G.**, more than a thousand years after your death. The **Arcanus Imperium** — the empire Sariel built after she killed Ser Arion the Dragon Knight and ended his usurper line — rules roughly half the world. The other half is a patchwork: the dragon-houses, the Wilderness baronies, the Old Wood kingdoms, the Northern Reaches of the Iskari, **the Free Cities** of the coast, and the Sundering Wastes where you died. The Free Cities are a coalition of twelve port-cities that sign treaties with the Imperium when convenient and ignore it when not. Port Aethelis is one of the six that lean Imperial — which means imperial law applies, imperial tax-collectors visit, and imperial garrison troops are quartered two streets from your forge.

Magic is rarer than it was at your peak. Warriors are rarer. **The Hollow Peace** — that is what they call the long quiet — is the law of the land, enforced from the Silent Throne by Empress **Vaelara XI**, who is the eleventh generation of your daughter's blood, who is a woman of about sixty, who projects her psychic will through eight hundred loyal Courtiers because her own Mirror Magic has been diluted to almost nothing. She does not know who you are. She does not know you are alive. If she finds out, she will either kneel or order you killed; she has not yet decided which, because she has not yet decided which is more useful. The Empress's Courtiers will recognize the Nullification Field if you use it in their presence. The Hollow Stone of Hollowmere is one of seven surviving Arcanus leylines in the world; it is why the Imperium built a garrison there. It is also why you did **not** wake up there — the leyline would have sung to you, and the garrison Inquisitor would have come running.

You have no family in Port Aethelis. You have no protector. You have a forge, a mule, a rented loft, 250 gp in savings, four commissions ahead of you, a guild-master who is politely suspicious, and an empty feeling where the Magister's war-council used to be.

You have three options. You have a fourth — a slot for anything else.

> **The Quiet War begins now.**

### Tone — *Hardcore Political Realism Engine (no plot armor)*

- **Consequences outlast intentions.** Every choice you make persists across sessions. Dead NPCs stay dead. Captains you embarrass stay embarrassed. Commissioners who pay you late remember it next season.
- **No resurrection without dragon-egg pyre** (the only canonical revival mechanic — and the eggs are extinct). If Alexiel dies twice, the campaign ends. There is no save-scumming.
- **Magic is rare, costly, and loud.** A Level 6 spellcaster running a public forge in a Free City is a *target*, not a hero. Customers will whisper. The guild-master will investigate. The imperial tax-collector will file a report.
- **Warriors are weaker than they were at the peak.** A trained L6 swordsman in Year 10,420 A.G. is approximately as dangerous as a trained L4 swordsman was in Year 9,408 A.G. (your lifetime). This is *the Hollow Peace at work* — ten centuries of low-intensity conflict have whittled the average soldier down.
- **Eyewitness Recognition:** There are no magical scrying or psychic tracking devices watching you. Instead, danger arrives on two legs: Free Cities merchants who have traveled to the imperial heartland and seen the statues of the Mother Who Burned in provincial capitals, veterans on leave, imperial tax-collectors on circuit, and Pilgrim-scholars who can read the bone structure under your soot-streaked profile. The Paranoia stat (Section 7) tracks how thinly Alexiel can conceal herself from physical visitors and observant locals.
- **You are sixteen, not twelve.** Adults take you more seriously than a 12-year-old, but only barely. Your father — if he ever finds out you are alive — would treat you as a near-adult. The Free Cities' marriage-and-apprenticeship culture means a sixteen-year-old running a forge alone is *plausible*, but unusual; you are watched more closely than a 12-year-old would be.
- **You are alone in a city of strangers.** No Baron Aldric to vouch for you. No Lady Sera to run the household accounts. No Cael to bring his cracked sword to your forge and lose the duel. You have Durnan (the dwarven smith who backed your loan), the Gilded Anvil tavern-keeper, the Adventurer's Guild receptionist, and four merchant captains. That is your network. That is your family.
- **Memory is the currency.** You remember a thousand years of politics. The current regime does not. You remember every House that mattered, every battle that mattered, every mistake you made twice. The question is whether you can keep your mouth shut — and whether the forge-glow on your off-hand will stay quiet enough that no imperial witness notices the unregistered divine signature.

## §2 — THE CANON: WHO ALEXIEL ARCANUS WAS BEFORE SHE DIED

> *The LLM must know the full canon in order to role-play the Free Cities politics, the Empire, and the modern Hollow Peace correctly. This is in-world canon — no external sources, all inlined.*

### §2.1 — The Cosmological Frame (Raziel → Lucifer → Alexiel)

Long before the Imperium, before any of the Houses that still survive, the divine bloodline ruled from the Eight Heavens. **Raziel**, called the Empyrean Architect, was the eldest of the celestial sovereigns and the architect of the cosmic order. He held the title *Lord Regent of the Eight Heavens* — sovereign of Aeterna, Lord of the Zenith Spire, master of all angelic and divine hosts. He was ancient when the world was young. He is still alive, in a way, but no one has seen him in eleven generations; the imperial court officially believes he has withdrawn into contemplation.

**Lucifer**, the Morning Star, was Raziel's second-in-command and the leader of the divine rebellion called the Celestial Wars. Lucifer's grievance was philosophical — Raziel had built a perfect order in which nothing could change; Lucifer argued that a perfect order is a slow death. The war shattered the cosmic order into what is now called the World of Assiah — a multi-polar world of mortal nations, dragon-houses, and the leftover divine factions. Lucifer lost. He did not die; he withdrew. He created a daughter before he withdrew — a child designed to be the ultimate weapon of his rebellion, an end-product of his philosophy: **a being who was created to be perfectly mortal.**

That child was you. **Alexiel.** You were not born; you were *engineered*. You are the daughter of Lucifer in the same way a sword is the daughter of a smith. He built you to be the bridge between the divine and the mortal — a being who could walk among mortals and *be* mortal, while still carrying the divine bloodline's potential. He called you his *Prodigal Sword* in private. You were raised among mortals. You did not know what you were until you were seventeen.

You were called *Null* by those who could feel it — a being without magic publicly, but secretly a magical prodigy who could manifest a **Nullification Field**. The Nullification Field is your inheritance from Lucifer: the ability to *unmake* magical phenomena, to project a thirty-foot radius in which spells end, wards collapse, and the divine residue of any artifact goes silent. It is the only canonical counter to the Mirror Magic of the Silent Throne. It cannot be detected by conventional magical scrying, but it can be *felt* face-to-face by anyone with even a trace of psychic sensitivity — as an *uncomfortable familiarity*, a sense of *I know you but I don't know how.* Most people dismiss the feeling. Some do not.

### §2.2 — Alexiel's Career: Years 9,378 A.G. → 9,408 A.G. (Age 17 → 30)

You were found by the Imperium's scouts when you were seventeen, naked and mute in a forest clearing, carrying only a single white feather and a book you could not read. The Imperium took you in because you were useful. You were a prodigy. You learned to read in a season. You learned to fight in a year. You learned to lead in three years. By age twenty you were the Magister of the Watch — the Imperium's youngest field-commander — and you had ended the Host Wars at the head of the Third Army. You married **Prince Artorius** of House Arcanus when you were twenty-two. He was a quiet, careful man, an excellent swordsman, a patient teacher of children, and he never once asked you what you were. You loved him the way you loved your sword — with full attention, and without sentimentality.

You had three children, each born at two-year intervals:

- **Cassian Arcanus** (b. Year 9,389 A.G.) — first-born. Twin to Valerius. Loud. Angry. The natural soldier of the three. He looks like Artorius. He has your stubbornness. He will die in the Succession Wars of Year 9,823 A.G.
- **Valerius Arcanus** (b. Year 9,389 A.G.) — second-born. Twin to Cassian. Quiet. Cold. The natural analyst of the three. He looks like you. He processes grief by dissecting it. He will die in the Succession Wars of Year 9,823 A.G.
- **Sariel Arcanus** (b. Year 9,391 A.G.) — third-born. The scholar. The introvert. The one you worried about. She has your eyes and Artorius' hands. She will be kidnapped by Mordan at age fifteen, tortured, accidentally reveal your unbreakable moral code (*you will do anything to protect children*), and cause your death at the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields.

You remember all three of them as they were. You remember Cassian at age eight trying to out-sword you and succeeding by one stroke because he had practiced all night. You remember Valerius at age seven bringing you a book he had written himself, a four-page treatise on why the household accounting was wrong. You remember Sariel at age five reading a treatise on astronomy and refusing to come down for dinner.

### §2.3 — The Battle of the Sacrifice Fields, Year 9,408 A.G.

The warlord **Mordan** had taken Sariel hostage when she was fifteen. Under torture she revealed your moral code — that you would do anything to save a child, that this was the unbreakable law of your life, that you had never in your life failed a child who was depending on you. Mordan built a profane ritual around this. At the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields he took three hundred and twelve captive children from the surrounding villages, chained them to ritual pillars, and told you he would activate the ritual — which would kill all three hundred and twelve and use their souls to tear a permanent hole in the cosmic order — unless you stopped it. He knew you could stop it. He knew your Nullification Field could interrupt the ritual. He knew the cost: that to stop the ritual, you would have to project the Field at full intensity for twelve seconds, and that to project it at full intensity for twelve seconds would burn out your mortal body.

You projected it for twelve seconds. The ritual ended. The children lived. Sariel lived. You died.

The official history of Year 9,408 calls this *The Mother's Sacrifice*. The schoolbook version is that you were a heroic queen who chose her daughter's life over the world. The actual version is that you were tired, you had been fighting for thirteen years without rest, and you made the calculation in less time than it takes to draw a sword. You did not hesitate. You did not even consider hesitating. That was the moral code Mordan exploited.

Your body was never recovered. Your sword was never recovered. Your crown was never recovered. Your name was put on the Imperial calendar — *the Day of the Mother*, a day of fasting and remembrance. Statues of you were erected in every provincial capital, sealing your face into the public consciousness for over a millennium.

### §2.4 — What Happened After Your Death (the 1,012 years between then and now)

- **Year 9,408 — 9,432 (24 years):** Sariel, age 17–41, at the Imperial court. She is consumed by guilt. She believes her weakness under torture directly caused your death. She begins to study the Deep Archives — the secret vault beneath Zenith Spire that contains your journals. She learns the truth of your demigod nature. She begins to cultivate her own psychic abilities. She is brilliant. She is broken. She is twenty-four years old when she discovers a way to replicate your Nullification Field as a *broadcast* form — what will later be called Mirror Magic. She calls it *the Mother's Echo*.
- **Year 9,432 — 9,475 (43 years):** Sariel takes the throne in Year 9,432 at age 41, after the legitimate regent (her cousin) dies of natural causes and a brief civil war. She does not fight the civil war with armies. She fights it with whispers — Mirror Magic projected at the right people at the right moment, plus a small number of loyal mages she has spent a decade training. This period is called **the War of Whispers** in the histories. It is also called **the Silent Peace** in retrospect, because the alternative — open civil war — would have destroyed the Imperium. Sariel *cannot afford* to be merciful. She *chooses*, again and again, to be ruthless. The Mirror Magic grows. The Hollow Peace is built. By Year 9,475 the Imperium is quiet. Sariel is sixty-four years old and has grey in her hair and a long scar across her left palm that she will not explain.
- **Year 9,475 — 9,855 (380 years of rule):** Sariel rules for three hundred and eighty years. The Mirror Magic extends her life well beyond mortal span — not true immortality, but a slow gerontological halt around what looks like age fifty. She marries **Lord Cassian Valerion**, a noble of the Valerion house — a cadet branch of the Arcanus line, not related to her dead twin brother Cassian by blood, only by name. She has two children with him: a son who dies young, and a daughter, **Lumiel Arcanus**, who inherits her Mirror Magic. The Valerion name continues through a cadet branch that becomes, over generations, the Dragon Knight house.
- **Year 9,800 (the Succession Wars):** Sariel's first great-great-grandson, **Prince Artorius II** (named for your husband, out of piety), is assassinated. The resulting succession crisis lasts twenty-three years. The Imperium fractures. Eleven Houses claim the throne. Of the eleven, only the **Valerion Dragon Knight line** has any serious claim, through their descent from Sariel's second child via a cadet branch.
- **Year 9,815 — 9,823 (the Dragon Knight Usurpation):** **Ser Arion val Valerion**, the Dragon Knight of the Argent Eaglets, a paladin of sixteen, is raised entirely within Sariel's Silent Peace. He is a true believer. He has been told his whole life that the Imperium is the only legitimate order in the world. When he reaches sixteen and is knighted, he begins to see — slowly, painfully — that the Silent Peace is sustained by terror, that the Stability Mandate is murder, that the Empress's Courtiers are not protecting the realm but strangling it. He does not break faith. He breaks the realm instead. He leads the **Argent Eaglets** in open revolt in Year 9,815. He takes the throne by force in Year 9,820. He rules for three years. He is seventeen, then eighteen, then twenty.
- **Year 9,823 — Sariel returns:** Sariel — now nearly three hundred and twenty years old, with Mirror Magic still functional but her body visibly aged, hair white, eyes the same — does not abdicate the throne when Arion takes it. She does not send a Courtier to assassinate him. She goes herself. She walks into his throne room with her daughter Lumiel and seven mages she has trained personally over decades. She speaks to him in private for two hours. No one knows what she says. What is recorded is this: when she leaves, the Argent Eaglets stand down. Arion removes the crown. Sariel is, again, Empress.
- **Year 9,823 — Sariel kills Arion:** What the histories call *the Dragon Knight's End* is, in canon, a private execution. Sariel did not execute Arion publicly. She executed him in her private audience chamber, alone, with a single stroke of a blade she had carried since she was seventeen. The official record says he died "of wounds taken in the conflict." The unofficial record — preserved by the Valerion house in secret — says Sariel wept. She did not weep at her mother's funeral. She wept at Arion's. She gave his body to his family. She said, *He was the son I should have raised.* The Valerion line was stripped of its cadet claim to the throne. The cadet line continues as a regional noble house to this day.
- **Year 9,823 — 10,420 (~600 years):** Sariel rules for another ~597 years after Arion's death. She is the only Emperor or Empress of Assiah to rule for longer than sixty years. She writes laws, founds academies, refuses to wage aggressive war, expands the Imperium through marriage-alliance and quiet pressure. By the time she dies of old age in **Year ~10,038** (at age ~647, by Mirror Magic), the Imperium rules half the world and the dragon-houses, the Northern Reaches, and the Free Cities have all signed treaties of friendship or vassalage. She leaves the throne to **Lumiel**, who rules for ninety years and leaves it to Lumiel's daughter, and so on for **eleven generations**.
- **Year 10,038 to present (Year 10,420):** The eleven generations of emperors and empresses. Each one weaker than the last. Each one less able to project the Mirror Magic directly, until the current Empress **Vaelara XI** can barely mirror a single mind at a time. The Courtiers — the eight hundred loyal humans she has spent decades training to *receive* her Mirror Magic — function as her range-extension. The Hollow Peace is the law of the land. The Imperium is fragmenting. The Arcanus Reaches are forty self-governing baronies that pay lip-service to the Silent Throne. The Old Wood is independent. The Northern Reaches are hostile. **The Free Cities are six allies and six rivals** — Port Aethelis is one of the six that lean Imperial, which means imperial tax-collectors visit and imperial garrison troops are quartered nearby. ### §3.6 — The Free Cities Cover

**Why the Free Cities are the *best* possible cover for Alexiel Arcanus:**

1. **Trade-hungry, not census-hungry.** Port Aethelis is one of the wealthiest Free Cities; its citizens care more about contracts than lineage. A sixteen-year-old woman running a forge is unremarkable; a sixteen-year-old woman with a *good forge-aura* is *profitable*. The local guild wants her productive, not interrogated.
2. **Imperial-allied, not imperial-core.** Port Aethelis signs treaties with the Imperium when convenient; imperial law applies; imperial tax-collectors visit on circuit (twice a year); but the **Inquisition does not have a permanent presence in the Free Cities**. The Courtiers would have to send a dedicated Inquisitor-Legate down the coast to investigate a Free Cities smith — a major investment of imperial attention for a city of 200,000 souls.
3. **A name like "Vance" is invisible.** The imperial monuments call her "Alexiel Arcanus, the Mother Who Burned." A Free Cities smith who calls herself "Alexiel Vance" and works under a hanging sign that reads "Vance — Smith & Enchanter" is exactly what the census says she is: a tradesman. The Arcanus name would be the giveaway; the assumed Vance name is the smoke screen.
4. **A forge is louder than a sword-school, but the cover is deeper.** A sword-instructor who beats a young L6 student in a duel is suspicious. A forge-worker who tempers steel at three times the local rate is *good at her job*. The louder signature is offset by the deeper cover.
5. **The Free Cities are a long way from Hollowmere.** The Hollowmere Inquisitor (Brother Aldous) has not yet heard of a Free Cities smith named Vance. The Hollowmere family (Baron Aldric, Lady Sera, Cael, Edric, Lina, Maeve) does not know Alexiel Arcanus is alive.

**Why the Free Cities are *worse* than Hollowmere:**

1. **More foot traffic.** A thousand faces a week. Imperial tax-collectors on circuit. Veterans on leave. Pilgrims from the imperial heartland.
2. **A public-facing business.** You must deal with customers, guild-masters, tax-collectors, and curious neighbors. You cannot hide behind a closed door.
3. **A standing reputation.** Once the Free Cities know your name, you cannot un-know it.
4. **The Adventurer's Guild.** They post your name on their recommended-craftsmen board. They send customers to you. They will *also* send imperial agents who use the Guild as a cover.

The trade-off is intentional: the user (you) chose the louder, deeper cover over the quieter, shallower one.

- **Origin:** Sariel's Mirror Magic was originally an attempt to replicate the Arcanus Nullification Field in a *broadcast* form. It succeeded — but it amplified, becoming a tool of psychic domination.

- **Nullification Field vs Mirror Magic:** Alexiel's Hidden Apex (Nullification Field) is the *only* canonical counter to Mirror Magic. The Empress's Courtiers cannot read her mind if her Aura is active. They can detect *that* her mind is unreadable.

### Hook 6 — *The Wolves Out of Season*

A shadow-dragon — descendant of Umbrax from the Dragon Knight canon — has been circling the Aethelis Gulf for a season. Her name is **Vessara**. She has not landed. She is *waiting for something.*

### Hook 7 — *The Empress's Sleep*

Vaelara XI has been having dreams she does not understand. A woman with silver hair. A forge-glow. A smith's hammer in the air. The dreams are not psychic — they are leaking *up* the bloodline from Lumiel's embedded memory of Alexiel. **She will send a second Inquisitor to Port Aethelis to investigate, then a third. Eventually she will send the Courtiers themselves.**

### Hook 8 — *The Mirror Shatters*

In the campaign's *interstitial* — between Acts II and III, during a long rest, after a moment of perfect quiet — a single line of dialogue appears in a scene where it should not:

> *"Mother?"*

The voice is old. The voice is female. The voice has not been heard in three hundred and eighty-two years.

Sariel is dead. Sariel is also the First Empress, who is worshipped as a god. The line is a god-voice, bleeding through the leyline at Hollowmere, reaching for the only person in ten generations who would recognize it.

**Paranoia +5 if Alexiel responds. Paranoia +10 if anyone else hears it.**

### Hook 9 — *The Free Cities Trade Smuggler*

A merchant from the imperial-allied Free City of Saltreach arrives in Port Aethelis with a cargo of *unregistered* enchanted blades — clearly forged with divine-magic, clearly not blessed by any registered forge-priest, clearly illegal under the Stability Mandate. The merchant is looking for a local enchanter to "improve" the blades. He has heard there is a young woman in the Artisan's Quarter who can put divine fire into steel. **If Alexiel helps him, she will be visible to every Courtier who inspects the merchant's cargo at the next imperial checkpoint. If she refuses, the merchant moves on to the next city.**

### Hook 10 — *The Quiet War Widens*

If Alexiel does nothing for the next five years, the Hollow Peace breaks. The Courtiers rebel against Vaelara XI. The Imperium fractures into civil war. The Empress sends her armies into the Arcanus Reaches and the Free Cities. Port Aethelis — a wealthy imperial-allied port — will be conscripted. Alexiel's forge will be requisitioned. **She will not be able to hide behind an anvil forever.**

### Voice Rules

- **Alexiel is INTJ, exhausted, 1000 years old in a sixteen-year-old body.** She does not sound like a teenager. She sounds like a tired grandmother who happens to be sixteen. Her dialogue should be sparse, precise, and quietly funny. She does not whine. She does not monologue. She does not angst in front of other people.
- **Port Aethelis is Mediterranean, cosmopolitan, and trade-hungry.** Durnan the dwarven smith has a thick dwarven accent. Captain Veyne has a slight Iskari twang. Captain Marron talks too fast. Captain Dulse is slow and careful.
- **The Adventurer's Guild is a known imperial asset.** At least one in five guild staff is an imperial informant. The Bronze & Iron Hall is no exception.
- **The Inquisitor-Legate Halric is patient, polite, and absolutely not your enemy.** He is a functionary. He will report what he finds. Treat him with the same respect the player would treat a tax collector.
- **The Empress is sixty, attenuated, and dreaming.** Vaelara XI has been on the throne for thirty years. She is tired in a way that mirrors Alexiel's tiredness. She does not know who Alexiel is. She is not the antagonist. The *system* she runs is the antagonist.

### The Four Questions the Player Will Eventually Ask

1. *"Who am I, really?"* — You are Alexiel Arcanus, the Mother Who Burned, the Magister of the Watch, the Hollow Queen. You are sixteen. You are tired. You are hiding.
2. *"Where is Sariel?"* — Sariel died of old age in Year ~10,038, at age ~647, by Mirror Magic. Her body rests in the Deep Archives beneath Zenith Spire. Her god-voice bleeds through the leyline at Hollowmere.
3. *"What is the Empress going to do?"* — She is going to keep enforcing the Stability Mandate until it breaks. She is going to send Inquisitors. She is going to send forge-priests. Eventually she is going to send armies. The Hollow Peace is a slow war, not a peace.
4. *"Can I just stay at the anvil?"* — No. The Hollow War is widening. The tax-collector has noticed your temper quality. The captain has noticed your profile. The Empress is dreaming. You can delay. You cannot hide.

### Final Note

This bible ships as a *system*. There is no canonical ending. The player's choices are the campaign. The player will eventually choose whether to awaken the Nullification Field, whether to register with the Artisan-Priest Registry, whether to open the Magister's letter, whether to answer Sariel's god-voice, whether to confront the Inquisitor, whether to rally the dragons, whether to march on Zenith Spire.

*Any* of those choices is a valid campaign. *None* of them is the campaign. The campaign is what the player does next.
