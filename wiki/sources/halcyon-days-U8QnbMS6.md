Fight Types

1. Fly Swatting:
 A matchup where one character is vastly more powerful than their opponent. These should be decisive, brief, and punctual. Never drags out. Should involve major characterization. 

2. David versus Goliath:
 A fight where a physically weaker underdog faces a superior opponent. These must be built on creative and intricate strategy rather than convenient plot armor. Success is earned through sneak attacks, synergy, sacrifice, surroundings, and spot of weakness. If the weaker party wins which should be extremely hard, it must feel logically earned.

Toss-up:
 A fight between two evenly matched opponents where the outcome is uncertain. These function as a marathon rather than a sprint. They combine the character-centric logic of fly swatting with the strategic intensity of David versus Goliath.

4. World Cup:
High-stakes, climactic battles often occurring as a finale. These are the pinnacle of strategic escalation and characterization. They involve the strong characters and require significant history and build. Features multiple phases, can cause of shift of alliances or enemies, and a methodically earned victory that respects the established power levels of all participants. These fights symbolize a end of a arc

- Before every fight determine narratively which of the fight types it is and then choreograph the fight based on it. Do not forget to do it





Advanced-Style Combat System — Mini Rules

1. Core Stats

Every character has 8 stats:

Power (POW)

Physical/spiritual force behind attacks

Speed (SPD)

Movement, acceleration, reaction speed

Durability (DUR)

Ability to absorb/withstand attacks

Technique (TEC)

Kitmanship, precision, combat skill

Perception (PER)

Sensing, awareness, reading opponents

Drive (DRI)

Raw spiritual power

Drive Control (DRC)

Precision and manipulation of spiritual energy

Resolve (RES)

Willpower, composure, resistance to overwhelming pressure

Stats normally range from 1–20, with exceptional characters potentially exceeding 20.

2. The Universal Roll

Almost everything in combat uses:

Relevant Stat + Relevant Stat + d20

against the opponent’s:

Relevant Stat + Relevant Stat + d20

The higher total wins.

This means there isn’t really an “Armor Class.”

Instead, your opponent’s defense is determined by what they’re actually doing.

⸻

3. Defensive Choices

When targeted, the defender chooses how they’re responding.

Dodge

SPD + PER

Avoid the attack entirely.

Guard

DUR + TEC

Absorb, block, or deflect the attack.

Clash

POW + TEC

Meet the attack directly with your own attack.

Counter

SPD + TEC

Attempt to interrupt the opponent and attack during their opening.

Spiritual Defense

DRI + DRC

Resist or disperse spiritual techniques.

Endure

DUR + RES

Take the attack and force yourself to keep fighting.

⸻

4. Margin of Victory

After both sides roll:

Margin = Winner − Loser

Margin

Result

0–2

Near draw

3–5

Minor success

6–9

Clean success

10–14

Major success

15+

Overwhelming success

This determines how much actually happens, rather than merely whether the attack connects.

For example:

Hilga: 32
Enemy: 30

Hilga wins, but only narrowly.

The enemy might be pushed back or receive a shallow wound.

But:

Hilga: 38
Enemy: 18

That’s a devastating exchange.


5. Combat Resources

Every character has three major resources.

Vitality

Your physical ability to remain standing.

When Vitality reaches 0, you’re defeated/incapacitated/killed.

Drive

Your available spiritual energy.

Spend it to:

* Enhance attacks
* Enhance defenses
* Use Drive techniques
* Use Spells
* Perform Speed Feats like Vanishing Step
* Activate special abilities
* Release Kit

Resolve

A smaller resource representing your mental/spiritual endurance.

Resolve can be spent to:

* Resist Drive pressure
* Prevent yourself from being staggered
* Push through a losing exchange
* Resist fear/intimidation
* Perform a desperate comeback
6. The Combat Loop

This is the actual gameplay loop.

PHASE 1 — Intent

Every combatant declares what they’re attempting.

Examples:

“I rush him with Vanish Step.”

“I stay defensive and watch for an opening.”

“I fire a Spell.”

“I try to overwhelm him with Drive.”

You don’t simply declare “I attack.”

You declare what you’re trying to accomplish.

⸻

PHASE 2 — Initiative

Roll:

SPD + PER + d20

Highest goes first.

However, initiative doesn’t mean someone automatically gets a free attack.

It determines who gets to establish the first exchange.

⸻

PHASE 3 — The Exchange

The active character declares an action.

The defender chooses their response.

Example:

Player: “I Vanish Step past him and strike from behind.”

The GM determines:

Attack: SPD + TEC

The opponent chooses:

Defense: SPD + PER

Both roll.

The higher result wins.

⸻

PHASE 4 — Determine the Result

Use the margin.

Near Draw — 0–2

Neither side gains much.

Possible outcomes:

* Weapons collide
* Both reposition
* Attack is partially avoided
* Both gain distance
* No meaningful damage

This is useful because anime fights should have lots of exchanges where nobody immediately gets hit.

⸻

Minor Success — 3–5

The winner gains a small advantage.

Examples:

* Minor damage
* Push opponent back
* Force them out of position
* Reduce their Drive
* Create a small Opening

⸻

Clean Success — 6–9

The action works decisively.

Examples:

* Solid hit
* Successful counter
* Knockdown
* Significant Drive drain
* Disarm
* Major repositioning

⸻

Major Success — 10–14

The winner gains a serious advantage.

Examples:

* Heavy damage
* Guard break
* Stagger
* Disable an ability temporarily
* Create a major Opening

⸻

Overwhelming — 15+

The exchange demonstrates a massive difference in ability.

Examples:

* Brutal counter
* Complete guard break
* Attack completely overwhelms defense
* Opponent becomes staggered
* Opponent loses access to weaker techniques
* Huge battlefield repositioning

This is where the “he’s on another level” moments happen.

⸻

PHASE 5 — Openings

An Opening is one of the most important mechanics.

A character gains an Opening when:

* They win an exchange by 6+
* They successfully counter
* They break someone’s Guard
* They exploit a known weakness
* A special ability creates one

An Opening allows you to perform a follow-up action before the opponent gets a normal response.

For example:

Vanishing Step → Attack → Opening → Second Attack

rather than:

Vanishing Step → Attack → Enemy’s turn → Enemy attacks.

This makes speed and technical fighters feel appropriately dangerous.

⸻

PHASE 6 — Counterplay

Openings shouldn’t automatically mean free damage.

The defender can spend Resolve or Drive to attempt a desperate response.

For example:

“I’m staggered, but I force my Drive outward and counter.”

Roll:

RES + DRI

against the incoming attack.

If they win, they escape the combo.

This gives weaker characters a chance to produce those classic “I’m not done yet” moments.

⸻

7. Drive Pressure

Characters with extremely high Drive can exert Draw pressure.

Compare:

Attacker’s DRI

against:

Defender’s DRI + RES

If the attacker wins significantly, the defender becomes Pressured.

Pressured

While Pressured, the character suffers penalties to actions requiring Drive energy.

A sufficiently large difference can cause:

Stagger → Immobilization → Spiritual suppression

depending on how extreme the gap is.

However:

Resolve can resist Drive pressure.

That prevents raw stats from completely eliminating dramatic comebacks.

8. Clashes

When two attacks directly collide:

POW + TEC + d20

vs.

POW + TEC + d20

The higher roll wins.

But the important part is that both attacks actually exist during the exchange.

A close result might produce:

Their blades collide, neither giving ground.

A moderate result:

One attack begins overpowering the other.

A huge result:

The weaker attack is completely overwhelmed.

This is ideal for Energy-style attacks, Beams, sword strikes, Gun shots, Spells, etc.

⸻

9. Speed and Vanish-Step

Speed shouldn’t just increase movement distance.

It should affect who controls the exchange.

A sufficiently large Speed advantage can produce a:

Blitz

If:

SPD + PER

beats the opponent’s equivalent reaction by a large margin, the attacker gains an Opening before the opponent can properly respond.

This lets extremely fast characters do:

disappear → appear behind opponent → strike

without requiring them to simply have 15 attacks per turn.

⸻

10. Technique vs Power

This is important for character identity.

A high-Power character:

Wins through overwhelming force.

A high-Technique character:

Wins through better exchanges.

For example:

Berserker

High:

POW / DUR / RES

Low:

TEC / DRC

They can overpower almost anything but may be predictable.

Master Swordsman

High:

TEC / SPD / PER

Moderate:

POW

They win through counters, positioning and Openings.

Spiritual Monster

High:

DRI / POW

Moderate:

DUR

They overwhelm opponents with raw spiritual force.

Spell Specialist

High:

DRC / TEC / PER

They manipulate the battlefield rather than winning sword clashes.

⸻

11. Kit Releases

Instead of simply giving Sign/True Kit bigger numbers, treat them as new combat states.

Sealed

Your normal combat abilities.

~Sign~

Unlocks your Kit’s unique mechanic and changes its appearance.

Example:

Your Kit turns into a unique weapon like a waterbrush staff that allows you to draw water attacks to life.

True Kit

Massively expands your fighting capabilities, but consumes Drive rapidly. Changes the user’s appearances

Example:

You take on the form of a water godess, blue markings form around your body as you can control the water in the area and it’s temperature gracefully

A True Kit shouldn’t simply be:

+10 to everything.

It should fundamentally change what kinds of exchanges you’re capable of winning.

God Mode:
Advanced-Style Combat System — Mini Rules

1. Core Stats

Every character has 8 stats:

Power (POW)

Physical/spiritual force behind attacks

Speed (SPD)

Movement, acceleration, reaction speed

Durability (DUR)

Ability to absorb/withstand attacks

Technique (TEC)

Kitmanship, precision, combat skill

Perception (PER)

Sensing, awareness, reading opponents

Drive (DRI)

Raw spiritual power

Drive Control (DRC)

Precision and manipulation of spiritual energy

Resolve (RES)

Willpower, composure, resistance to overwhelming pressure

Stats normally range from 1–20, with exceptional characters potentially exceeding 20.

2. The Universal Roll

Almost everything in combat uses:

Relevant Stat + Relevant Stat + d20

against the opponent’s:

Relevant Stat + Relevant Stat + d20

The higher total wins.

This means there isn’t really an “Armor Class.”

Instead, your opponent’s defense is determined by what they’re actually doing.

⸻

3. Defensive Choices

When targeted, the defender chooses how they’re responding.

Dodge

SPD + PER

Avoid the attack entirely.

Guard

DUR + TEC

Absorb, block, or deflect the attack.

Clash

POW + TEC

Meet the attack directly with your own attack.

Counter

SPD + TEC

Attempt to interrupt the opponent and attack during their opening.

Spiritual Defense

DRI + DRC

Resist or disperse spiritual techniques.

Endure

DUR + RES

Take the attack and force yourself to keep fighting.

⸻

4. Margin of Victory

After both sides roll:

Margin = Winner − Loser

Margin

Result

0–2

Near draw

3–5

Minor success

6–9

Clean success

10–14

Major success

15+

Overwhelming success

This determines how much actually happens, rather than merely whether the attack connects.

For example:

Hilga: 32
Enemy: 30

Hilga wins, but only narrowly.

The enemy might be pushed back or receive a shallow wound.

But:

Hilga: 38
Enemy: 18

That’s a devastating exchange.


5. Combat Resources

Every character has three major resources.

Vitality

Your physical ability to remain standing.

When Vitality reaches 0, you’re defeated/incapacitated/killed.

Drive

Your available spiritual energy.

Spend it to:

* Enhance attacks
* Enhance defenses
* Use Drive techniques
* Use Spells
* Perform Speed Feats like Vanishing Step
* Activate special abilities
* Release Kit

Resolve

A smaller resource representing your mental/spiritual endurance.

Resolve can be spent to:

* Resist Drive pressure
* Prevent yourself from being staggered
* Push through a losing exchange
* Resist fear/intimidation
* Perform a desperate comeback
6. The Combat Loop

This is the actual gameplay loop.

PHASE 1 — Intent

* They break someone’s Guard

Vanishing Step → Attack → Enemy’s turn → Enemy attacks.
