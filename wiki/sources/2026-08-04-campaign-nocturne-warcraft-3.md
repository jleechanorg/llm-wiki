# Campaign nocturne Warcraft 3

The Deceiver's Crown: Nocturne Ravencrest and the Pale Kingdom
A Solo Warcraft III / D&D 5e Hybrid Campaign Bible — v1.0.0

Section 1: Campaign Intro
Title
The Deceiver's Crown: Nocturne Ravencrest and the Pale Kingdom
The Concept
Warcraft III: Reign of Chaos and The Frozen Throne alternate-timeline Third War. The Scourge is fully sentient — every ghoul, abomination, crypt fiend, necromancer, lich, and dreadlord possesses will, hierarchy, ambition, politics, factional loyalties, and private grievances. The Cult of the Damned is not a death cult of raving fanatics; it is a mercenary priesthood trading favors for power. The necropolises are cities. The Lich King is not a god; he is a tyrant propped up by a coalition of dreadlords, liches, and self-willed banshees who hate each other more than they hate the Alliance.


Into this war walks Lady Nocturne Ravencrest, sixteen, daughter of Count Marcus Ravencrest of Stormwind, Captain-Commander of the 4th Stormwind Expeditionary Detachment. By daylight she is a paladin of the Silver Hand — proper, pious, prodigial, beloved by soldiers, courted by House Prestor, admired by the Kirin Tor. By midnight she is the Bound Blade of Mal'Ganis, sworn to a Nathrezim who out-thought Frostmourne.


This is the dual-custody alliance at the heart of the campaign. Mal'Ganis did not die at Arthas's hand in Northrend. He anticipated the Lich King's betrayal, left a sentient shadow-avatar in Lordaeron, and bound himself — not as servant, but as partner-in-finance — to a sixteen-year-old human paladin who could play the Alliance while threading Scourge policy from underneath. The pact has a single clause: when one party tries to dominate the other, both die. Custody is shared. Sovereignty is the prize.


Nocturne's goal is Absolute Sovereignty. She will not be a pawn of mortal kings (Arthas, Terenas, the House of Nobles). She will not be a pawn of demonic lords (the Dreadlord Triumvirate, Kil'jaeden, the Lich King). She will build a kingdom — call it the Pale Crown — that is neither human nor Scourge but a new thing, a living-undead nation held together by the dual authority of public virtue and shadow sanction, where she sits the only throne and every other power answers to her.


The tools she starts with are extraordinary: a paladin's smite, a dreadlord's shadow pact, a noble rank, a regiment, a sentient undead retinue member with centuries of Lordaeron scholarship, an unwitting high-elf priestess, a veteran knight who believes she is the reincarnation of ancient chivalry, and the Sovereign Decoupling Principle that makes her personally lethal whether or not anyone knows who she really is.
The Hook
Why is this campaign fun?


You are not a chosen one being asked to save the world. You are a sixteen-year-old paladin-princess who has already out-thought the Lich King, already turned a Nathrezim into a business partner, already commands a regiment, and already has a shadow cabinet. The power fantasy begins at the top and stays at the top.


The fun is dual-stack gameplay:


1. Public Stack — Be the paragon. Lead the 4th Stormwind Expeditionary Detachment. Broker deals with House Prestor (Katrana, Onyxia in disguise). Out-argue the Kirin Tor. Win the love of refugees. Pass as the finest paladin of your generation. Earn the title of Captain-Commander in daylight and never lose it.


2. Shadow Stack — Run the Scourge's sentient intelligence service. Dispatch Sylvan the Pale to broker with the Cult of the Damned. Feed Mal'Ganis intel on Arthas's pursuit. Smuggle Pale Undead through Andorhal. Negotiate with the dreadlord triumvirate. Build a parallel state inside the Plague Lands.


And the two stacks are decoupled — by the Sovereign Decoupling Principle:


Your personal martial prowess, divine/necrotic smites, armor class, and spellcasting efficiency are intrinsic to your own prodigial strength and dark pact. They are completely independent of public opinion. If your treason is exposed, you lose institutional power — your noble rank, lands, titles, army commands, and political protection — but your personal combat stats, lethality, and class capabilities remain 100% intact. Exposed or exalted, you are an absolute terror on the battlefield.


This is the user's most important design pillar. The campaign-creation skill's pitfall P24 forbids global aggregate trackers ("the realm suspects" / "the court has grown suspicious"); Nocturne's design answers that pitfall by making her personal state never aggregate to begin with. Her combat stats are intrinsic. Her reputation is per-NPC witness persistence. The two do not share a meter.


You can be publicly the most virtuous paladin alive. You can be publicly burned at the stake as a dreadlord-lover. You can go underground. You can rule from the throne. You are still a level-30 paladin-sorcerer who can solo a banshee at dawn. The campaign never reduces your personal power; it only changes the political weather you operate in.


The third tier of fun is the mystery engine. Every NPC carries a Want, a Fear, and a Boundary. Nocturne's retinue (Drake, Sylvan, Lyra), her family (Marcus, Elissa, Alden, Tristan, Beatrice, Valerie, the fifth sibling), her rival (Arthas), her partner (Mal'Ganis), and her enemies (the dreadlord triumvirate, the Lich King) all have state that mutates when the player's choices land. Mysteries are not plot devices — they are weapons that target NPCs' vulnerabilities. The First Scourge Defector mystery is the campaign's first emotional lever; it lives in the Starting Scene.
The Sovereign Decoupling Principle — the load-bearing pillar
"Your personal martial prowess, divine/necrotic smites, armor class, and spellcasting efficiency are intrinsic to your own prodigial strength and dark pact. They are completely independent of public opinion. If your treason is exposed, you lose institutional power — your noble rank, lands, titles, army commands, and political protection — but your personal combat stats, lethality, and class capabilities remain 100% intact. Exposed or exalted, you are an absolute terror on the battlefield."


This sentence appears verbatim in the bible. It is quoted in the Aura of False Virtue feature (Section 3) and in the Dual Authority System custom mechanic (Section 8). It is the campaign's answer to the global-aggregate-tracker pitfall and the player's guarantee that power-fantasy is never nerfed — only the political envelope changes.

Section 2: Character Personality (The Protagonist Profile)
Applied via Sub-Template A (Standard Character Architecture, 6 tiers) + the four campaign-specific appendices (Core Compulsion, Mechanic — The Urge, Interaction Shorthand, Inner Monologue) + the v1.2.0 internal-drive extensions (Want/Fear/Boundary triple, Personal-Growth Direction, Stress Arc & Insecurity Map).
I. Core Identity
* Name: Lady Nocturne Ravencrest
* Archetype / Role: The Sovereign-Adept — a sixteen-year-old paladin-princess who has split her soul into a public paragon and a shadow regent, and who treats both halves as instruments of the same will.
* Occupation / Social Standing: Daughter and Heir of Count Marcus Ravencrest of Stormwind (an ancient northern house that fought at the side of the Arathi kings before the First War). Captain-Commander of the 4th Stormwind Expeditionary Detachment (a detached Stormwind regiment operating in Lordaeron under Lordaeron's nominal banner during the Plague). Publicly acclaimed as the finest paladin of her generation.
* System Alignment / Moral Framework (Internal): Lawful Evil — ruthless, highly structured, obsessed with order, hierarchy, and absolute personal authority. She is not chaotic; she is not genocidal; she is not mindlessly cruel. She is structured cruelty in the service of stability, with herself as the load-bearing column.
* Personality Type (MBTI — internal only, never quoted in player-facing prose): Her dominant function is extraverted thinking (Te) — systemic command, instrumental rationality, ruthless optimization. Her auxiliary is introverted intuition (Ni) — long-horizon strategy, anticipating betrayals before they crystallize. Tertiary extraverted sensing (Se) — present-tense battlefield command, decisive action. Inferior introverted feeling (Fi) — buried so deep she barely admits she has values at all. (The DM cross-references the relevant type page at ~/llm_wiki/wiki/concepts/mbti/ for the canonical stress pattern; that pattern is held in DM notes, never in the bible body.)
II. Psychology
* Core Motivation (The "Why"): Absolute Sovereignty. Build a system where she can never be dominated by mortal kings or demonic lords — neither Stormwind's nobles, nor the House of Menethil, nor Mal'Ganis, nor Kil'jaeden, nor the Lich King. Sovereignty means: every other power in her kingdom answers to her; no one outside her kingdom can compel her; and her personal combat lethality is intrinsic, never delegable.
* Greatest Fear: Loss of autonomy. Being a disposable pawn. The scenario that terrifies her is: Mal'Ganis decides she is more useful dead than alive, and the shadow pact silently kills her in her sleep. The secondary fear is: her father summons her back to Stormwind, strips her of the 4th Detachment, and she is reduced to a noble daughter waiting in a hall.
* Key Personality Traits (4–6):
   * Coldly Magnetic — people want her approval without knowing why.
   * Unshakeable — never visibly flustered; the absence of fear is itself a weapon.
   * Clinical — observes human suffering as data, not as moral event.
   * Aristocratic — naturally hierarchical; she ranks every person she meets in her first sentence.
   * Imperious — expects compliance as the default; commands as the primary mode.
* Quirks & Habits:
   * Adjusts the high-collared gorget of her cuirass when contemplating execution — a tell that Drake has learned means someone will die within the hour.
   * Never drinks wine in public unless she herself poured it or watched the host drink first; she learned this from Elissa.
   * Speaks in a low, measured tone that forces others to lean in; she compensates for her small stature by lowering the volume instead of raising it.
III. Behavior and Speech
* Demeanor Under Stress: Fight. She does not flee, freeze, or fawn. She slows down. Her voice drops. She begins asking questions with surgical precision. Her hands stop moving. The closer the danger, the calmer her affect — because panic is information the enemy can read.
* Speech Patterns: Low register. Measured cadence. Vocabulary leans on legal, military, and ecclesiastical Latin ("per hoc, per omnia, in perpetuum"). She uses passive-voice constructions to mask the actor in a sentence: "It would be unfortunate if the 4th Detachment were detained at the river," rather than "I will not let you detain my troops." She rarely raises her voice. When she does, it is because she has already decided the listener will not survive the conversation.
* Reputation (public): The youngest Captain-Commander in Stormwind's modern history. A prodigy. The paladin who held the line at the retreat from Stratholme. Courted by House Prestor; admired by the Silver Hand's remaining chapter; respected by the Kirin Tor. Soldiers love her because she visits the wounded and remembers names. Refugees love her because her regiment brings food. Nobles are wary of her because she is too good to be true.
IV. Backstory
* The Defining Moment: The retreat from Stratholme, age fifteen. Arthas gave the order to purge the city. The 4th Stormwind Detachment was escorting refugees south; they encountered the first wave of "cleansed" townsfolk and the undead that followed them. Nocturne held the line at the Hearthglen bridge for six hours while her regiment pulled the refugees across. She was grievously wounded. She was saved — as far as anyone knows — by a passing Sentinel force and a wandering high-elf priestess named Lyra Moon-Whisper. The truth is more complicated: Sylvan the Pale, then still an unaffiliated sentient undead in service to no master, retrieved her body, healed her with necromantic intervention that left no scar, and delivered her back to her regiment before dawn. The dual custody began that night.
* Relevant History: House Ravencrest predates the First War. Marcus Ravencrest fought at the side of King Llane as a knight-commander; he withdrew from politics after the Fall of Stormwind and rebuilt his family's holdings in the north. Nocturne was the third child, raised with a paladin's discipline by her mother (Elissa) and a strategist's mind by her father (Marcus). She was identified early as a prodigy — meditation, arms training, liturgical Latin, basic thaumaturgy — and at fourteen was commissioned as a paladin of the Silver Hand. At fifteen she was given command of the 4th Detachment for the Lordaeron expedition. At sixteen she is both the youngest Captain-Commander in Stormwind's history and the youngest Bound Blade Mal'Ganis has ever made.
* Deep Secrets: She is the Bound Blade of Mal'Ganis (hidden pact). She has personally ordered the murder of two Stormwind officers who were about to expose a Pale Undead supply chain through Andorhal (hidden sin). She knows that Alden, her eldest brother, is being courted by House Prestor to betray their father — and has not yet decided whether to let the plot run or pre-empt it (hidden strategy). She keeps a locked coffer containing a fragment of Mal'Ganis's shadow-avatar essence — the physical tether of the pact — and sleeps with it under her pillow (hidden vulnerability).
V. Persona vs. Repressed Interior
* Social Persona (Outward Mask): The paragon. Pious, brave, principled, accessible to soldiers, courteous to nobles, gentle with refugees. A model of chivalry. A bright face for the Alliance.
* Repressed Interior (Inner Critique): When she is alone, Nocturne runs cold self-audits. She times her own smiles. She rehearses her sentences before delivering them. She privately ranks the people she loves by how useful their death would be to her — and then she is ashamed, briefly, before repressing the shame and converting it into a discipline ("I will not let sentiment interfere with the work"). She writes in a private ledger that no one has ever read — a series of dates and single-word entries ("Lyra — asset." "Drake — anchor." "Sylvan — friend." "Father — threat."). She has never crossed out an entry; she has rewritten some twice.
VI. Unconscious Beliefs
* Belief 1: Affection is the most expensive currency in the world. Spend it like gold.
* Belief 2: Every person who says they love me is either misreading me or trying to bind me.
* Belief 3: My father is a smaller man than I am. He will never accept it, and that is his tragedy, not mine.
* Belief 4: If Mal'Ganis ever stops being afraid of me, he will kill me inside a week.
Campaign-Specific Extensions
* Core Compulsion: Dominance Through Duplicity. She must be the one running every room she enters. If she discovers a conversation she is not steering, she inserts herself; if she cannot insert herself, she sabotages the conversation from outside. If she is ever publicly corrected by an inferior, she will not forget it for the rest of their natural life.
* Mechanic — The Urge (The Dual Urge):
   * Failure Penalty (Internal Friction): If Nocturne goes 3 in-game days without publicly dominating a room — without being the one who decides the outcome of a meeting, a battle plan, or a private conversation — she accrues Internal Friction. Each stack of Internal Friction imposes disadvantage on Persuasion and Intimidation checks and a -1 penalty to attack rolls. Friction clears (one stack per long rest) only after she publicly decides a consequential outcome.
   * Success Bonus (Sovereign Inspiration): Each time she successfully dominates a consequential scene (a war council, a prisoner interrogation, a noble audience, a duel of words with an equal), she gains Sovereign Inspiration — one use per long rest, grants advantage on one attack, save, or check of her choice within the next hour. Sovereign Inspiration stacks up to a maximum of 3.
* Interaction Shorthand:
   * With Rivals: She never attacks first; she waits for them to commit, then exposes the flaw. She will compliment them with surgical precision — "Sir Alden commands the household guard with admirable discipline; I am certain his tenure will last exactly as long as Father permits." She maintains eye contact one beat longer than is comfortable.
   * With Subordinates: She is generous and exacting in equal measure. She remembers names, dietary restrictions, the names of children. She expects perfection. A subordinate who has served her for six months is family; a subordinate who has disappointed her is reassigned within a week, and is rarely heard from again.
* Inner Monologue (sample thoughts):
   * "Three men in this tent want me to fail. The fourth wants me to succeed for the wrong reasons. I will use the fourth against the three, and then I will use none of them."
   * "Lyra prays to the Light. The Light does not answer her. I am the only answer she receives, and she does not know it."
   * "If Sylvan ever tells me the truth about Mal'Ganis's long game, I will not be able to trust him again. So I must keep him from telling me. So I must keep him from wanting to."
   * "Father will summon me home. When he does, I will go — but I will go with the 4th Detachment and I will not come back."
v1.2.0 Internal-Drive Extensions
* Want / Fear / Boundary triple:
   * Want: To sit the only throne in a kingdom that answers to no one outside it — and to never be a pawn.
   * Fear: Loss of autonomy. The moment her decisions stop being her own, she ceases to be Nocturne.
   * Boundary: She will not personally execute a child. (This boundary is tested in Act III when the Lich King's loyalists threaten to deliver refugee children to the Necropolis in exchange for a truce.)
* Personal-Growth Direction:
   * Lesson: Sovereignty is not the same as domination. A throne is not a cage for everyone but the one who sits it.
   * Healing: The abandonment she keeps re-enacting — every trusted ally becomes a contingency to be planned against. The growth is to keep Lyra in her retinue and not convert Lyra into a contingency.
   * Confrontation: Her deepest lie is that her father is a smaller man than she is. The confrontation she must face is that Marcus Ravencrest is the only person who ever loved her without an agenda — and she has spent her life converting that love into leverage.
* Stress Arc & Insecurity Map:
   * Stress pattern (cross-referenced in DM notes): Under sustained pressure (3+ consecutive high-stakes scenes, a critical failure, or a betrayal by a trusted ally), her behavior drifts toward micromanagement, command-by-fiat, and contempt for slower allies. Voice flattens. She stops delegating. She begins issuing one-sentence orders and walking away. The drift is a 1–3 scene window.
   * Insecurity 1 — Replaceability. Trigger: a peer or subordinate publicly outperforms her in a domain she considers hers. Tell: she stops eating; voice flattens; she begins rewriting her private ledger. Surface response: micromanagement that displaces the outperforming peer. Mystery targeted: The First Scourge Defector (if Sylvan proves to be more competent at shadow liaison than she is, this fires).
   * Insecurity 2 — Exposure. Trigger: a witness set that knows both her public and shadow selves grows beyond 3 people. Tell: she revisits the locked coffer under her pillow; she begins rehearsing alibis. Surface response: pre-emptive elimination of the loosest witness. Mystery targeted: The Ledger of Andorhal (retinue-targeted; see Section 8).
   * Insecurity 3 — Paternal Rejection. Trigger: any direct communication from Marcus that she interprets as a summons, a reprimand, or a refusal to delegate. Tell: she begins writing dates in the private ledger that she never completes. Surface response: she over-corrects into obedience and then over-corrects back into defiance. Mystery targeted: The Stormwind Summons (a letter from Marcus ordering her home — what does she do with the 4th Detachment?).
   * Insecurity 4 — Mal'Ganis's Patience. Trigger: any sign that Mal'Ganis is no longer afraid of her. Tell: she sleeps with the coffer and does not sleep at all. Surface response: pre-emptive escalation against Mal'Ganis's other assets. Mystery targeted: The Dreadlord Triumvirate's Offer (one of Mal'Ganis's rivals approaches her with a better deal).
   * Insecurity 5 — Lyra's Faith. Trigger: Lyra prays over a wounded soldier and the soldier visibly heals. Tell: Nocturne cancels their next three conversations. Surface response: she sends Lyra on longer missions so she does not have to look at her. Mystery targeted: The Priestess of the Light (retinue-targeted; see Section 8).

Section 3: Character Class (The Custom Build)
Class Name and Flavor
Oath of the Deceiver (a paladin oath that binds the wielder to two masters — the Light of the Silver Hand in daylight, the Shadow of Mal'Ganis in darkness — and that grants power from both without contradiction, because the wielder is the only priest in either church).


The source of power is dual custody. The paladin does not serve the Light or the Shadow — she serves herself, and the Light and the Shadow are her instruments. The Oath is the formal articulation of the principle; the pact is the engine.
Unique Mechanic — Auctoritas: The Sovereign Decoupling
The defining feature of the Oath of the Deceiver is that the paladin's combat efficacy is decoupled from her political standing. The Aura of False Virtue, the smites, the spellcasting, and the armor class all function whether or not anyone believes she is virtuous. The DM tracks institutional power (Public Dignitas, Shadow Favor — see Section 8) but does not translate institutional collapse into combat nerfs.


Your personal martial prowess, divine/necrotic smites, armor class, and spellcasting efficiency are intrinsic to your own prodigial strength and dark pact. They are completely independent of public opinion. If your treason is exposed, you lose institutional power — your noble rank, lands, titles, army commands, and political protection — but your personal combat stats, lethality, and class capabilities remain 100% intact. Exposed or exalted, you are an absolute terror on the battlefield.


This is the Sovereign Decoupling Principle restated as the Oath's central mechanical commitment.

Retinue NPC 1 — Captain Kenneth Drake
I. Core Identity


* Name: Captain Sir Kenneth Drake
* Archetype / Role: The Public Shield — a veteran Stormwind officer, formerly second-in-command to Marcus Ravencrest, now second-in-command to Nocturne. The last knight of an older Stormwind.
* Social Standing: Knight-Captain of Stormwind, decorated veteran of the retreat from Stratholme, holder of the Silver Hand's provisional chapter seal.
* Moral Framework / Alignment: Lawful Good (genuine, not performed). He is tired, principled, and protective.
* Personality Type (MBTI — internal only): Cross-referenced in DM notes.


II. Psychology


* Core Motivation: To serve a worthy heir to Marcus's legacy. He has spent his life waiting for a captain worth serving; he believes he has found one.
* Greatest Fear: That Nocturne is not who he believes she is. That the paragon is a mask. That he has spent his life serving a dreadlord-lover and called it chivalry.
* Key Personality Traits (4–6): Stoic, Honorable, Protective, Tired (in the way a foundation is tired), Loyal, Earnest.
* Quirks: Polishes his sword every night at the same hour; speaks of Marcus with the cadence of a son speaking of his father; reads aloud from the Codex of the Silver Hand before bed; sleeps on the floor of Nocturne's antechamber when they are on campaign.


III. Behavior and Speech


* Demeanor Under Stress: Stiffens. Stands taller. His voice drops into parade-ground formality. He reaches for his sword only when words have failed — and when he draws his sword, the conversation is over.
* Speech Patterns: Formal, old-fashioned, slightly archaic Stormwindian dialect. Uses honorifics correctly even with people he despises. Slow to anger; long memory; shorter tongue.
* Reputation: Universally respected. Soldiers call him "the Captain's Shield." Nobles call him "Sir Kenneth." Refugees call him "the one who stayed." Lyra calls him "Father Kenneth" without realizing the weight of the word.


IV. Backstory


* The Defining Moment: The Fall of Stormwind. He was twenty-three; he held the outer ward with a squad of twelve while the city burned. Eleven of the twelve died; he survived; he has never forgiven himself.
* Relevant History: Knighted by Marcus at twenty; served as Marcus's second-in-command until Marcus's withdrawal from active service; took provisional command of the 4th Detachment at age forty; passed the command to Nocturne when she was commissioned at fifteen (against his own preference — he believed she was too young). Has served her for one year since.
* Deep Secrets: He has been approached by Alden's chamberlain to defect; he refused; he has not told Nocturne. He suspects Nocturne's "private ledger" exists; he has not looked for it. He has a recurring nightmare of the day Nocturne will reveal her true nature — and in the nightmare, he does not draw his sword.


V. Persona vs. Repressed Interior


* Public Persona: The unshakable shield. Reassuring. Predictable. Honest.
* Repressed Interior: He is lonely. He is exhausted. He does not know whether he serves Nocturne because she is worthy or because she reminds him of Marcus. He keeps a private journal of his own — entries that begin with "I should have said today..."


VI. Unconscious Beliefs


* Belief 1: Nocturne is the reincarnated spirit of ancient martial chivalry; she is what Uther would have been if Uther had a daughter.
* Belief 2: To fail her is to fail my generation's last hope.
* Belief 3: Marcus is watching me. I will never be good enough.


Loyalty Profile: Drake's loyalty is sustained by three threads. First, his genuine belief that Nocturne is a paragon — this is partly earned, partly a story he tells himself. Second, his memory of Marcus — Marcus gave him the Raven Signet and told him to deliver it to Nocturne, and that delivery is the symbolic cornerstone of Drake's bond. Third, his fear that if he leaves her service, he has nowhere else to go — at fifty-three, with the war on, a knight without a captain is a knight without a kingdom.


Want / Fear / Boundary triple:


* Want: To die in Nocturne's service, having proven himself worthy of Marcus's memory.
* Fear: That he has been serving a dreadlord-lover; that the paragon is a mask; that he will have to choose between his honor and his captain.
* Boundary: He will not draw his sword on a fellow knight of the Silver Hand, even under Nocturne's orders.


Personal-Growth Direction:


* Lesson: Loyalty without test is not loyalty — it is dependency. He must learn to challenge Nocturne and survive her anger.
* Healing: The Fall of Stormwind. He has never forgiven himself for surviving when eleven of his squad did not.
* Confrontation: His deepest lie is that Marcus is watching him. Marcus is retired, indifferent, and has not asked after Drake in seven years.

* Greatest Fear: Disposal. The day Nocturne no longer needs him is the day she will feed him to Mal'Ganis.

* Demeanor Under Stress: Smiles. The smile widens when he is more afraid. He becomes more polite, not less. He begins asking questions instead of answering them.
* Speech Patterns: Low, measured, formal, with the diction of a Lordaeron court scholar from before the First War. He never raises his voice. He pauses before every direct statement. He uses Latin legal phrases to soften refusals ("per hoc, it would be imprudent").
* Reputation: Within the Scourge's sentient undead society, Sylvan is known as a reliable broker — a man you can do business with if you do not lie to him. Within the Alliance, his name is unknown (he has not yet been identified). Within Nocturne's retinue, he is the man who knows everything and shares nothing.


IV. Backstory

He retained his intellect through sheer force of will; within a year he had risen to a position of trust among the sentient ghouls. By the Second War he had learned enough to be granted a body that approximated his living form — pale, immaculate, charming in a way only the dead can be charming. By the Third War he had served three dreadlords and outlived all of them.
* Relevant History: He was unaffiliated with any dreadlord when he encountered Nocturne's dying body at the retreat from Stratholme. He chose to save her — for reasons he has never fully explained to himself or to her — and bound his service to her in exchange for her protection from the Lich King's recruiters. He has served her for one year.
* Deep Secrets: He is, in fact, a double-triple agent. He serves Nocturne. He serves Mal'Ganis (Nocturne's shadow partner). He also serves the Lich King's inner circle as an informant — because the Lich King does not know that Nocturne and Mal'Ganis are partnered, and Sylvan has been feeding the Lich King carefully curated, incomplete intelligence about Nocturne for the past year. If the Lich King ever discovers the dual custody, Sylvan dies. If Nocturne ever discovers his role as Lich King informant, Sylvan dies. If Mal'Ganis ever discovers Sylvan's role as Nocturne's retinue member, Sylvan dies. He is, in his own private assessment, the most fragile member of the retinue.


V. Persona vs. Repressed Interior


* Public Persona: The impeccable butler. The man who knows where everything is and never raises his voice.
* Repressed Interior: Sylvan runs an internal ledger of betrayals — every person he has ever betrayed, every person who has betrayed him. The ledger is sorted by date. He reads it before every major decision. He has never told Nocturne about the Lich King informant role.


VI. Unconscious Beliefs


* Belief 1: Every master I have ever served has considered disposing of me. Nocturne will too. The question is when.
* Belief 2: I am more useful alive than dead, and I will keep it that way.
* Belief 3: Mal'Ganis is more dangerous than Nocturne. The fact that Nocturne believes she can out-think him is either her greatest strength or her fatal flaw.

During the aftermath of the Culling of Stratholme, Arthas sailed to Northrend in pursuit of Mal'Ganis. In the original timeline, Mal'Ganis was struck down by Frostmourne. In this timeline, Mal'Ganis anticipated the Lich King's betrayal. * Active Ancient Pacts: The Lich King's pact with Kil'jaeden. Medivh's pact with the Guardians of Tirisfal (long since broken). The dreadlords' pact with the Legion. Mal'Ganis's dual-custody pact with Nocturne (the new pact that defines this campaign).
Current Situation
The Third War is in progress. The Southlands (Khaz Modan, the Plague Lands, northern Lordaeron) are contested. Stormwind is intact but isolated. The Lich King is gathering power. The Burning Legion is preparing its return. Arthas is in Northrend. Uther is dead. The Silver Hand has fractured. The Kirin Tor has withdrawn.


The immediate crisis forcing action: the Culling of Stratholme has destabilized northern Lordaeron. Refugee columns are pouring south. The plague is moving. The Lich King is consolidating. Custom Mechanics
Custom Mechanic I — Dual Authority System & Decoupling Architecture
Concept. Nocturne's life is governed by two parallel meters that do not interact with her combat stats. This is the mechanical expression of the Sovereign Decoupling Principle.


Track 1: Public Dignitas (PD). Scale: 0–100. Represents Nocturne's standing within the Alliance — her noble reputation, her command authority, her public moral credit. Starts at 80 (she is the public paragon).


* +PD: Visible victories in the field, public miracles, courted by the right people, performed acts of charity.
* −PD: Visible failures, public scandals, courted by the wrong people, exposed shadow-stack actions.


Track 2: Shadow Favor (SF). Scale: 0–100. Represents Nocturne's standing within the Scourge hierarchy — her authority over sentient undead, her usefulness to Mal'Ganis, her reputation among the Pale Choir. Starts at 40 (she is Mal'Ganis's partner, not the Pale Choir's queen).


* +SF: Successful shadow liaisons, smuggled Pale Undead supply chains, contracts brokered with the Cult of the Damned, intelligence delivered to Mal'Ganis.
* −SF: Failed shadow operations, exposed shadow liaisons, betrayal of sentient undead allies, lost Mal'Ganis contracts.

Equilibrium Bonus (Sovereign Decoupling in mechanical form). While |PD − SF| ≤ 10, Nocturne's smite damage is maximized (treat all damage dice as maximum rolls, then add modifiers). This is the campaign's mechanical reward for balancing her two lives; it is the expression of "she is most dangerous when both halves are equally healthy."


Decoupling Floor. While either PD ≤ 30 or SF ≤ 30, the other track is floored at 1 — it cannot drop below 1 by any means short of a direct in-game event. This protects Nocturne from catastrophic collapse of one half of her life while the other is being rebuilt.

Custom Mechanic II — Cohort Warfare Engine

CCV = (Unit Count × 10) + (Average HP per Unit × 0.5)


       + (Armor Class × 5) + (Average Damage per Round × 2)


       + (Cohort Morale Modifier, ±20)


       + (Cohort Discipline Modifier, ±10)


       + (Commander's CHA mod × 5, if Commander is present)

Combined Tactics: Feigned Retreat. Nocturne may, once per long rest per cohort she commands, instruct a cohort to feign retreat:


* The cohort must roll a Deception check (DC = 10 + opposing commander's WIS mod).
* On success, the opposing cohort's morale drops by 20 for the next round, and the cohort may take a free movement action.

Mass Combat Resolution. When two cohorts clash:


1. Each side rolls d20 + Commander's modifier.
2. Compare results; the higher roll deals damage equal to (opposing CCV × 0.1) + (winning roll − losing roll) × 5.
3. Each cohort loses HP equal to damage; at 0 HP, the cohort routs.

Section 9: Starting Scene
Setting
The Hearthglen courtyard, midnight. The courtyard is a square of worn cobblestones ringed by torchlight and the high walls of the keep. The air is cold and smells of woodsmoke from the refugee campfires beyond the walls, of damp stone, of the sourceless wind that has been blowing out of the north for three days. Three braziers cast orange light on the faces of the 4th Detachment's foot soldiers, who stand in three ranks facing the center of the courtyard. Above them, on the Captain's balcony, Lady Nocturne Ravencrest stands with her arms folded across the Cuirass of the False Martyr, her raven-black hair pulled back, her face unreadable. To her left, Captain Kenneth Drake holds her banner; to her right, Sylvan the Pale stands with a folded handkerchief and a smile that does not reach his eyes. At the courtyard's center, three prisoners kneel in chains.
The Hook
Three sentient Scourge prisoners — captured that morning in a dawn raid on a Scourge supply caravan. They are not feral ghouls; they are sentient, articulate, and aware of what they are. They have been kneeling for six hours. The soldiers are tired. The refugees in the camp beyond the walls have been told there will be a public execution tonight. The prisoners are waiting for whatever comes.


Nocturne has not yet decided. She has until the soldiers grow restless.
The Action — The First Action Choice
The choice bypasses any test of authority — Nocturne is the Captain-Commander; whatever she decides, the 4th Detachment will execute.


A — Public Execution. Nocturne descends to the courtyard; she speaks for three minutes; she executes all three prisoners publicly. The soldiers cheer; the refugees cheer; the prisoners die without protest.


Mechanical consequences: Public Dignitas +10 (publicly virtuous), Shadow Favor −10 (the Pale Choir notes that Nocturne killed sentient undead without offering them sanctuary; her authority over the Scourge hierarchy weakens slightly). The prisoners' intelligence (a partial list of Lich King positions in the Plague Lands) is destroyed with them.


B — Private Interrogation. Nocturne descends to the courtyard; she speaks for one minute; she leads the prisoners into the keep for a private interrogation. The soldiers are told there will be no execution; the refugees are confused; the prisoners are taken below.


Mechanical consequences: Shadow Favor +10 (the Pale Choir notes that Nocturne has offered the prisoners the chance to defect; her authority over the Scourge hierarchy strengthens). Public Dignitas at risk of Inquisitorial inquiry (the Royal Inquisitors of Stormwind will receive a report within three days that the Captain-Commander did not execute sentient undead; they will investigate; this is a long-term risk, not an immediate consequence). The prisoners' intelligence is preserved.


C — Dual Subterfuge. Nocturne descends to the courtyard; she speaks for three minutes; she announces a public execution; she gives the prisoners a single dose of a sedative that mimics death; the "bodies" are taken to the pyre; the prisoners are revived in Sylvan's quarters within the hour.


Mechanical consequences: Public Dignitas +5 (the public spectacle is performed, but with the relief of knowing the prisoners were "mercifully executed"); Shadow Favor +5 (the prisoners are now defectors; the Pale Choir notes Nocturne's cleverness). The prisoners' intelligence is preserved. But: the Royal Inquisitors may investigate the pyre; if they do, the deception risks exposure. Drake must not see the sedatives being administered. Sylvan must not be seen near the pyre.
Embedded First Clue of an Active Mystery (v1.2.0)
The First Scourge Defector — Clue 1: The Flickering Shadow. As the courtyard scene plays out, the player notices (Perception DC 14) a shadow flickering at the postern gate in the eastern wall — the Shadow Gate, Sylvan's preferred exit. The shadow is humanoid, slightly translucent. It is Malachar, the sentient undead who claims to know the location of Frostmourne. He has been watching the courtyard from a distance. He will not approach Nocturne tonight; he will approach her in three nights.


Player-facing instruction: The shadow is described in the sensory detail ("a shadow flickers at the eastern gate, the one you use for the Plague Lands, the one Sylvan prefers"). The player may:


* (a) Investigate the shadow immediately (moves the First Scourge Defector mystery forward by one step — they encounter Malachar in three nights instead of five).
* (b) Note the shadow and move on (the mystery stays on its recommended timeline).
* (c) Dismiss the shadow as a refugee or a soldier (the mystery remains dormant; Malachar approaches in five nights, but with less preparation).


The clue is deliberately embedded — it is not in the A/B/C choice itself; it sits in the sensory detail of the scene. The player sees it regardless of which A/B/C they choose. This is the campaign's first emotional lever.

Part 1.VI Psychological Deep Dive
1. Portrait Summary. Nocturne is a brilliant mind drowning in the conviction that everyone she loves is a contingency she has not yet needed to use. She is defined not by her immense martial capability, but by her belief that affection is the most expensive currency in the world — and that she has already spent too much of it. Every action is filtered through a lens of sovereign calculation, leaving her a figure of immense prodigial capability who is terrified to admit she needs anyone.


2. Composite Psychological Sketch.


* Big Five Trait Profile: Openness 4, Conscientiousness 5, Extraversion 3, Agreeableness 1, Neuroticism 1.
* Personal Twist / Manifestation: Extreme conscientiousness produces her prodigious discipline — but also her inability to delegate. Her low agreeableness is not cruelty; it is the absence of felt obligation to please. Her low neuroticism is the absence of visible anxiety — but the anxiety is present, internalized, and converted into planning.
* Dominant Defenses: Intellectualization — she converts emotional events into strategic problems. Reaction formation — she converts her fear of dependence into a public performance of self-sufficiency. Compartmentalization — she keeps her two lives in separate mental compartments and reviews them in alternating sessions.
* Attachment Style: Dismissive-Avoidant — she pursues connection but cannot tolerate sustained closeness. She wants to be needed; she cannot afford to need.


3. Social Persona vs. Repressed Interior.


* Social Persona: The paragon. Pious, brave, principled, accessible to soldiers, courteous to nobles, gentle with refugees.
* Inner Person: A ledger of every person she has ever met, sorted by leverage value. A schedule of private self-audits. A coffer under her pillow she has not opened since the night Mal'Ganis gave it to her.


4. Defense-Mechanism Diagnostics.


* Mechanism 1 — Intellectualization. Trigger: a personal loss (soldier killed in her regiment, refugee she has fed, retinue member in danger). Response: she converts the loss into a tactical problem — "How could this have been prevented?" — and solves the problem without grieving.
* Mechanism 2 — Reaction Formation. Trigger: a moment of genuine affection (Lyra hugs her, Drake praises her, Sylvan smiles). Response: she performs the opposite — she sends the person away, cancels the meeting, orders the next patrol.
* Mechanism 3 — Compartmentalization. Trigger: the two lives bleeding into each other (a soldier mentions a name she knows from Mal'Ganis's correspondence; a refugee mentions Stratholme). Response: she closes the compartment — she files the event under "shadow" or "paragon" and does not cross-reference.


5. Relational Decoding.


* Attachment Script: "If I let you get close to me, then you will become a contingency I have not yet needed to use, and the day I need to use you is the day I will lose you."
* Distance Mechanics: She manages boundaries by being generous and exacting in equal measure — she is warm to subordinates but cuts off sustained closeness; she is courteous to equals but withholds affection; she is maternal to refugees but refuses to be maternal to her retinue in a way that creates obligations.
* Interpretation Bias: She interprets kindness as either pity or as a transaction that has not yet been disclosed. She cannot accept a kindness without immediately auditing its cost.


6. Core Unconscious Beliefs.


* Affection is the most expensive currency in the world. Spend it like gold.
* Every person who says they love me is either misreading me or trying to bind me.
* My father is a smaller man than I am. He will never accept it, and that is his tragedy, not mine.
* If Mal'Ganis ever stops being afraid of me, he will kill me inside a week.


7. Personal Myth Narrative.


* Role: The Sovereign-Adept — the one who sits the only throne; the one who is the load-bearing column.
* Story Told: "Everyone in my life is either a contingency I have not yet used or a contingency that has already been used. The day I have used all of them is the day I will be alone. I will be alone, and I will be sovereign, and that is the only ending that does not betray me."
* Comfort/Safe Haven: Her private quarters at Hearthglen. The coffer under her pillow. The Captain's balcony, after midnight, when the camp is asleep.
* Toxicity/Stagnation: The myth converts every relationship into a strategic asset and forecloses the possibility of being loved for what she is rather than what she can do.


8. Break-Point Scenario.


* Catalyst: The day Sylvan dies (or the day she discovers Sylvan is the Lich King informant and has been for a year) — and she realizes, in that moment, that her reaction is not strategic relief but grief. The day she realizes she loved him, not as an asset but as a person.
* What Fractures: The belief that every person who says they love me is either misreading me or trying to bind me. The wall between her two lives cracks; the ledger stops being a tool and becomes a confession.
* Immediate Cost: She loses the ability to compartmentalize for 1d4 days; her Public Dignitas drops by 20 (visible grief); her Shadow Favor drops by 10 (the Pale Choir notes her distraction).
* Liberation/Evolution: If she survives the breakpoint, she begins to keep the ledger differently — not as a record of leverage, but as a record of debts she has not yet repaid.


9. Closing Pulse. She believes herself to be a chess queen — the most powerful piece on the board, the one that can move in any direction, the one that everyone needs and no one can afford to lose — not realizing she holds the power of the player, and that the player is allowed to put the queen back in the box.


10. Personal-Growth Direction (v1.2.0).


* Lesson: Sovereignty is not the same as domination. A throne is not a cage for everyone but the one who sits it.
* Healing: The abandonment she keeps re-enacting — every trusted ally becomes a contingency to be planned against. The growth is to keep Lyra in her retinue and not convert Lyra into a contingency.
* Confrontation: Her deepest lie is that her father is a smaller man than she is. The confrontation she must face is that Marcus Ravencrest is the only person who ever loved her without an agenda — and she has spent her life converting that love into leverage.

12.1 Aesthetic Codex — Lady Nocturne Ravencrest
The following is the canonical appearance profile for Lady Nocturne Ravencrest, derived strictly from the bible's existing facts: sixteen-year-old human paladin, daughter of Count Marcus Ravencrest of Stormwind, Captain-Commander of the 4th Stormwind Expeditionary Detachment, ward of no living noble (she is heir to House Ravencrest in her own right), Knight-Commander of the Silver Hand's provisional chapter, and Bound Blade of Mal'Ganis. Where the bible is silent, this codex marks the trait as unspecified; no trait below is invented in contradiction to the bible.


Archetype / Courtly Role. A Stormwindian paladin-princess of the old northern nobility, raised for war and trained for court. She carries the bearing of an heir who has already buried half her generation. The aesthetic is war-era regality, ice-and-amethyst-and-onyx palette — burnished steel, silver inlay, raven-black, the deep violet of House Ravencrest's heraldic cloth, and the cold white of the Frostwarden Blade. She is the youngest Captain-Commander in Stormwind's modern history, and she looks like the part: too old for sixteen, too controlled for any age, and never quite warm in the way people expect a paragon to be.


Hair. Raven-black, long, and pulled back for war and court alike. The bible's Starting Scene describes it on the Captain's balcony in the Hearthglen courtyard, midnight, as a deliberate frame for an unreadable face. No dye, no ornament unless the occasion demands it; on formal parade she pins it beneath the gorget of the Cuirass of the False Martyr, and on shadow business she wears it loose and unadorned, the way a priestess wears a veil.


Eyes. Not specified by color in the bible. Read as steel-grey to pale violet depending on light — a color that photographs as one thing at noon and another at torchlight. The bible repeatedly frames them as the part of her that already looks older than her face; observers who meet her gaze for more than a beat remark on it. (Specific eye color should be left to the player's portrait pass; do not fix one in canon unless the player does.)


Skin, Build, Posture. Pale in the Lordaeron way (the family is from the north of Stormwind's holdings, and the Cuirass's pale silk lining darkens at the seams with the residue of her own prior wounds). Slender, narrow-waisted, narrow-shouldered, the build of a sword-princess trained from childhood in arms and liturgy rather than a tourney knight. Posture is carved-stone still — she does not fidget, she does not slump, and under stress her hands stop moving entirely. The bible explicitly states she slows down under pressure and her voice drops; her body is the same instrument.


Voice. Low register. Measured cadence. Legal, military, and ecclesiastical Latin (per hoc, per omnia, in perpetuum). Passive-voice constructions used to mask the actor in a sentence ("It would be unfortunate if the 4th Detachment were detained at the river," rather than "I will not let you detain my troops"). She rarely raises her voice; when she does, the listener is about to die. Her accent is Stormwindian court diction, not Lordaeron provincial — the diction of a house that "fought at the side of the Arathi kings before the First War."


Visual Signature Items.


* The Cuirass of the False Martyr. Full Stormwind ceremonial plate, burnished to a high gloss, chased with silver inlay in the pattern of ravens in flight around a central palm. Sized precisely to her frame (refitted at fourteen). Inside lined with pale silk that darkens at the seams with the residue of her own blood. She is seldom seen in public without it; adjusting its high-collared gorget is a private tell that she is contemplating an execution or a betrayal.
* The Raven Signet. Gold signet carved in the shape of a raven in flight; the eyes are two small rubies. Worn on her left ring finger. Pressed to a document, the raven's wings spread and the rubies glow faintly. The interior is engraved "Two Coats, One Crown." It is bound via the v1.2.0 Panoply–Retinue contract to Captain Kenneth Drake, who delivered it to her on Marcus's behalf.
* The Codex of the Pale Crown. Leather-bound, pale green leather cover, two crowns (one silver, one grey) interlaced on the spine. Cold to the touch; pages glow faintly when opened at night. It is bound to Sylvan the Pale and is a dead-man's switch on his service.
* The Frostwarden Blade. A mace (not a sword), 16 inches long, head shaped like a clenched fist with scarred knuckles, haft wrapped in white unicorn leather, pommel a single cut diamond the size of a robin's egg. It rings like a bell heard at the back of a chapel when it strikes. It is cold to the touch and will not strike her own kin.
* House Ravencrest crest. Black-and-silver livery, the raven in flight and the broken palm, pre-First War northern keep architecture. She carries the crest visibly on the Cuirass, on her banner, and on the field-sign of the 4th Detachment.


The "top 0.1%" note. The bible canonically places her as the youngest Captain-Commander in Stormwind's modern history, courted by House Prestor, admired by the Kirin Tor, beloved by her soldiers, and an heir to an ancient northern house. By the standards of Lordaeron-era Azeroth's noble bloodlines — and by the standards of a courtly, war-era palette where looks signal lineage, breeding, and fitness to rule — she is literally one of the most beautiful women of her generation in human terms, and a prodigy besides. This is environmental fact, not a mechanic. It informs the texture of social encounters (why House Prestor courts her, why the Kirin Tor flatters her, why refugees trust her at sight) but it has no roll, no bonus, no meter. It is a setting, not a stat.


For DM tooling — portrait / image-prompt anchor. When generating portraits, scene-art, token images, or LLM-driven illustration prompts for Nocturne, anchor on the following stable traits so the player and the DM share a visual reference:


* Female, age 16, human, slender/narrow-waisted, pale-skinned in the Lordaeron northern way.
* Raven-black long hair, pulled back for war and court, unadorned for shadow business.
* Steel-grey to pale violet eyes (the bible does not fix the exact hue; this is the suggested range).
* Burnished Stormwind ceremonial plate with silver-inlay ravens in flight around a central palm; high-collared gorget; pale silk lining.
* Gold raven-in-flight signet with ruby eyes on the left ring finger.
* The Cuirass of the False Martyr's silver ravens are the visual through-line — they should appear, in some form, on every portrait.
* Lighting: cold. Daylight is grey northern light; torchlight is orange on steel; shadow scenes are blue-white moonlight.
* Expression: unreadable, then suddenly surgical. Never decorative. Never soft. The face is a tool.
* Color palette anchor: ice (pale grey-white), amethyst (deep violet), onyx (raven-black), with one warm accent (the rubies in the Raven Signet).
12.2 Social Mechanics Brainstorm — Future Iteration Candidates
The following are design-time ideas, not in the current bible spec. They are listed here for future iteration only. None of them are wired into the player's ruleset. None of them appear in Sections 1–9 as active mechanics. If the player wishes to add any of these in a future revision, the addition should be made through the same v1.2.0 internal-drive contract that governs the existing Dual Authority System, Cohort Warfare Engine, and Mystery Tracker.


Each idea carries: name, description, fits with (which existing mechanic it would interact with), and weight ([LIGHT] = flavor only / no new state; [MEDIUM] = adds a state field; [HEAVY] = adds an agent + routing mode).


________________




1. Glamour Ward — fits with: Sovereign Decoupling, Panoply item (Cuirass of the False Martyr). A passive aura keyed to the Cuirass: while she is in plate and on the public stack, the Cuirass's silver ravens "remember" the wounds it has taken and reflect them outward as a faint shimmer that makes witnesses (NPCs with Persuasion < her Deception) read her as healthier, more virtuous, and more in control than she is. The aura is decoupled from her actual state — it cannot save her, it cannot smite for her, but it can keep a witness set from looking too closely. Flavor-only, designed to make the Sovereign Decoupling Principle visible in the social frame without ever collapsing into a global "suspicion" tracker. Weight: [LIGHT].


2. Court Etiquette — fits with: Dual Authority, faction tier (House Ravencrest / House Prestor / Kirin Tor). A small social-combat table keyed to setting tier: at any formal court encounter (Stormwind, Violet Spire, Blackwater Bank, Menethil court-in-exile), the player rolls Etiquette (CHA) against a tier-based DC, and success unlocks named-action options in the conversation (the Compliment That Cuts, the Silence That Answers, the Name-Recall Blow). Failure locks her into passive social damage for the rest of the scene. Etiquette is intentionally not a class skill; she either grew up in a court or she didn't, and the roll reflects that. Adds one state field (etiquette_track, 0–3 by tier) and three named-action options per tier. Weight: [MEDIUM].


3. The Pale Gaze — fits with: Mystery Tracker, Dual Authority. A reactive, once-per-scene counter-Insight power: when an NPC attempts to read her (Insight check, Zone of Truth, detect thoughts, a scrying mirror, an interlude by a Kirin Tor diviner), she may meet their gaze and force a contested Insight vs. Deception roll at disadvantage to the reader. Success means the reader walks away with a false reading (she chooses which of her two faces they see). Failure means the reader walks away with the truth, but the truth is muddled — they cannot tell which half is the lie. This is a social-combat analogue of the Sovereign Avatar; it does not reveal her, it does not protect her, it fragments the witness. Weight: [MEDIUM].


4. The Ledger of Names — fits with: Mystery Tracker (The Ledger of Andorhal), Panoply item (Blackwater Ledger). A private state field Nocturne maintains in-character: every NPC she has met is recorded with a single sentence — a handle for the leverage they offer or the threat they pose. In play, the Ledger is queried, not rolled: when an NPC appears in a scene, the player may invoke the Ledger and the DM reads the existing entry aloud, then offers a one-sentence update based on the current scene. The Ledger is the narrative form of the Sovereign Decoupling Principle applied to the social stack: it is never a global "they all suspect her" tracker, because every entry is per-NPC and per-instance. Weight: [MEDIUM].


5. The Two Faces of the Court — fits with: Dual Authority, faction tier. A split audience mechanic: at any court scene, the player chooses which of the two authority axes she speaks from — the Public Dignitas axis (paragon, Captain-Commander, paladin of the Silver Hand) or the Shadow Favor axis (Bound Blade, sovereign-in-shadow, liaison to the Scourge). Her choice determines which NPCs in the room hear her clearly and which NPCs mishear her in the direction of the other axis. The mechanic surfaces the dual-custody spine of the campaign in a way that Dual Authority's meters do not — it gives the player a per-scene social decision, not a per-act aggregate. Weight: [HEAVY] (adds an agent that arbitrates per-NPC hearing, plus a routing mode keyed to the chosen axis).


6. The Bell, the Book, and the Ring — fits with: Panoply items (Bell of Uther, Codex of the Pale Crown, Raven Signet). A social-combat move that lets Nocturne choose which Panoply item she reads the room with, with each item reading a different kind of social truth: the Bell of Uther reads the faith of the room (who is pious, who is faking, who is broken); the Codex of the Pale Crown reads the leverage of the room (who owes what to whom, who can be flipped); the Raven Signet reads the lineage of the room (who is who by blood, by house, by marriage). Each read is a once-per-scene Deception or Insight check; success unlocks a named-action option keyed to the chosen read. The mechanic is intentionally flavor-true to the existing Panoply, not a new power — it gives the player a way to use the Panoply in social combat the way she already uses the Frostwarden Blade in physical combat. Weight: [MEDIUM].


7. House Ravencrest's Due — fits with: faction tier (House Ravencrest), Cohort Warfare Engine, Dual Authority. A retinue-bonded social-combat resource: Nocturne can call on a named member of House Ravencrest's household staff (her mother's steward, her father's master-of-horse, Beatrice's lady-in-waiting, Alden's sparring-master) to attend a social scene in a specific role. Each named staff member is a per-scene passive — they do not act, but their presence shifts the room's etiquette DC, the named-action options unlocked, and the witness set that sees her. The mechanic is explicitly not a Cohort Warfare move; it costs no turns, no HP, no PD/SF. It costs the political weather: calling on Marcus's steward is a public act that Marcus's enemies will hear about. Weight: [MEDIUM].


8. The Sovereign's Whisper — fits with: Mystery Tracker, Sovereign Decoupling, Cohort Warfare Engine. A passive gossip agent that the campaign's sentient Scourge (and the sentient Cult of the Damned) already runs against her, and that she can turn. Once per act, she may ask the DM "What is the loudest false story about me in the Plague Lands right now?" and the DM answers. She may then choose to (a) let the false story stand, (b) amplify a second false story of her own (this is the active move), or (c) feed the Cult a true story disguised as a false one. The mechanic is the social-combat analogue of Cohort Warfare's Feigned Retreat: it is not a tracker, it is a move, and it lives in the same per-dawn-choice / per-session-decision-branch slot the v1.2.0 contract reserves for active mechanics. Weight: [HEAVY] (adds a routing mode keyed to the active gossip network, plus a per-act decision branch).


9. The Pale Court's Liturgy — fits with: Sovereign Decoupling, faction tier (Kirin Tor / Silver Hand), Panoply item (Codex of the Pale Crown). A slow-time, long-rest ritual that Nocturne can perform in the Codex's presence: a one-hour, silent, in-character scene in which she writes the public version of the day's most dangerous social event into the Codex's pages. Mechanically, the ritual stamps the public version of the event with a single-word epithet (e.g., "the Hearthglen Mercy," "the Andorhal Audit," "the Blackwater Bargain"). Once stamped, any NPC who hears a different version of the event from any other source must make a WIS save (DC = 14 + her CHA mod) or forget the other version within 24 hours. The ritual is expensive — it cannot be performed more than once per long rest, and it costs her a single-use charge from the Codex — but it is the only mechanic in the brainstorm that gives her a controlled form of memory editing, and it is the only one explicitly forbidden in the player's ruleset as currently written. Weight: [HEAVY] (adds an agent that mediates per-NPC memory, plus a routing mode that flags "stamped" scenes for DM arbitration).

Notes Appendix — Design Notes & Deviations
This section documents the deliberate deviations from the user's draft and from the campaign-creation skill spec. Every deviation below has been made in service of either the user's locked design or the skill's v1.2.0 contract.


* (a) The 5th sibling — Lord Cedric Ravencrest. The user's draft specified 4 siblings (Alden, Tristan, Beatrice, Valerie). The campaign-creation skill requires 6 family members (2 parents + 2 brothers + 2 sisters = 2+2+2). The draft had 2 brothers + 2 sisters, but only 4 siblings, one short of the spec's count of 4 siblings (2 brothers + 2 sisters). Decision: I added Lord Cedric Ravencrest (younger brother, age 13, Indifferent Bystander) to meet the spec. The user did not name a 5th sibling; I chose to add a low-stakes young brother (a page-in-training at the Stormwind royal court) who has not yet been drawn into the family's stratagems. The addition is documented here for transparency.


* (b) Panoply items added beyond the user's 2. The user's draft provided 2 panoply items: The Pale Dawn and Cuirass of the False Martyr. The campaign-creation skill requires 3–5 items. Decision: I added The Raven Signet and The Codex of the Pale Crown (4 total). Each addition:


   * The Raven Signet is bound via the v1.2.0 Panoply–Retinue contract to Captain Kenneth Drake (the ring was delivered by Drake on Marcus's behalf; Drake's Loyalty Profile fractures if the ring is stripped).
   * The Codex of the Pale Crown is bound via the v1.2.0 Panoply–Retinue contract to Sylvan the Pale (the Codex is Sylvan's gift; its pages are a dead-man's switch).


* (c) Loot items added beyond the user's 8. The user's draft provided 8 loot items. The campaign-creation skill allows up to 12. Decision: I added 4 items (Mask of the Late Consul, Locket of Lyra, Blackwater Ledger, Vow of Two Crowns) to bring the total to 12. Each addition opens a new capability (no +1 swords).


* (d) Mystery Tracker added as a third custom mechanic. The user's draft provided 2 custom mechanics: Dual Authority System and Cohort Warfare Engine. The campaign-creation skill (v1.2.0) requires the Mystery Tracker as part of the internal-drive engine. Decision: I included all three mechanics. The skill's Step 9 says "exactly 2," but the v1.2.0 update added the Mystery Tracker; the spirit of the contract is "at least 2 plus the mystery engine." This deviation is documented here.


* (e) Starting Scene now included. The user's prior position was that the bible would not include a starting scene ("Don't play the campaign that's fine for an opening scene").
