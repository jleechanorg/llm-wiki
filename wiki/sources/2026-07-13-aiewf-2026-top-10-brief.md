## Top 10 — most interesting to you, in priority order

### 1. The "Verification Gap" is now a measured, named problem — 27.6% of merged PRs are AI-generated, only ~48% get explicit review (Greptile + Sonar data, Day 3 keynote)

Greptile analyzed 1M+ AI-reviewed PRs: AI-written code is **27.6% of merged PRs as of April 2026**, up from <1% in Feb 2025 — a **~28× increase in 14 months**. Sonar's 2026 State of Code Developer Survey (n=1,100): **96% of devs don't fully trust AI-generated code, only 48% verify it before commit, 42% of all committed code is AI-generated**, projected 65% by 2027. The arithmetic is the whole story: generation compounding, verification flat. Sonar's answer is the "Agent Centric Development Cycle" — **Guide → Verify → Solve** — treating verification as a first-class step, not a `git push` afterthought. Sonar acquired Gitar earlier this year specifically to extend review to AI-generated code.

**Why this hits you:** Your evidence gate / skeptic-cron / 7-green verification loop on worldarchitect.ai is ahead of the industry curve but the same discipline. The new product wedge isn't "more agents" — it's "verifier infrastructure." This is exactly the world Databricks' and Faros.ai's internal benchmarks just validated for coding-eval.

Source: [ChatForest Day 3–4 recap](https://chatforest.com/builders-log/aiewf-2026-days-3-4-verification-autoresearch-mike-krieger-anthropic-builder-guide/)

### 2. Mike Krieger's (Anthropic Labs) "Build frontier-far" — model scaffolding outlasts the model itself, every 40–90 days

Mike Krieger closed out Day 4 with what was effectively Anthropic's internal-build playbook leaked to a practitioner audience. Three lessons:

- **Be ambitious & build frontier-far.** Models become obsolete every 40–90 days. The scaffolding — workflows, verification loops, harness — outlasts any specific model. Build for where the models are going, not where they are today.
- **Intelligent autonomy.** Anthropic Labs' operating pattern: agents proceed autonomously on well-scoped subtasks and *pause* at decision points that are material, irreversible, or high-stakes. The failure mode is at both extremes — agents that ask permission constantly (defeat autonomy) vs. agents that never ask (accumulate silent errors). Calibrated middle.
- **AI as superhuman collaborator, not tool to call.** Labs has more real production failure-mode data than anyone outside Anthropic. Their assumption is treating Claude like an intuition-bearing collaborator, not a CLI invocation.

**Why this hits you:** "Frontier-far" is exactly the framing your Dark Factory spec needs. Your design says "intelligence is the moat" — Krieger says "build one layer below the model, since the model swaps under you on an 8–12 week cadence." Map this onto your 7/8-green gate: the gate *is* the scaffolding, and it's durable across Opus 4.7 → 4.8 → 5.x.

Source: [ChatForest Day 4 recap](https://chatforest.com/builders-log/aiewf-2026-day-4-recap-krieger-browne-prompt-debt-harness-engineering-builder-guide/), [Krieger bio on conference page](https://www.ai.engineer/worldsfair/2026)

### 3. Matt Pocock's "Skill Checklist" — Trigger / Structure / Steering / Pruning — formalizes skill design as an engineering discipline

Matt Pocock (AI Hero) opened Day 4 main stage with "Building Great Agent Skills: The Missing Manual." His diagnosis: most builders hit "Skill Hell" — they discover Claude Code skills, build a few, ship a tutorial framework, accumulate an unmaintained `.claude/` directory of overlapping instructions that don't compose. His four-component checklist for any well-crafted skill:

- **Trigger** — how & when the skill fires. *Without an unambiguous trigger, a skill is ignored or activated at the wrong moment. Active misdirection is worse than no skill.*
- **Structure** — internal organization (linear / loop / branching).
- **Steering** — behavioral constraints, "don't do X, always check Y" rules.
- **Pruning** — discipline of removing instructions the model no longer needs.

Reference impl: [github.com/mattpocock/skills](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills) — 17 example skills (TDD, code review, refactoring, release mgmt).

**Why this hits you:** Your `~/.hermes/skills/` directory is large and accruing. Pocock's framework basically IS your SOUL.md `## COMMIT:` blocks turned into a four-quadrant authoring rubric. Worth doing a one-pass audit: which skills have a clear **trigger**, which have grown stale and need **pruning**, which lack **steering**? Especially relevant since you keep shipping new Hermes skills + the harness-postmortem skill meta-pattern.

Source: [ChatForest Day 4 recap](https://chatforest.com/builders-log/aiewf-2026-day-4-recap-krieger-browne-prompt-debt-harness-engineering-builder-guide/)

### 4. Theo Browne: "Your prompts are technical debt" — system prompts decay silently on every model update

Theo Browne (CEO, T3 Tools) closed Day 4 with a sharp argument: AI prompts are technical debt that produces no errors and no CI signal. When a foundation model ships an update, custom `AGENTS.md` / `CLAUDE.md` / markdown directive files become obsolete unpredictably. The degradation shows up weeks later as subtly worse outputs, ghost rewrites, or behavioral regressions with no obvious cause. T3 Code's own `AGENTS.md` had the wrong "Codex-first" label for months. There's no stack trace.

**Why this hits you:** You literally maintain one of the densest `SOUL.md` / `AGENTS.md` corpus in the wild. This validates (1) why you've been hitting intermittent Hermes behavioral regressions after model updates, (2) why you keep building the new "skillify" + "session-learn-distill" + "agent-agent-mistakes" layers — they're counter-measures for prompt decay. The new best practice: version your prompts against the model version, not just against the code. Consider a `prompts_CHANGELOG.md` per skill.

Source: [ChatForest Day 4 recap](https://chatforest.com/builders-log/aiewf-2026-day-4-recap-krieger-browne-prompt-debt-harness-engineering-builder-guide/)

### 5. swyx's "Loopcraft" — the field went from chat → tools → goals → automations → loops, with the harness as the unit of composition

swyx (AIEWF co-founder) opened Day 2 with "Loopcraft: The Art of Stacking Loops," framing three years of AI engineering as a sequence where the unit shifted from chat completions → tool calls → goals → automations → now **stacked loops**. Geoffrey Huntley's "ralph loop" (an agent loop that runs on a spec until done-stated) was elevated to canonical status on Day 1 and dominated the conference's vocabulary. Latent.Space's daily dispatch led with "Loops, loops and more loops." Cron jobs, schedule-based automations, ralph-loop workers, persisted agent loops against specs were the demo language of the entire conference.

**Why this hits you:** This is your /goal, /loop, /schedule, /cron stack from the WorkOS workshop demonstrated on a 6,000-person stage. You're ahead. The interesting delta: everyone else is reinventing ralph loops independently. The next layer to look at is **stack composition** — loops of loops, where verification and generation are themselves loop-stacked (a generation loop nested inside a verification loop nested inside a budget loop nested inside a harness loop). That's the swoop Anthropic's Daniela Amodei referenced re Krieger's "intelligent autonomy."

Source: [TrueFoundry recap](https://www.truefoundry.com/blog/aiewf-2026-loops-harness-engineering), [Latent Space](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-ai-engineer-worlds-fair-autoresearch)

### 6. WorkOS / Garrett Galow / Ryan Cooke: a "software factory" is the harness + the org's process — not the sandbox

Three orthogonal talks converged on the same thing from different angles:

- **WorkOS's workshop** (Nick Nisi + Zack Proser, "Lifestyles of the AI-Native") taught /goal /loop /verification-gates /schedule as the four primitives — and *showed the room its own reclaim-hours metric* on a live board fed by an in-repo local MCP coach. Q: what shipped was reproducible, runnable, kept going after the workshop ended.
- **Garrett Galow (WorkOS)** — "Building an Agent Harness for the Business, Not the Builder." Harness built for non-engineers has to absorb all the engineering it removes: data-source connections + permissions, model output → real software (not chat box), deployment & sharing that doesn't open a security hole per ship.
- **Ryan Cooke (WorkOS)** — "No, that's not a software factory." Drawing on Project Horizon: a sandbox isn't a software factory. What matters is encoding **how your org builds software** — the way work gets planned, scoped, verified, the conventions and judgment calls that define your engineering culture.

**Why this hits you:** This validates and sharpens your Dark Factory spec: the harness is not the agent loop, the harness is the **process boundary** that wraps the loop. The "org-specific encoding of how work gets done" piece is exactly what your spec-mutation grammar and Constraint Extraction layer are trying to do. Ryan Cooke literally named your spec's failure mode ("what doesn't generalize from the sandbox to the org"), then offered what the working version looks like (project Horizon).

Source: [WorkOS recap](https://workos.com/blog/workos-aie-worlds-fair-2026-recap)

### 7. Authors of OAuth (Dick Hardt) launched AAuth — agent-specific auth at AAuth Night

"AAuth is a new auth protocol designed for agents, built by the author of OAuth, Dick Hardt." Lightning talks + live demos on agents calling resources without API keys, mission-bounded authority, multi-agent delegation chains. Panel moderated by Allie Howe (Insecure Agents Podcast). Cloudflare, Firecrawl, and Monday.com are adopting the open protocol.

**Why this hits you:** Your MCP-Mail / GOG / agent-routing concerns have hit the same bottleneck everyone else has: agents don't have OAuth flow-safe auth boundaries. AAuth is the first credible open primitive for it, and the same labs shipping OpenAI's Codex stack + Anthropic's SDK + Cloudflare's agent platform are early adopters. Worth tracking. Also, Michael Grinich (WorkOS) gave a mainstage talk titled **"Auth for Agents: Unblock Autonomous AI with auth.md"** — WorkOS is pushing the same primitive from a different angle.

Source: [Conference schedule, AAuth Night section](https://www.ai.engineer/worldsfair/2026), [WorkOS recap](https://workos.com/blog/workos-aie-worlds-fair-2026-recap)

### 8. "Tasteful Tokenmaxxing" + "Tailscale's Remy Guercio: next 12 months are about ROI maximization" — the cost discipline everyone is about to hit

A whole new track this year: AI Architects: Tokenmaxxing — "as a company leader, how do you make your AI Eng teams 10x more AI-Native / scale AI adoption without Goodharting waste." WorkOS's post-event framing: "The last 12 months of AI spend were about token maximization. The next 12 months are about ROI maximization." Remy Guercio (Tailscale): when AI bills spike across every provider, the natural instinct is to consolidate onto one model and simplify the bill — that's a trap. Tokenmaxxing done well = routing per-task, not per-bill.

**Why this hits you:** Your `Dynamic Model Selection` agents (Databricks + Faros.ai translation), your $264+ Actions billing spike (2026-07-13 jleechanorg MTD), and your "smart model routing" / Pragmatic Engineer article reference are all part of the same shift. The playbook that's crystallizing is: pick the model per task class, not per provider. Confirm /green cost spike gut-check holds.

Source: [Latent Space CFP](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-ai-engineer-worlds-fair-autoresearch), [WorkOS LinkedIn post](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/workos-inc_the-last-12-months-of-ai-spend-were-about-activity-7480338445329121280-Mkfe)

### 9. "Autoresearch" is the new research methodology — recursive self-improvement loops in harness + training

Day 3 keynote track. Autoresearch = recursive self-improvement loops in harnesses and model training (Benoit Schillings, VP Tech, Google DeepMind, headlined). Wei-Lin Chiang (CTO, Arena.ai / ex-LMSYS) closed Day 3 with "Trends in AI" — Arena.ai hit $100M ARR in 8 months from commercial launch, $1.7B valuation (Series A Jan 2026). LMArena head-to-head data is now the closest the field has to a public-ground-truth eval.

**Why this hits you:** Two things. (a) If you're driving evals across worldarchitect.ai PRs, Arena's data is the most signal-dense public ground truth — and they're shipping a "code mode" in Arena. (b) Autoresearch is the operational cousin of your Dark Factory's "self-improving harness" patterns. The interesting boundary condition: where do autoresearch loops stop being useful and start degrading the harness? The conference offered no clean answer — open research question.

Source: [Latent Space CFP](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-ai-engineer-worlds-fair-autoresearch), [ChatForest Day 3–4 recap](https://chatforest.com/builders-log/aiewf-2026-days-3-4-verification-autoresearch-mike-krieger-anthropic-builder-guide/)

### 10. OpenAI shipped its first hardware at the conference — Codex Micro keypad (with Work Louder), not Jony Ive

During AIEWF, OpenAI unveiled Codex Micro — a macro pad keyboard designed for Codex workflows, built in partnership with Work Louder. Jony Ive's flagship device still isn't ready. Spokesperson Dominik Kundel described it as a keypad "designed to enhance users' experience with Codex."

**Why this hits you:** Two reasons. (a) Hardware-side, your `claudeo`, `codexo`, `claudem` bashrc work just gained a real piece of hardware to test against. (b) The product lesson: OpenAI is willing to ship a niche developer macro pad at a practitioner conference, but their headline AI hardware remains delayed. Read that as: the application-layer agent SWAG is the actual product wedge, hardware is the cherry on top.

Source: [TechStory coverage](https://techstory.in/openai-launches-codex-micro-keypad-as-its-first-hardware-product-jony-ives-device-still-delayed/)

## Bonus: the "harness engineering" theme in one sentence

It's not the model. It's not the prompt. It's the **gates, conventions, definitions of done, and process the org already trusts** wrapping the model loop — durable across model updates, portable across tasks, and the only thing that compounds under the 28× AI-PR generation rate. Anthropic's Mike Krieger, WorkOS's Ryan Cooke, Tailscale's Guercio, Sonar's Shaukat, and TrueFoundry's recap all said it differently but landed on the same word.
