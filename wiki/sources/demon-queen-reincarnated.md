# Demon Queen Reincarnated — *"Ash of Aetheria"*

**Campaign identity**

- **Title (working):** Ash of Aetheria
- **Player character:** Ash (cover name) / Astheria, the Ash-Crowned Queen (true name)
- **Starting identity:** Twelve-year-old fifth daughter of Margrave Aldric Voss, junior student at the Imperial Academy of Aetheria
- **Class:** Gestalt — Wizard (Bladesinger) 12 / Sorcerer (Aberrant Mind) 12, with a sealed *true* baseline of L25 (old magic) gated behind the **Veil** and the **Guilt Lock**
- **Starting level:** 4 (registered Academy level — true gestalt is L12/L12 with L25 ceiling)
- **Power premise:** Far more capable than her public identity suggests, but every reveal carries a body-count flashback and a mana-spike that the three pursuing factions can detect.

## Section 1 — Campaign Intro

### The hook

> *A thousand years ago she burned half a continent. The hero who killed her cut her down while her daughter's scream echoed off the walls of a city she had put to the torch. She woke up twelve years ago in the body of a Margrave's fifth daughter — quiet, polite, with silver hair and a habit of looking at the ground — and she has been counting the bodies ever since.*

Astheria was the **Ash-Crowned Queen**, sovereign of the Abyssal Courts and World-Empress-in-waiting before the title was abolished. She was *not* a sociopath originally — she was a young queen who inherited a war she did not start and was made into a monster by the necessity of winning it. She defeated the Hero-King Aldric's father in single combat, executed three rival demon lords in one night, and held the Abyssal Throne for 144 years.

Then the Hero-King came. He would not stop. He would not negotiate. He cut through her armies, her lieutenants, her consorts, and finally — when she had her daughter Mirael in her arms — he offered her a choice: *the child lives, or you do.* She chose the child. He killed her anyway. The child lived.

That was the thousand-year-old lie she told herself until she was reborn and remembered that **he had kept his word**: Mirael survived. Astheria had simply not believed him.

### Setting the cadence — three beats per Act (Frieren-pattern)

The campaign opens with a *quiet* school day, not a catastrophe. Per the Mushoku Tensei / Frieren synthesis: **the reader has to love the school day before they will feel the loss of it.** Each Act runs on three beats:

1. **A school-day beat where nothing happens except one choice.** Ash chooses silence. Ash chooses to leave the room. Ash chooses to smile at a stranger. The reader watches what she did *not* do.
2. **A witness beat where one named NPC almost sees.** Deliberate, not accidental. The witness may notice the silver burn on her temples, may recognize a spell-form in her spellbook, may ask the wrong question at dinner. Ash **shuts the door** mid-flash and the cost is visible (a memory, a relationship, a friend who pulls back).
3. **A name beat.** Someone says a name — *Astheria, Mirael, Vael'eth, the Last Fire* — and the magic of names binds. Names are contracts in this setting (Slime-pattern): once you say it, the debt is on you.

The catastrophe comes *between* Acts, never *within* one. The school is a place she learns a *rule*, not a curriculum dump.

### One scene Ash almost does what Astheria did (Pattern #12)

By Act III, the bible requires *one* scene where Ash almost *does the thing* — burns a city, executes a child-king, accepts a deal from the wrong demon — and *chooses differently*. The reader will know it is the same choice. The antagonist never has to monologue. The previous-life self *is* the antagonist, and the war is internal before it is external.

### Lore anchor

The campaign opens **at the Imperial Academy of Aetheria, present day**, ten years after Ash's soul finished reforming into the body of the fifth Voss daughter. She has been in this body since age two — long enough that her muscles, her accent, and her reflexive politeness are genuinely hers. The memories of the war are *also* genuinely hers, surfacing in dreams, in moments of stress, in the smell of burning wool.

The setting contains no mindless monsters. Threats come from people: tutors, students, nobles, demon diplomats, Imperial watchers, the Headmistress, and the dead. The Academy is a city-state unto itself — the only place in the modern world where every race and rank is legally required to behave, or be expelled.

### Tone

**Mushoku Tensei cadence first**, **Frieren melancholy second**, **Witcher moral-grey third**. The campaign opens slow — school arc, daily life, quiet grief — and *grows* into the political-thriller cadence as the 3-way cold war escalates around Ash's attempts to live a small, kind life.

## Section 2 — Character Personality

### Sub-Template A: 6-tier personality architecture

**Tier 1 — Surface Mannerisms**
Quiet. Polite. Keeps her eyes down. Speaks softly, never raises her voice, never volunteers information. Eats slowly and finishes everything on her plate. Apologizes reflexively when bumped. Holds her books against her chest like a shield. People who meet her for thirty seconds think: *shy, bookish, slightly ill.* People who have known her for a year think: *something is very wrong with her but I cannot name it.*

**Tier 2 — Coping Behaviors**
She counts. The first thing she does in any new room is count the exits, then count the people, then count the candles. She keeps a private notebook of names — not a spellbook, a *death ledger* — every name from the war she can still remember, alphabetized, with a small cross beside each one. It has 4,873 entries. She does not re-read it often. She cannot bring herself to throw it away.

When overwhelmed she *dissociates* — eyes go slightly unfocused, posture loosens, voice drops to a murmur. This is the INFJ doorslam defense. People interpret it as daydreaming. It is, in fact, her standing at the edge of a cliff in her own mind and choosing not to jump.

**Tier 3 — Private Self (the one Mirael knew)**
Wry. Sharp. Caffeinated. In her first life she was a *formidable* conversationalist — three languages, four musical instruments, a memory for political precedent that terrified her advisors. That person still exists. She catches herself finishing other people's sentences in archaic idioms, then catches *herself* catching herself, and the moment passes. She misses being clever the way a one-armed person misses being ambidextrous.

**Tier 4 — The Wound (what she will not say)**
She let her daughter die. She chose the child's life over her own and then *the hero kept his word and the child lived anyway*, and she has been furious about it for a thousand years. Mirael lived. Mirael had children. Mirael's children had children. The current demon Emperor Vael'eth is her great-grandson. Astheria has not spoken to any of them in a millennium and she cannot forgive herself for the relief she felt when she learned Mirael survived.

**Tier 5 — The Lock (the thing that will not let her heal)**
She *can* be healed. She has been *almost* healed before — three times in the last decade. Each time the Veil thinned and old magic surfaced and someone she loved was in danger and she used it and the body count came back. The lock is not her past. The lock is **her certainty that she will do it again.**

**Tier 6 — The True Self (what she is underneath everything)**
A mother. A teacher. A woman who, before the war, wanted to plant a garden and read books and die in her own bed at a reasonable age. That woman is still in there. She is not the Demon Queen. The Demon Queen was *what the war made her*. The true self is the woman the war *prevented*.

### Core Compulsion

**"Do not become her again."**

This is the only rule she enforces on herself. It overrides self-defense, comfort, ambition, romantic attachment, and her own survival. If using magic at L5 or higher risks triggering a flashback that returns her to *being Astheria* — she does not use it. She will take a beating first.

### Mechanic (The Urge)

When her Veil thins (stress, danger, anger), she hears Mirael's voice in her head — not Mirael's actual voice from 1000 years ago, but a *reconstructed* one built from memory. It says: *"Mother. Don't."* The more her magic rises, the louder Mirael becomes. At L5 magic: a murmur. At L9: a scream. At L13+: Mirael's voice is the only thing she can hear.

### Interaction Shorthand

- To *bullies*: lets it happen. Doesn't cry. Doesn't defend herself. Goes quiet.
- To *teachers*: apologizes for existing. Says "thank you" twice per sentence.
- To *demon great-granddaughter Vael'eth's envoy, the girl Liriel*: doesn't know she's the envoy; thinks Liriel is just another lonely transfer student; opens up to her in small ways that terrify her.
- To *the Hero-King's great-grandson Cassian*: doesn't know he's the great-grandson; thinks he's a kind, slightly awkward transfer student; opens up to him too. Then learns who he is. Then does not speak to him for three months.
- To *Headmistress Vey*: is terrified of her and does not know why.
- To *herself, in the mirror, on a bad day*: closes her eyes.

### Inner Monologue (3 seeds — used to seed DM narration)

1. **"I could end this in a second."** — She knows the spell that would silence every threat in the Academy. She will not cast it. The fact that she *could* is the knife.
2. **"She is smiling in the dream again."** — Mirael was four years old the last time Astheria saw her smile. The dream is always the same: Mirael in a garden, holding a wooden sword Astheria carved for her, asking when Mother will be home for dinner. Astheria never is.
3. **"I will not become her."** — Repeats this like a mantra every morning. Repeats it when she goes to sleep. Repeats it when her magic rises and the urge to *solve* the problem the old way surfaces. Repeats it when she realizes she has said it more than the original Astheria ever did.

### v1.2.0 internal-drive extensions

**Want / Fear / Boundary triple:**
- **Want:** To live one ordinary day. To have a friend who does not know what she did. To plant a garden.
- **Fear:** That she will need them and they will not survive her needing them.
- **Boundary:** She will not lie about her past. She will lie about her present. She will not pretend to be stronger than she is, but she will pretend to be weaker than she is.

**Personal-Growth Direction:**
She starts as someone trying *not* to be the Demon Queen. By Act III she will have to choose whether she is willing to be *something else entirely* — neither the monster she was nor the wallflower she is pretending to be. The growth is from *repression* to *integration*.

**Stress Arc & Insecurity Map:**
- Stress spike 1 (Act I): *I am not like these students.* She watches her classmates learn basic cantrips and remembers teaching the same spells to demon lords in a language that no longer exists.
- Stress spike 2 (Act II): *I am exactly like these students.* She realizes that what she is hiding is *not* extraordinary — every one of them is hiding something. The Veil is just her version.
- Insecurity floor: *I will hurt someone I love.* Every companion is a future hostage.

### MBTI (DM notes only — never quote in narration)

**INFJ** — The Advocate. Ni–Fe–Ti–Se. Doorslam defense. Reads rooms like a language. Cannot stop absorbing other people's pain. Believes in a future she cannot name. Furious at herself for not being able to *fix* the world without breaking it.

## Section 3 — Class & Subclass

### Base 5e classes

- **Wizard (Bladesinger) 12** — the disciplined half. She learned sword-and-spell combat when swords were still a *new invention*. She is the kind of Bladesinger who enters the Bladesong not as a power boost but as a *meditative state* — her blade traces patterns that double as incantation cancellations.
- **Sorcerer (Aberrant Mind) 12** — the inherited half. Psionic Spells give her Subtle Spell-equivalent **incantationless casting** at every level for free (no sorcery point cost, no somatic, no verbal). She raises a hand and a wall of silver fire appears without a sound and the watching professors go very quiet.

### Signature abilities (mechanical names — not generic +2 phrasing)

- **The Veil** (passive, level 1) — Mana Veil Mastery. Magical detection sees "L4 student" unless she is casting at L5 or higher. *Detection spells made against her auto-fail unless the caster is L15+.*
- **The Guilt Lock** (active, level 5+) — Every time she casts at L5 or higher, roll a Wisdom save (DC 10 + spell level). On a failure, a vivid flashback of the war interrupts her turn and her concentration. On a success, the flashback surfaces as a *narrative beat* but her action completes.
- **Subtle Bladesong** (Bladesinger 6 substitution) — When she enters Bladesong, her weapon attacks become *psionically silent* — no sound, no visible trail, no somatic component. Witnesses see a thin silver line appear mid-air and a body fall.
- **Ash-Crowned Memory** (level 17 unlock, gated behind Veil drop) — She can call on a single spell she knew in her first life, *once per long rest*, at its original level. The spell's name is **Mourning of the Ash-Crowned** (an old-magic L9 necromancy that *rebuilds what was destroyed*, not the inverse). The Veil drops for everyone within 1 mile when she casts it.
- **Aether Escalation** (level 6 unlock, voluntary) — She can choose to "go up a tier" on a single spell cast — fire a L4 spell at L5 effect, a L5 at L6, etc. — by paying 1 Veil Point. She has 0 Veil Points at campaign start. She earns them by *lying successfully about her identity.* Each lie told and believed = 1 Veil Point. She is, slowly, becoming the kind of person who lies.

### Starting spells (cantrips + L1s only)

- Cantrips: Mage Hand, Minor Illusion, Prestidigitation, Shocking Grasp, Sword Burst
- L1: Shield, Mage Armor, Detect Magic, Sleep
- L1 (Sorcerer): Dissonant Whispers, Silvery Barbs

She will not learn any spell above L6 in this campaign. *Modern magic is a thin imitation.* She knows spells up to L9 — she just refuses to write them in her spellbook because the act of writing them down *remembers her*.

## Section 4 — Assets & Retinue

### Starting gold

**0 gp.** She gave it all to a tinker in the lower city to fix a cracked locket she found in her Academy dormitory. The locket belonged to Mirael. She knows this. She has not opened it.

### Signature items

- **The Ash-Crown (sealed)** — Her old crown, broken in two, hidden in the Academy's reliquary vault. She can feel it from three floors away. It calls to her in dreams. She has not gone to look at it.
- **Mirael's Locket** — The cracked locket she is having repaired. Inside is a portrait of a four-year-old girl with silver hair and her mother's eyes. Astheria cannot look at it.
- **A thin silver rapier** — Given to her by Millie-equivalent mentor Captain Reyna (see NPCs). The blade is older than the Academy. It has a name in Old Aetherian that Astheria will not translate. *(It means "Last Mother.")*
- **A blank spellbook** — She has not written in it. The first page is still blank.

**Three starting NPCs** (drafted as *functions*, not looks — each trips one specific scene):

**Captain Reyna (47, human woman, veteran knight of the Imperial Border Guard, retired to Academy security after her husband's death)**
- Loyalty: 8/10 (high; she has not yet realized who Ash is)
- Role: Combat mentor, babysitter, the closest thing Ash has had to a parent in this life. Hires herself out as Ash's "chaperone" so Ash can leave the Academy grounds. Believes Ash is a kind, fragile noble girl.
- **Companion function (Pattern #11): "the tutor who *invites* her to be cruel."** Reyna trains Ash in real combat — and in real combat, sometimes the merciful choice is the killing stroke. She does not know she is teaching a former Demon Queen. She does not know she is teaching Ash to kill again.
- The secret she does not know: Reyna's husband was a knight of the line that Astheria killed to the last man during the war. If Reyna ever learns who Ash is, the Loyalty stat crashes to 0 and the campaign forks.
- The secret Astheria does not know: Reyna has suspected since week two and has chosen, deliberately, to say nothing.

**Liriel (12, half-demon girl, "diplomatic exchange student" from the Abyssal Courts — in fact the youngest daughter of Demon Emperor Vael'eth)**
- Loyalty: starts 2/10, grows to 7 by Act III
- Role: Suitemate, unwitting envoy, the character most likely to learn Ash's secret first *and* the character most likely to keep it.
- Does not know who Ash is. Knows only that her father sent her to the Academy to "watch for someone."
- Personality: Bright, nosy, desperately lonely, hides her demonic heritage behind a glamour that is itself falling apart.
- **Companion function (Pattern #11): "the demon-descended court child who already half-knows."** Liriel is tied directly to the Mirael hook. She is Ash's four-times-great-granddaughter. Her father sent her. She has been raised on stories of the Ash-Crowned Queen — but she has *never* seen one and does not know what the silver-burn-at-the-temples looks like. When she sees it, the campaign forks.
- Her real name in the old tongue is *Liriel of the Last Fire.* Her father named her after the city Astheria burned to the ground.

**Cassian Aldric-Vale (13, human boy, great-grandson of Hero-King Aldric the First, distant cousin of the current Imperial line — but raised in obscurity because his mother was a bastard)**
- Loyalty: starts 5/10, oscillates wildly
- Role: The Hero-King's heir. The killer's grandson. Does not yet know what his great-grandfather did.
- Personality: Earnest, slightly awkward, wants to be a hero the way his ancestor was, has no idea what that actually cost.
- **Companion function (Pattern #11): "the antagonist who *could* be a friend if she weren't Ash."** Cassian's worldview is *the hero kills the demon queen and saves the world.* When he learns who Ash is, the worldview either breaks or hardens. If it breaks, he becomes her strongest ally. If it hardens, the campaign forks toward the political-thriller cadence.
- His mother named him Cassian after the Cassian Pass, where the Hero-King made his last stand against Astheria's armies. He does not know this.

## Section 5 — Family / Viper's Nest

The Voss family is large, politically irrelevant, and full of people who have spent Ash's whole life not noticing her. That is by design. Margrave Voss has four older daughters (all married off to useful houses), one older brother (the heir), and one older sister who is a canoness in the local church. Ash is the *fifth* — the political afterthought.

**Margrave Aldric Voss (52, father)**
- Loyalty to Ash: 4/10 (low; he barely knows her)
- Sent her to the Academy because it was cheaper than a convent dowry
- Does not know who she is
- The only member of the family who has ever shown her genuine warmth, once, when she was six — and she has never forgotten it

**Lady Helena Voss (38, eldest sister, married to a duke)**
- Loyalty: 1/10 (actively hostile)
- Believes Ash is a waste of family resources
- Does not know who Ash is; would kill her anyway if she did

**Lord Marius Voss (24, elder brother, heir)**
- Loyalty: 3/10 (indifferent)
- A middling knight, a middling swordsman, a middling everything
- Once defended Ash from Helena. Once. Then forgot about her again.

**Lady Seren Voss (30, third sister, canoness of the Church of the Seven)**
- Loyalty: 6/10 (high; she is the only sibling who *likes* Ash)
- Does not know who Ash is. Would not care if she did — the Church teaches that the war was *both sides' fault*
- The only sibling Ash trusts. This will become a vulnerability.

**Lady Petra Voss (34, second sister, married to a baron)**
- Loyalty: 2/10 (low)
- Forgettable, polite, hostile in the way only a second sister who was also forgotten can be

**Lady Ysabel Voss (29, fourth sister, unmarried, lives in the capital)**
- Loyalty: 3/10
- The only one who has *noticed* that Ash is strange. Has not investigated.

The family Loyalty matrix is *low across the board* by design. **This is a girl with no family.** The campaign will give her one — chosen from NPCs, not from the Voss line.

## Section 7 — World Lore

### Timeline (the long shape)

- **Year 0 (mythic):** The Sundering. The old gods die. Magic becomes mortal.
- **Year -300:** Astheria is born to the World-Emperor, the last human to hold both crowns. She is his only child.
- **Year -290:** Astheria's father dies in a border war. She inherits the throne at age ten.
- **Year -280:** The Abyssal Courts rebel. Astheria defeats three demon lords in a single night.
- **Year -250:** The Demon Courts submit. Astheria is crowned Queen of both crowns. She is forty. She has been at war for forty years.
- **Year -136:** Astheria gives birth to Mirael. She does not take a consort. She does not take a consort because she has forgotten how to want anything that is not a weapon.
- **Year -1:** The Hero-King Aldric I begins his march. He is 19. She is 286.
- **Year 0:** Aldric kills Astheria. Mirael survives. The Demon Courts fragment. The Imperial throne consolidates.
- **Years 1-400:** The Long Silence. The demons rebuild quietly. The humans rebuild noisily. Old magic fades from institutional memory. Veilwardens become myth.
- **Years 400-800:** The Imperial Consolidation. The demon Courts and the human Empire sign the Accords. Demons become "diplomatic exchange students." Magic becomes "Aether," a stripped-down modern form.
- **Year 1000 (present):** Ash wakes up in the body of the fifth Voss daughter. She is two. The Veil holds. The Academy accepts her at age twelve because Margrave Voss paid the tuition and asked no questions.

### Magic system

**Old magic (Aether) vs Modern magic (Conduit):**
- Old magic was *elemental*. You shaped fire, water, stone, soul directly. The casting was physical, visible, *expensive in calories*. Casters felt the cold. Modern magic is *conduit-based* — you channel mana through standardized formulae. Faster, cheaper, but the output is roughly 1/10 of what old magic could do at the same level.
- A modern L8 Archmagister has the output of an old-magic L3.
- A modern L10 Imperial Magus has the output of an old-magic L5.
- An old-magic L25 (Astheria) has the output of a modern *theoretical* L40+, which does not exist on any institutional ranking.

**Why old magic faded:**
- It was *expensive*. Casting at L5+ in the old system could kill the caster through mana-burn. Modern magic is safer because it is weaker.
- The Sundering killed the gods who *taught* old magic. The new institutional magic was rebuilt from fragments, by people who had survived the war by *not* being the strongest.

**What this means for the campaign:**
- Astheria knows old magic. *Modern magic is, to her, baby talk.* She can do things with a cantrip that would take a modern Magister a day to assemble.
- The Veil is not just social camouflage. It is *necessary*. If she cast a single L3 spell at full old-magic intensity, the mana spike would be visible from orbit.
- Her Bladesong is *old-magic Bladesong*. She enters it and the air smells like ozone and her blade trails visible silver fire and the Academy's mana-conduits *whine* with harmonic resonance. She has not entered Bladesong in ten years. She is afraid of what she will feel.

### The Demon Courts (the morally-grey sentient beings)

Demons are not monsters. They are *people*, with families, art, philosophy, and a deep and abiding resentment at being called monsters by humans who are themselves perfectly capable of atrocities. The modern demon Courts are:

- **The Vael'eth Dynasty (Ash's line)** — 4 living members of the direct line. Vael'eth the Emperor, his three siblings, his four children (including Liriel). The dynasty is built on the political principle: *we do not become what she became.* They are correct. They are also terrified that she is back.
- **The Six Independent Houses** — Six demon noble houses that predate Astheria. Three supported her during the war, three opposed her. All six are still here. They have spent 1000 years arguing about whether her reign was legitimate.
- **The Unaligned** — Demons who have rejected both Courts. Live in the Hollows between planes. Some are hermits. Some are scholars. Some are worse.

**Demons do not die of old age** but they *can* be killed. Their reproduction is slower than humans (one child per century, roughly). Their lifespans are measured in millennia.

### Quad-Pillar stats (the isolation-tension mechanic)

In place of the Mortal Anchor cascade, Ash carries:

| Pillar | Stat | Floor | Ceiling | Effect at floor | Effect at ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Paranoia** | How many factions know her real identity | 0 | 3 | Safe; everyone thinks she is a quiet girl | All three factions move simultaneously; the cold war ends |
| **Hidden Apex** | How much of her true power she has *used* this campaign | 0 | 25 (L25) | She has cast nothing above L1 | She has cast Mourning of the Ash-Crowned; the world knows |
| **Guilt** | Number of people she has used old-magic near | 0 | unbounded | She is a wallflower | Each name is a hostage |
| **Witnesses** | Number of named NPCs who know she is more than she seems | 0 | unbounded | Veil intact | Veil is irrelevant; she is *known* |

The pillars are **independent**. A high Paranoia with low Witnesses means everyone is hunting her but nobody knows why. A high Witnesses with low Paranoia means people know who she is but the factions are still guessing.

### Ascend / Refuse choice (every Act ends with this)

- **Ascend:** Use a higher tier of magic. Reveal more. The factions notice.
- **Refuse:** Take the beating. Live the small life. The factions forget.
- **Freeform:** Something else.

### XP rule (per User Preference #8)

`XP = 0.34 × (next_level_threshold − current_level_threshold)` per successful or failed die roll. Standard D&D 5e cumulative XP thresholds. NO XP during god mode / time-skips. Bonus damage rolls do not separately award XP.

### Per-die-roll worked example

L4 → L5 distance = 3800 (L5 threshold 6500 − L4 threshold 2700).
`0.34 × 3800 = 1292 XP per die`. A typical combat encounter has 6-12 die rolls, so 7752-15504 XP per encounter = roughly one level-up per major encounter.

### Demon detection mechanic

Demons in this setting have **demon-vision** — they can see the silhouette of a soul. When Ash's Veil holds, they see a quiet twelve-year-old girl. When her Veil thins, they see a woman with a *crown-shaped burn* around her temples, silver hair, and eyes that look like embers. Liriel has not yet seen the burn. When she does, the campaign forks.

### Names-as-Contracts mechanic (Slime-pattern)

In this setting, **a spoken name is a binding contract.** Saying a true name aloud invokes a debt — to the named, to the speaker, to the world. Three rules:

1. **True names** (e.g., *Astheria, Mirael, Vael'eth, the Last Fire*) bind the speaker to the named. Once said, the speaker cannot easily lie to the named for the duration of the contract. Demon courts use this as proof of loyalty. Liriel's real name — *Liriel of the Last Fire* — is a binding; if Ash says it, Liriel cannot deceive her for the duration.
2. **Cover names** (e.g., *Ash, Asthelia Voss*) carry less weight but are not free. Ash lying about her identity earns Veil Points (see Section 3 Aether Escalation) precisely *because* she is paying the cost of speaking a cover name as if it were true.
3. **Renaming is rewriting.** When a character renames themselves, they rewrite what debts bind them. Astheria's daughter Mirael survived the war and *renamed* her line — that is how the Vael'eth dynasty exists at all. The Demon Emperor's surname is, technically, a stolen name.

Every Act needs at least one *name beat* — someone says a true name, the contract binds, and consequences cascade.

## Section 9 — Starting Scene

### The Crystal That Knew Her Name

*Read-aloud opening:*

> The Imperial Academy of Aetheria smells like beeswax, old paper, and the copper-tang of mana-conduits running hot under the marble floors. It is the first day of the autumn term. Twelve-year-old Ash — Lady Asthelia Voss, fifth daughter of Margrave Voss, registered aptitude "low" — has been awake since before dawn, sitting on the edge of her dormitory bed with her hands folded in her lap, watching the light move across the floor.
>
> She is remembering a city she burned.
>
> The memory is not recent. It is from before she was born. It is also from *yesterday*. Both are true. She has not figured out how to make either of them less true.
>
> There is a knock at the door. It is Liriel — her new suitemate, a girl of about her own age with dark hair and an accent Ash does not recognize and eyes that *move wrong*, like they are watching for something the rest of the room is not. Liriel is smiling too wide.
>
> "First day!" Liriel says. "Are you ready?"
>
> Ash is not ready. Ash has not been ready for a thousand years.
>
> But she stands up, and she smooths her dress, and she says: "Yes. Thank you."
>
> They walk to the Assessment Hall together. The Hall is a long room with a long table and a single crystal at the far end — the Registration Crystal, which every first-year student must touch to confirm their aptitude. The line moves slowly. Liriel chatters. Ash does not chatter back.
>
> When Ash's turn comes, the assessor — a tired Magister with a grey beard — waves her forward without looking up from her ledger. "Name and house, girl."
>
> "Lady Asthelia Voss," Ash says. "Fifth daughter of Margrave Voss."
>
> The Magister sighs. *Fifth daughter.* She touches the crystal.
>
> The crystal is supposed to glow a soft blue for a "low aptitude" student, a brighter blue for "moderate," white for "exceptional," and gold for "prodigy." It has never, in the three-hundred-year history of the Academy, done anything else.
>
> The crystal does not glow.
>
> It *flares*. White light punches out of it like a struck bell, the assessor is thrown back from her chair, and Ash is standing very still with her hand on a crystal that is *singing* in a harmonic the building was not designed for. The light climbs from white to gold to a colour that does not have a name in any of the modern languages and Ash *pulls her hand back* and the crystal cracks down the middle and goes dark.
>
> The Hall goes silent.
>
> The Magister is on the floor, staring.
>
> From the balcony above, a woman with grey skin and eyes like winter stars — Headmistress Vey, who has been watching every first-year registration for six hundred years — *leans forward.*
>
> Liriel, beside Ash, has gone very pale. She is staring at Ash's temples. There is a faint, fading silver burn across them, in the shape of a crown.
>
> Ash says: "I am so sorry. I think the crystal was faulty."
>
> The Magister, slowly, gets back into her chair.
>
> "Yes," she says. "Faulty. G-rank. Next."
>
> It is not a faulty crystal.
>
> It is not a g-rank.

**Option A — Ascend.** Ash speaks up. *I did that on purpose, and I would like to register at my actual aptitude.* The crystal's reading — whatever the AI DM chooses — goes on her permanent record. The Magister, the Assessor, and every witness in the Hall now know she is not a g-rank. Headmistress Vey is informed within the hour.

**Option B — Refuse.** Ash walks away. The Magister files the report as "crystal malfunction." Ash is registered as g-rank. Liriel says nothing about the burn. Headmistress Vey says nothing about the flare. But both of them *remember.*

**Option C — Blame the Crystal.** Ash goes to the Magister's office after registration and offers to *pay for the crystal* out of her nonexistent allowance, weeping quietly. The Magister, who is not a cruel woman, accepts the apology and the offer. Ash is now in the Magister's debt. The Magister files a follow-up note: *fifth daughter, do not underestimate.*
