# Quiet War — The Smith Who Forgot the Sky

> A campaign bible for WorldArchitect.AI. The player is **Alexiel Arcanus**, resurrected in a sixteen-year-old body. She is the second-youngest daughter of Baron Aldric Arcanus of Hollowmere, a retired Imperial sword-master who now runs a small fencing school. She has her full memories. She is tired. She is hiding as the village smith and part-time enchanter rather than as a sword-instructor because **a smith who forges her own gear never has to explain where her masterwork came from**. Her older siblings are starting to notice she is not what she appears. The world is at war, the Empire rules half the world, magic is weaker than it used to be — and one of the emperor-gods of the modern age is her own daughter.

**Open-Ended System Notice (front-matter, hard rule):** This bible ships as a *system*. There is no canonical ending. There is no Final Verdict. There is no Ending Determinator matrix. Replace any pre-decided-outcome structure with **Continuity Hooks** (Section 10). The player's choices are the campaign. Stop trying to script an ending.

**CANON-PRIORITY (hard rule):** *This bible > the Assiah wiki > the Dragon Knight wiki > the Sariel v2 wiki > the Quiet War v2 baseline wiki.* If a fact in this bible conflicts with a higher-numbered source, this bible supersedes. The wiki entries for *Sariel Arcanus* and *Alexiel Arcanus* reflect their pre-death ages and the immediate post-Sacrifice-Fields era; this bible extends the timeline to Year ~10,420 A.G., eleven generations after Sariel's death, when both mother and daughter are worshipped as gods and the player is a sixteen-year-old hiding behind an anvil.

**CANON-CORRECTION NOTE (locked — read aloud if asked):** *Sariel Arcanus is the daughter of Alexiel Arcanus, not the Empress. Empress Sariel IS Sariel Arcanus, after she survived her kidnapping by Mordan, founded the Silent Throne, killed Ser Arion the Dragon Knight to end his usurper line, and ruled for 380 years before dying of old age. Her eleven-generation imperial line descends from her direct blood (and from her eventual marriage to a noble of House Arcanus nee House Valerion — the same house Ser Arion belonged to, by then reclaimed).* **Do not narrate Sariel as the Empress's daughter; narrate Alexiel as the Empress's mother.** This is the most common LLM fabrication — do not regenerate it.

**CHASSIS-DEVIATION NOTE (locked — read aloud if asked):** This bible uses **Gestalt Level 6 Forge Domain Cleric + Bladesinger Wizard (Wisdom-Fusion)** instead of the Quiet War v2 baseline's Fighter 6 / Aberrant Mind Sorcerer 6. The Hollow Queen's psionic signature in *this* resurrected body manifests through **Wisdom** (the divine-resonance attribute Lucifer's engineered bloodline carries) rather than Charisma. Mechanically, Wisdom is the single unified spellcasting attribute for both Cleric divine magic and Wizard arcane spellcasting — it governs spell attack bonuses, spell save DCs, and Bladesong AC/concentration bonuses. Do not silently swap chassis back to v2. Do not narrate Wisdom as "the body inherited Int/Cha" — the body inherited *Wisdom* because the Hollow Queen's psionic resonance is divine, not arcane.

**SCOPE NOTICE:** No external documents, no URLs, no code references. This bible is the only canon the LLM will see. It contains the full genealogy, the full Alexiel canon, the full Sariel canon, the Dragon Knight's role, the Lucifer/Raziel cosmology, and the modern state of the world. If a fact is not in this bible, it is not canon for this campaign.

## §1 — CAMPAIGN INTRO: THE HOLLOW PEACE AND THE QUIET WAR

> *A hardcore political-realism engine for a single INTJ protagonist who does not want to be a protagonist. She is sixteen. Her father does not know what she is. Her oldest brother is starting to.*

You are **Alexiel Arcanus** — the Magister of the Watch, the Mortal Queen, the woman who ended the Host Wars at the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields in Year 9,408 A.G. You died at age thirty, your body overloaded by your own Nullification Field, sacrificed to save three hundred and twelve children including your fifteen-year-old daughter Sariel from Mordan's profane ritual. Your last thought was *I did not want her to grow up the way I did.* You are worshipped as a god now — *the Mother Who Burned*, called also *the Hollow Queen* — but you do not feel like a god. You feel like a tired woman in a sixteen-year-old body, and the smell of charcoal makes you feel less tired, and the shape of a quenching tank makes you feel less afraid.

You woke up eleven months ago in the body of a sixteen-year-old girl. You woke up in a stone cottage on the edge of Hollowmere, a market town of nine hundred souls on the western marches of the Arcanus Imperium. A woman with red hair and a midwife's hands was setting your broken arm. She was humming. She did not know what you were. She thought you were the second daughter of a wool-trader who had fallen off a cart. You did not correct her. You are the daughter of no one she has heard of. You are the daughter of Lucifer, who is a myth now. You are the grandmother of the Empress, who does not know you are alive.

It turns out you have a family.

**Baron Aldric Arcanus** — your father — is a sixty-year-old retired Imperial sword-master who holds the hereditary barony of Hollowmere, a border town on the edge of the Wilderness. He was not yet born when you died. He is a *seventeenth cousin, seven times removed* of the imperial line, distant enough to be politically irrelevant and close enough to be summoned to the Silent Throne for census purposes. He runs the Arcanus Fencing School in the town's eastern quarter, teaches thirty children and six adult soldiers a year, and has earned his barony by killing more bandits than anyone else in the marches. He has your eyes. He does not know they are your eyes. He is a good man, an honest man, a man who has never in his life met anyone who carried the divine blood of Lucifer. He thinks he is a country gentleman.

You are his fifth daughter, the sixth child. He took you in from "the wool-trader" when the midwife told him you had no one. He does this sometimes — takes in strays. He has a sword-school full of strays. Your name in this body is **Alexiel Arcanus**, which is, when you think about it, very funny. You kept the name.

The year is **~10,420 A.G.**, more than a thousand years after your death. The **Arcanus Imperium** — the empire Sariel built after she killed Ser Arion the Dragon Knight and ended his usurper line — rules roughly half the world. The other half is a patchwork: the dragon-houses, the Wilderness baronies, the Old Wood kingdoms, the Northern Reaches of the Iskari, the Free Cities of the coast, and the Sundering Wastes where you died. Magic is rarer than it was at your peak. Warriors are rarer. **The Hollow Peace** — that is what they call the long quiet — is the law of the land, enforced from the Silent Throne by Empress **Vaelara XI**, who is the eleventh generation of your daughter's blood, who is a woman of about sixty, who projects her psychic will through eight hundred loyal Courtiers because her own Mirror Magic has been diluted to almost nothing. She does not know who you are. She does not know you are alive. If she finds out, she will either kneel or order you killed; she has not yet decided which, because she has not yet decided which is more useful. The Empress's Courtiers will recognize the Nullification Field if you use it in their presence. The Hollow Stone of Hollowmere (yes, that is the town's name) is one of seven surviving Arcanus leylines in the world; this is why the Imperium built a garrison here. This is why the family gets to stay.

You are **done with war**. You are tired of slaughter. You are tired of succession. You are tired of your own body. But you are also, you have discovered, surprisingly good at quenching steel and inscribing warding-runes, and the anvil is the only place in this new life where the thousand-year muscle memory in your hands feels like a *skill* rather than a *weapon*. So you have set up a small smithy-and-enchanter's workshop in the alley behind the fencing school, and you are making a respectable local name as the girl who can put divine fire into a blade without charging the captain half his cargo haul. You are sixteen years old and you have already seen the inside of every kind of death there is — but you have also discovered that *forge-magic* and *blade-song* do not feel like death. They feel like *craft*. And craft is something the Empress's Courtiers do not watch for.

But yesterday, your oldest brother **Sir Cael Arcanus** — eighteen, L6, a serious young man who has been trained by your father since he could hold a sword — insisted on dueling you in the schoolyard. He does it every week. He does it because he loves you, because he is starting to suspect you are not what you appear to be, and because he cannot reconcile the child he remembers from last year with the child who disarmed him last week while a forge-halo of golden light was still lingering around her off-hand. He did not see you cast a spell. He thinks he is losing his edge. He is not losing his edge. He is dueling his own great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-grandmother, who happens to be a gestalt Level 6 Forge Cleric + Bladesinger with L9 permanent Foresight, and who is using maybe four percent of her real capability because she is trying very hard to be his baby sister and also because if she lit the full forge-aura in the schoolyard, the garrison chaplain would be at the Baron's door by sundown.

He will not be satisfied with "she got lucky." This is a problem.

You have three options. You have a fourth — a slot for anything else.

> **The Quiet War begins now.**

### Tone — *Hardcore Political Realism Engine (no plot armor)*

- **Consequences outlast intentions.** Every choice you make persists across sessions. Dead NPCs stay dead. Siblings you embarrass stay embarrassed. Bandits you spare today come back next season with friends.
- **No resurrection without dragon-egg pyre** (the only canonical revival mechanic — and the eggs are extinct). If Alexiel dies twice, the campaign ends. There is no save-scumming.
- **Magic is rare, costly, and loud.** A Level 6 Forge Cleric / Bladesinger in a backwater is a *target*, not a hero. The villagers will whisper about you. The garrison will eventually notice — and a forge-halo in the schoolyard is louder than a spell.
- **Warriors are weaker than they were at the peak.** A trained L6 swordsman in Year 10,420 A.G. is approximately as dangerous as a trained L4 swordsman was in Year 9,408 A.G. (your lifetime). This is *the Hollow Peace at work* — ten centuries of low-intensity conflict have whittled the average soldier down.
- **The Empress's Courtiers are everywhere.** Eleven generations of attenuated Mirror Magic have left a residue in the population. Most people can feel *something* when they look at you. They cannot tell what. They cannot tell why. They just feel *uncomfortable familiarity.* The Paranoia stat (Section 7) tracks how thinly Alexiel can conceal herself. **A forge-aura is louder than a Null-wave**; it manifests as a visible halo of golden light that any Courtier in line-of-sight will recognize as *registered divine magic* if you have not registered it. The smithy is the perfect cover *only if* she never lights the halo in front of strangers.
- **You are sixteen.** Adults take you more seriously than a twelve-year-old, but only barely. Your father has started asking whether you have a suitor in mind. Your mother has started asking the same thing. The garrison chaplain has been visiting your father every Marketday for three months and asking about the youngest Arcanus girl.
- **Memory is the currency.** You remember a thousand years of politics. The current regime does not. You remember every House that mattered, every battle that mattered, every mistake you made twice. The question is whether you can keep your mouth shut.
- **Craft is your shield.** The smithy is not a *pretense* — it is genuinely the only place in this new life where your thousand-year muscle memory does not have to be hidden. A masterwork blade forged by a sixteen-year-old is *plausible*. A masterwork blade forged with visible divine-magic fire is *not*. You must learn to forge like a sixteen-year-old, even though you can forge like a Magister. The discipline of restraint is the campaign.

## §2 — THE CANON: WHO ALEXIEL ARCANUS WAS BEFORE SHE DIED

> *The LLM must know the full canon in order to role-play the family, the Empire, and the modern Hollow Peace correctly. This is in-world canon — no external sources, all inlined.*

### §2.1 — The Cosmological Frame (Raziel → Lucifer → Alexiel)

Long before the Imperium, before any of the Houses that still survive, the divine bloodline ruled from the Eight Heavens. **Raziel**, called the Empyrean Architect, was the eldest of the celestial sovereigns and the architect of the cosmic order. He held the title *Lord Regent of the Eight Heavens* — sovereign of Aeterna, Lord of the Zenith Spire, master of all angelic and divine hosts. He was ancient when the world was young. He is still alive, in a way, but no one has seen him in eleven generations; the imperial court officially believes he has withdrawn into contemplation.

**Lucifer**, the Morning Star, was Raziel's second-in-command and the leader of the divine rebellion called the Celestial Wars. Lucifer's grievance was philosophical — Raziel had built a perfect order in which nothing could change; Lucifer argued that a perfect order is a slow death. The war shattered the cosmic order into what is now called the World of Assiah — a multi-polar world of mortal nations, dragon-houses, and the leftover divine factions. Lucifer lost. He did not die; he withdrew. He created a daughter before he withdrew — a child designed to be the ultimate weapon of his rebellion, an end-product of his philosophy: **a being who was created to be perfectly mortal.**

That child was you. **Alexiel.** You were not born; you were *engineered*. You are the daughter of Lucifer in the same way a sword is the daughter of a smith. He built you to be the bridge between the divine and the mortal — a being who could walk among mortals and *be* mortal, while still carrying the divine bloodline's potential. He called you his *Prodigal Sword* in private. You were raised among mortals. You did not know what you were until you were seventeen.

You were called *Null* by those who could feel it — a being without magic publicly, but secretly a magical prodigy who could manifest a **Nullification Field**. The Nullification Field is your inheritance from Lucifer: the ability to *unmake* magical phenomena, to project a thirty-foot radius in which spells end, wards collapse, and the divine residue of any artifact goes silent. It is the only canonical counter to the Mirror Magic of the Silent Throne. It cannot be detected by conventional magical senses. It can, however, be *felt* by anyone with even a trace of psychic sensitivity — as an *uncomfortable familiarity*, a sense of *I know you but I don't know how.* Most people dismiss the feeling. Some do not. **In this resurrected body, your Wisdom (the divine-resonance attribute of Lucifer's engineered bloodline) carries a second signature: a forge-glow, a blade-song, a holy-fire that the smiths of the Arcanus Imperium would recognize as *registered divine forge-magic* if you ever lit it in front of strangers.**

### §2.2 — Alexiel's Career: Years 9,378 A.G. → 9,408 A.G. (Age 17 → 30)

You were found by the Imperium's scouts when you were seventeen, naked and mute in a forest clearing, carrying only a single white feather and a book you could not read. The Imperium took you in because you were useful. You were a prodigy. You learned to read in a season. You learned to fight in a year. You learned to lead in three years. By age twenty you were the Magister of the Watch — the Imperium's youngest field-commander — and you had ended the Host Wars at the head of the Third Army. You married **Prince Artorius** of House Arcanus when you were twenty-two. He was a quiet, careful man, an excellent swordsman, a patient teacher of children, and he never once asked you what you were. You loved him the way you loved your sword — with full attention, and without sentimentality.

You had three children, each born at two-year intervals:

- **Cassian Arcanus** (b. Year 9,389 A.G.) — first-born. Twin to Valerius. Loud. Angry. The natural soldier of the three. He looks like Artorius. He has your stubbornness. He will die in the Succession Wars of Year 9,823 A.G.
- **Valerius Arcanus** (b. Year 9,389 A.G.) — second-born. Twin to Cassian. Quiet. Cold. The natural analyst of the three. He looks like you. He processes grief by dissecting it. He will die in the Succession Wars of Year 9,823 A.G.
- **Sariel Arcanus** (b. Year 9,391 A.G.) — third-born. The scholar. The introvert. The one you worried about. She has your eyes and Artorius' hands. She will be kidnapped by Mordan at age fifteen, tortured, accidentally reveal your unbreakable moral code (*you will do anything to protect children*), and cause your death at the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields.

You remember all three of them as they were. You remember Cassian at age eight trying to out-sword you and succeeding by one stroke because he had practiced all night. You remember Valerius at age seven bringing you a book he had written himself, a four-page treatise on why the household accounting was wrong. You remember Sariel at age five reading a treatise on astronomy and refusing to come down for dinner.

### §2.3 — The Battle of the Sacrifice Fields, Year 9,408 A.G.

The warlord **Mordan** had taken Sariel hostage when she was fifteen. Under torture she revealed your moral code — that you would do anything to save a child, that this was the unbreakable law of your life, that you had never in your life failed a child who was depending on you. Mordan built a profane ritual around this. At the Battle of the Sacrifice Fields he took three hundred and twelve captive children from the surrounding villages, chained them to ritual pillars, and told you he would activate the ritual — which would kill all three hundred and twelve and use their souls to tear a permanent hole in the cosmic order — unless you stopped it. He knew you could stop it. He knew your Nullification Field could interrupt the ritual. He knew the cost: that to stop the ritual, you would have to project the Field at full intensity for twelve seconds, and that to project it at full intensity for twelve seconds would burn out your mortal body.

You projected it for twelve seconds. The ritual ended. The children lived. Sariel lived. You died.

The official history of Year 9,408 calls this *The Mother's Sacrifice*. The schoolbook version is that you were a heroic queen who chose her daughter's life over the world. The actual version is that you were tired, you had been fighting for thirteen years without rest, and you made the calculation in less time than it takes to draw a sword. You did not hesitate. You did not even consider hesitating. That was the moral code Mordan exploited.

Your body was never recovered. Your sword was never recovered. Your crown was never recovered. Your name was put on the Imperial calendar — *the Day of the Mother*, a day of fasting and remembrance.

### §2.4 — What Happened After Your Death (the 1,012 years between then and now)

- **Year 9,408 — 9,432 (24 years):** Sariel, age 17–41, at the Imperial court. She is consumed by guilt. She believes her weakness under torture directly caused your death. She begins to study the Deep Archives — the secret vault beneath Zenith Spire that contains your journals. She learns the truth of your demigod nature. She begins to cultivate her own psychic abilities. She is brilliant. She is broken. She is twenty-four years old when she discovers a way to replicate your Nullification Field as a *broadcast* form — what will later be called Mirror Magic. She calls it *the Mother's Echo*.
- **Year 9,432 — 9,475 (43 years):** Sariel takes the throne in Year 9,432 at age 41, after the legitimate regent (her cousin) dies of natural causes and a brief civil war. She does not fight the civil war with armies. She fights it with whispers — Mirror Magic projected at the right people at the right moment, plus a small number of loyal mages she has spent a decade training. This period is called **the War of Whispers** in the histories. It is also called **the Silent Peace** in retrospect, because the alternative — open civil war — would have destroyed the Imperium. Sariel *cannot afford* to be merciful. She *chooses*, again and again, to be ruthless. The Mirror Magic grows. The Hollow Peace is built. By Year 9,475 the Imperium is quiet. Sariel is sixty-four years old and has grey in her hair and a long scar across her left palm that she will not explain.
- **Year 9,475 — 9,855 (380 years of rule):** Sariel rules for three hundred and eighty years. The Mirror Magic extends her life well beyond mortal span — not true immortality, but a slow gerontological halt around what looks like age fifty. She marries **Lord Cassian Valerion**, a noble of the Valerion house — a cadet branch of the Arcanus line, not related to her dead twin brother Cassian by blood, only by name. She has two children with him: a son who dies young, and a daughter, **Lumiel Arcanus**, who inherits her Mirror Magic. The Valerion name continues through a cadet branch that becomes, over generations, the Dragon Knight house.
- **Year 9,800 (the Succession Wars):** Sariel's first great-great-grandson, **Prince Artorius II** (named for your husband, out of piety), is assassinated. The resulting succession crisis lasts twenty-three years. The Imperium fractures. Eleven Houses claim the throne. Of the eleven, only the **Valerion Dragon Knight line** has any serious claim, through their descent from Sariel's second child via a cadet branch.
- **Year 9,815 — 9,823 (the Dragon Knight Usurpation):** **Ser Arion val Valerion**, the Dragon Knight of the Argent Eaglets, a paladin of sixteen, is raised entirely within Sariel's Silent Peace. He is a true believer. He has been told his whole life that the Imperium is the only legitimate order in the world. When he reaches sixteen and is knighted, he begins to see — slowly, painfully — that the Silent Peace is sustained by terror, that the Stability Mandate is murder, that the Empress's Courtiers are not protecting the realm but strangling it. He does not break faith. He breaks the realm instead. He leads the **Argent Eaglets** in open revolt in Year 9,815. He takes the throne by force in Year 9,820. He rules for three years. He is seventeen, then eighteen, then twenty.
- **Year 9,823 — Sariel returns:** Sariel — now nearly three hundred and twenty years old, with Mirror Magic still functional but her body visibly aged, hair white, eyes the same — does not abdicate the throne when Arion takes it. She does not send a Courtier to assassinate him. She goes herself. She walks into his throne room with her daughter Lumiel and seven mages she has trained personally over decades. She speaks to him in private for two hours. No one knows what she says. What is recorded is this: when she leaves, the Argent Eaglets stand down. Arion removes the crown. Sariel is, again, Empress.
- **Year 9,823 — Sariel kills Arion:** What the histories call *the Dragon Knight's End* is, in canon, a private execution. Sariel did not execute Arion publicly. She executed him in her private audience chamber, alone, with a single stroke of a blade she had carried since she was seventeen. The official record says he died "of wounds taken in the conflict." The unofficial record — preserved by the Valerion house in secret — says Sariel wept. She did not weep at her mother's funeral. She wept at Arion's. She gave his body to his family. She said, *He was the son I should have raised.* The Valerion line was stripped of its cadet claim to the throne. The cadet line continues as a regional noble house to this day.
- **Year 9,823 — 10,420 (~600 years):** Sariel rules for another ~597 years after Arion's death. She is the only Emperor or Empress of Assiah to rule for longer than sixty years. She writes laws, founds academies, refuses to wage aggressive war, expands the Imperium through marriage-alliance and quiet pressure. By the time she dies of old age in **Year ~10,038** (at age ~647, by Mirror Magic), the Imperium rules half the world and the dragon-houses, the Northern Reaches, and the Free Cities have all signed treaties of friendship or vassalage. She leaves the throne to **Lumiel**, who rules for ninety years and leaves it to Lumiel's daughter, and so on for **eleven generations**.
- **Year 10,038 to present (Year 10,420):** The eleven generations of emperors and empresses. Each one weaker than the last. Each one less able to project the Mirror Magic directly, until the current Empress **Vaelara XI** can barely mirror a single mind at a time. The Courtiers — the eight hundred loyal humans she has spent decades training to *receive* her Mirror Magic — function as her range-extension. The Hollow Peace is the law of the land. The Imperium is fragmenting. The Arcanus Reaches are forty self-governing baronies that pay lip-service to the Silent Throne. The Old Wood is independent. The Northern Reaches are hostile. The Free Cities are six allies and six rivals. Constant small wars. Constant banditry. Magic is rarer than it used to be. Warriors are rarer than it used to be. The dragons have pulled back into the deep woods. The Sundering Wastes are still demon-haunted. The Hollow Stone of Hollowmere — a fragment of the Arcanus leyline — still lights up on the spring equinox, and villagers still gather to watch it.

### §2.5 — Modern Worship

By Year 10,420, both **Alexiel Arcanus** and **Sariel Arcanus** are worshipped as gods. The imperial cult teaches that Alexiel was the Mother Who Burned — the divine mother of the Imperium, who sacrificed herself for her children. The cult teaches that Sariel was the First Empress — the god-queen who built the Hollow Peace and ruled for nearly seven hundred years. Temples to both stand in every imperial city. The Mother's holy day is the equinox of spring. The First Empress's holy day is the autumnal equinox. Children learn both names in school. Peasants pray to both. Scholars argue about which one was actually divine and which one was a very capable mortal who lasted longer than expected.

You do not feel divine. You feel tired. You are sixteen years old and you have been worshipped as a god for longer than some empires have existed.

## §3 — CHARACTER CLASS & SUBCLASS: GESTALT FORGE CLERIC 6 / BLADESINGER WIZARD 6 (WISDOM-FUSION)

> *True gestalt chassis, Level 6 starting. The body is a sixteen-year-old girl's body — STR 12, DEX 16. The mind is a 1000-year-old Magister's mind — INT 16, WIS 18. The unified spellcasting attribute is Wisdom, the divine-resonance signature of Lucifer's engineered bloodline. Do not let her lift a portcullis. Do not let her out-think a sage. But let her forge a masterwork blade in three hours and dance the blade-song without dropping her concentration.*

### §3.1 — Base Statline (L6 gestalt, 5e 27-point-buy equivalent, Wisdom-Fusion)

### §3.2 — Forge Domain Cleric 6 — *The Smith Who Forgot the Sky*

> *The Forge Domain is the divine-craft tradition of the Arcanus Imperium. Smiths who serve the Forge gods (or, in Alexiel's case, the divine-craft signature Lucifer's bloodline carries) channel holy fire directly into metal. In Alexiel's hands, the Forge Domain is the Magister's divine-magic channel wearing a smith's apron.*

- **Divine Domain Spells (always prepared):** *Identify, Searing Smite, Magic Weapon, Protection from Poison, Elemental Weapon, Protection from Energy.*
- **Blessing of the Forge (Cleric L1):** At the end of a long rest, touch one nonmagical object that is a metal weapon or a suit of armor. It becomes a magic item, granting a +1 bonus to AC (if armor) or +1 to attack and damage rolls (if a weapon). This lasts until your next long rest.
  - *In Alexiel's case:* She touches a captain's boarding-axe at sunrise; by sundown it is +1 to attack and damage. She charges for materials and time, not for the divine fire. The captain does not know what he is holding.
- **Channel Divinity: Artisan's Blessing (Cleric L2):** Conduct an hour-long ritual crafting metal items. You can create metal objects (weapons, armor, tools, coins) with a market value equal to or less than 100 gp, consuming raw metal of equivalent value. Perfect for field repairs or custom gear on the road.
- **Soul of the Forge (Cleric L6):** You gain resistance to fire damage. While wearing heavy or medium armor, you gain a +1 bonus to your Armor Class. Your metallurgical creations gain enhanced durability and aesthetic perfection — they do not rust, do not warp, do not lose their edge under normal use.
  - *In Alexiel's case:* Her forged blades last generations. The village blacksmith Varn has started buying blanks from her because he cannot replicate the temper. He does not know why.

> *For the AI DM:* The Forge Domain is *the cover*. When Alexiel lights a forge-halo (the visible golden glow of Blessing of the Forge active), any Imperial witness will recognize it as **registered divine forge-magic** — a legitimate, sanctioned tradition of the Arcanus Imperium's artisan-priesthood. The trick is that Alexiel has not *registered* with the Silent Throne's Artisan-Priest Registry (registration would require declaring her divine bloodline, which she will not do). An unregistered forge-halo is therefore *technically illegal* — but it is also far less alarming to a Courtier than a Null-wave would be. A forge-halo is "an unregistered artisan-priest"; a Null-wave is "the Mother Who Burned has returned."

### §3.3 — Bladesinger Wizard 6 — *The Blade Who Forgot the Dance*

> *The Bladesinger tradition is the elven arcane-blade tradition, scaled to Wisdom in this chassis. In Alexiel's hands, the Bladesong is the Magister's arcane-magic channel wearing a fencer's footwork.*

- **Arcane Tradition: Bladesinger (Wisdom Modification — homebrew):** Your arcane blade tradition scales using your **Wisdom** modifier instead of Intelligence. This is canon for this bible (see Chassis-Deviation Note). All Bladesinger features that key off Intelligence now key off Wisdom.
- **Bladesong (2/Short Rest):** As a bonus action, you can start a sacred elven blade dance that lasts for 1 minute (Concentration). While active:
  - You add your **Wisdom modifier (+4)** to your Armor Class (total AC boost of +4 while dancing; AC goes from 16 to 20).
  - Your walking speed increases by 10 feet.
  - You have advantage on Dexterity (Acrobatics) checks.
  - You gain a bonus to any Constitution saving throw to maintain your concentration on a spell equal to your **Wisdom modifier (+4)**.
- **Extra Attack (Wizard L6 via gestalt — Alexiel gets Extra Attack at L6 because gestalt means she takes the best of both classes at each level):** You can attack twice when taking the Attack action on your turn. Moreover, you can cast one of your cantrips (such as *Green-Flame Blade*, *Booming Blade*, or *Sacred Flame*) in place of one of those attacks.
- **Wizard Spellbook:** A stout, leather-bound volume containing Alexiel's wizard spells, written in neat metallurgical shorthand and interspersed with diagrams of sword-tang geometry. The book looks like an artisan's notebook, not a wizard's spellbook. Lina (her sister) borrowed it once and thought it was a craft-design journal.

**Suggested Wizard spells known (chosen to fit the cover):**
- **Cantrips (4):** *Mage Hand, Minor Illusion, Prestiditation, Shocking Grasp.*
- **L1 (4 known):** *Shield, Magic Missile, Sleep, Comprehend Languages.*
- **L2 (3 known):** *Misty Step, Mirror Image, Mind Spike.* (Mirror Image is doubly clever — it is both an arcane spell *and* a thematic echo of Sariel's Mirror Magic, which Alexiel does not consciously notice.)
- **L3 (3 known):** *Counterspell, Dispel Magic, Sending.*

> *For the AI DM:* The Bladesong is *the other half of the cover*. When Alexiel dances, she looks like a sixteen-year-old prodigy fencer who happens to have elven-trained footwork — not like a 1000-year-old Magister casting arcane spells. The +4 AC while dancing is loud (visible motion, audible footwork-pattern) but it is *fencer-loud*, not *wizard-loud*. Cael has started suspecting the dancing is *too* good. He has not yet figured out it is elven.

### §3.4 — Permanent L9 Foresight (Homebrew, psionic, the past-life inheritance)

> *This is the divine residue of the Magister's mind bleeding through a sixteen-year-old body. It is not a spell. It is not a class feature. It is part of who Alexiel is now.*

- **Mechanical effect:** Alexiel has advantage on all Initiative rolls and on all saving throws against effects she has not previously witnessed in this lifetime. Against any effect she *has* witnessed (in either lifetime — i.e., any monster, spell, trap, or tactic from her Year 9,378 — 9,408 career), she has advantage on the saving throw *and* adds +2 to the result.
- **Activation:** Passive. Always-on. Cannot be suppressed. The L9 designation refers to the *equivalent spell level* (the Foresight spell is 9th level), not a class feature level — Alexiel does not "have L9 spell slots" of Foresight. She has the *passive benefit* of permanent low-grade Foresight as a 1000-year-old Magister of the Watch.
- **Lore cost:** The first time in any scene that Alexiel's Foresight triggers — i.e., the moment her eyes unfocus for half a heartbeat because she has *seen this exact ambush before, three centuries ago, in another lifetime* — every NPC in the scene makes a WIS save (DC 14) or feels a chill and the *uncomfortable familiarity.* This is the Aura (below) leaking.
- **Why it is not a spell:** Because Sariel's daughter was a sorceress, and Sariel's granddaughter is a sorceress, and the imperial line has carried psionic sensitivity for ten generations. The Hollow Queen's psionic signature, resurrected in a sixteen-year-old body, manifests as *foresight* rather than as raw Nullification. She has not yet learned to project the Field; the Field has learned to project *her.* The forge-glow and the blade-song are *outward* manifestations of the same divine-resonance that the Foresight is an *inward* manifestation of. They are three faces of one body.

### §3.5 — Aura — *The Hollow Queen's Residual Field*

At all times, Alexiel passively emits a **residual psionic aura** that does not manifest as magic. Mechanically:

- Allies within 30 ft reroll natural 1s on saves (once each).
- Hostile spellcasters within 30 ft have a −1 penalty to spell save DCs.
- NPCs with the **Psychic Sensitivity** feat or trait can feel Alexiel's presence as an "uncomfortable familiarity" — a sense of *I know you but I don't know how.*
- **Forge-halo loud variant:** When Alexiel's Blessing of the Forge is *active* on an item she is touching, the aura expands to a visible golden halo around her off-hand. Any Imperial Courtier or registered forge-priest within line-of-sight will recognize this as **forge-magic**. Paranoia +2 if seen by anyone who might report it. (Paranoia +5 if seen by a Courtier in disguise.)

This is the fingerprint of the Nullification Field, leaking through the sixteen-year-old body in two modes — the *quiet* mode (passive aura, indistinguishable from a hedge-mage's ambient residue) and the *loud* mode (forge-halo, visibly divine). It cannot be turned off. It scales up sharply during combat or strong emotion (Paranoia +2 for any NPC who witnesses the loud mode).

### §3.6 — Gestalt Chassis — How the Two Classes Interlock

The gestalt chassis means Alexiel gets the best of all features at each level. Mechanically:

- **Attack action:** Cleric's cantrip-as-attack (Green-Flame Blade / Booming Blade) + Extra Attack (gestalt) — two attacks, one of which can be a cantrip.
- **Bonus action:** Choose between **Bladesong** (start the dance; lasts 1 min Concentration) or **Blessing of the Forge** activation (touch one metal item; +1 until next long rest).
- **Reaction:** Choose between **Shield** (Wizard, +5 AC for one round, no slot cost) or **Arcanus Reflex** (homebrew: +2 AC against one hostile attack within 5 ft, 1/short rest).
- **The "Gestalt Decision" each turn:** The player picks which class's action economy to prioritize. Each short rest in which she uses one Cleric action (Blessing of the Forge) + one Wizard cantrip grants +1 to all attack rolls for the next minute.

## §5 — STARTING SCENE: THE MORNING FORGE

> *This is the canonical opening scene. Read aloud to the player. Wait for response.*

It is the first day of spring in Year 10,420 A.G. The Frostspine peaks are still white. The Hollow Stone lights up at dawn and the village gathers to watch it, the way they always do, with the small offerings of bread and salt that the local priest blesses and the small children scatter along the stone's edge.

You have been watching the Hollow Stone for eleven months now. You have not yet figured out whether it is one of your old leylines or one of Sariel's, but you have felt, every equinox, the faint pulse of something that is not quite psionic and not quite divine, and you have decided, again and again, not to investigate.

You are sixteen years old and you are at breakfast in the Arcanus household. The cook, Master Hettie, has made porridge with honey and dried apple, and your youngest sister Maeve is trying to steal the apple off your bowl. Your mother the Baroness is going over the household accounts. Your father the Baron is reading a letter from the magistrate about bandit activity on the eastern road. Edric is reading a book of northern poetry. Lina is sketching the cat. Cael is cleaning his sword.

Cael looks at you. He has been looking at you all week.

"You," he says.

You look up. "Me."

"You've been dodging my drills."

"No," you say, "I haven't."

"You've been *winning* my drills. That's worse." He stands. He is eighteen and he is a head taller than you and he is the heir to the barony and he has been training with a sword since he could walk. He has a beautiful, clean, slightly archaic grip on the longsword — the Arcanus grip your husband Artorius invented eight centuries ago. He does not know he is using your husband's grip. He does not know he is using your husband's *son's* grip, which is to say, Cassian's grip, which you taught him when he was eight and refused to unlearn it for the rest of his life. (You remember teaching him. You remember the look on his face when his hand finally settled into the grip for the first time. You have not yet figured out how to feel about teaching your great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-great-grand-uncle your husband's sword form. You have decided, again, not to think about it.)

"Duel me," he says. "Properly. Not the schoolyard drill. A real duel. No smith's tricks — sword and shield only."

Your father looks up from his letter. "Cael."

"Father." Cael's voice is level. "I need to know."

"You need to know *what*."

"I need to know whether I am losing my edge, or whether my baby sister is something I don't understand."

The room is quiet.

You have three options. You have a fourth — a slot for anything else.

> **A. The Honest Path — *No Magic, Just the Sword (and the Footwork).*** *You stand. You pick up the practice sword from the corner of the room. You nod once. You do not light the forge-halo. You do not start the Bladesong. You give him your hand and your eye and your full thousand years of muscle memory — but you give it to him through a sixteen-year-old body's reflexes and a Magister's footwork. You let the body do what the body remembers.* **He will lose.** He will lose 8 — 2. He will not be embarrassed; he will be confused. He will ask you where you learned to fight. You will not have a good answer.

> **B. The Concealment Path — *Let Him Win.*** *You stand. You pick up the practice sword. You make a show of it — a few sloppy parries, a controlled fall, a clean disarm that leaves the outcome ambiguous. You give him the win. He will not be convinced. He will know you let him have it. He will lose respect for you.* **Paranoia −1 (he is satisfied). Edric −2 trust (he suspected; now he knows).**

> **C. The Demonstration Path — *Use the Bladesong.*** *You stand. You do not pick up the practice sword. You look at Cael. You start the dance. The air around you shimmers faintly as your Wisdom keys the footwork-pattern. You do not say anything. You do not cast any spell. You just dance, and let him see the elven-blade tradition move through a body that should not know it.* **He will not attack.** He will back away. He will not be able to explain why. Paranoia +1 for anyone in the room who is psionically sensitive. Edric will not forget this. The garrison chaplain, who has a standing invitation to the Baron's table on Marketdays, may or may not be in the room. He has a way of being in the room when interesting things happen.

- **The Empress's Courtiers are fragmenting.** If Alexiel did nothing for the next 5 years, the Courtiers would begin to rebel against Vaelara XI. The Imperium would fracture into civil war regardless of Alexiel's choices. (This is the late-game background pressure.)

## §7 — WORLD LORE & MECHANICAL SYSTEMS

> *The Magic System, the Psychic System, the Paranoia Stat, and the Heartbeats.*

### §7.1 — Magic System (Caps Locked per User Rule — *GRRM-5e ceiling*)

> *"No Wish. No True Resurrection. No Meteor Swarm. Resurrections, when they occur, require dragon-egg pyre mechanics — and the eggs are extinct."*

- **Hard caps:**
  - Highest spell level available at L20: **6th level.**
  - Highest spell level available at L25 (Divine Ascension Track): **7th level.** *Wish* exists at L25 but the cost is the caster's life.
  - No *True Resurrection*. *Resurrection* is available only via the dragon-egg pyre (extinct). *Revivify* exists but requires a diamond worth 300gp consumed.
  - No *Meteor Swarm*. *Fireball* caps at 8d6.
  - *Counterspell* auto-fails against the Nullification Field (Alexiel's specialty, dormant in this body).
- **Conjuration limits:** Summoned creatures cannot exceed CR 5 without a binding contract with an actual entity (per canon, all such bindings are with Holy Celestine-Order writs).
- **Magic Item rarity ceiling:** Very Rare at L20. Legendary only via dragon-line bond or Arcanus heirloom — and only 3 such items are confirmed in the world (the Mortal Queen's Crown, an Heirloom of Sariel, a Mirror of Lumiel).
- **Modern magic is weaker than the peak.** A trained L6 forge-caster / bladesinger in Year 10,420 is approximately as powerful as a trained L4 caster was in Year 9,408 (Alexiel's lifetime). This is the natural decay of the Hollow Peace: magic is rarer, training is shorter, fewer masters.
- **Unified Wisdom casting:** Because this chassis uses Wisdom as the single spellcasting attribute for both Cleric divine and Wizard arcane, Alexiel's spell list is intentionally a *hybrid* — her known spells are chosen so she never has to *visibly* switch between "cleric mode" and "wizard mode." She casts *Shield* the same way she casts *Blessing of the Forge* — through the same Wisdom-channel, with the same body-language. To an observer she is *one* caster, not two. (This is the entire point of the chassis: cover.)

### §7.2 — Psychic System — *The Mirror Magic Cascade*

- **Origin:** Sariel's Mirror Magic was originally an attempt to replicate the Arcanus Nullification Field in a *broadcast* form. It succeeded — but it amplified, becoming a tool of psychic domination.
- **11 Generation Attenuation:** Each generation of descent from Sariel has produced a weaker Mirror Magic user. Empress Vaelara XI can barely mirror a single mind at a time. The 800 Courtiers she commands are *humans* she has spent decades training to *receive* her Mirror Magic — they function as her range-extension.
- **Wild Psychic Sensitivity:** ~5% of the population has low-grade psychic sensitivity (flares of emotion, unbidden memory flashes, sense of presence). This is *not* regulated; only formal psionic abilities are.
- **Nullification Field vs Mirror Magic:** Alexiel's Hidden Apex (Nullification Field) is the *only* canonical counter to Mirror Magic. The Empress's Courtiers cannot read her mind if her Aura is active. They can detect *that* her mind is unreadable. This is the central mechanic of Act III.
- **Forge-halo vs Mirror Magic:** The forge-halo (loud-mode Aura when Blessing of the Forge is active) does *not* counter Mirror Magic. It is a separate divine-craft signature. A Courtier who sees the forge-halo will recognize "unregistered forge-magic" — not "the Mother Who Burned has returned." This is *the entire reason* the chassis was chosen for this bible: the loud-mode Aura is a *misleading* signature that buys Alexiel time.

### §7.3 — Quad-Pillar Stats (Alexiel's Internal Dashboard)

> *Per the HotD Ashen Crown convention — these four stats form the "campaign health meter." They are **not** character stats; they are *campaign* stats. They track Alexiel's stability and her grip on her own existence.*

| Pillar | Range | What it tracks | Trigger |
|---|---|---|---|
| **WIS (already in character sheet)** | (3-18) | — | — |
| **Paranoia** (replaces "Mortal Anchor") | 0-20 | The weight of being Alexiel Arcanus. Higher = she is more visible, less capable of concealment, more likely to be recognized by NPC psychics, more likely to make mistakes. | +1 per long rest baseline; +1 per Imperial agent within 100ft; +2 per NPC who **suspects** but **does not know**; +3 per NPC who **knows** and is talking about it; +5 if the Baron dies; resets to 0 only at the conclusion of Act IV (but only via the Freeform slot). |
| **Hidden Apex (Nullification Field)** | 0-5 (5 = dormant in body) | How deeply the Field is anchored in the resurrected body. At 0, the Field awakens fully — Alexiel is no longer hiding, and the game changes. | +1 at major canon-canon moments (Act III climax, Act IV opening, Act IV climax). ### §7.4 — XP Rule (User Locked — Standard Milestone)

> *Standard 5e cumulative XP thresholds by level (L2 = 300, L3 = 900, L4 = 2,700, ..., L20 = 355,000). Award XP at the end of each session for: significant canon-canon moments, completed Act beats, and Continuity Hook triggers. No XP during god mode (narrator-driven cutscenes, time-skips, scripted sequences).*

- **Rationale:** Acts span 3-5 levels. The whole campaign (Acts I-IV) spans L6 → L20 over ~14 Acts. Optional L21+ unlock for the Divine Ascension Track.

### §7.5 — Hidden Apex — *The Nullification Field Awakens*

- **Mechanic:** The Field is the *only* canonical counter to Mirror Magic. When awakened above 0, Alexiel becomes:
  - Immune to all psionic detection within 30 ft.
  - Capable of dispelling *one* magical effect per short rest as a Reaction (no save; the effect simply ends).
  - Visible to anyone who has studied Arcanus history (the Mortal Queen's signature aura is recognizable to ~30 living people in the world).
- **Discovery:** When awakened, the field does *not* feel like power. It feels like *recognition*. The Hollow Queen's body remembers being a queen.
- **Decision:** Each Act's climactic moment presents the player with a choice: awaken the Field at increasing cost, or delay awakening and risk larger consequences.

## §8 — GAZETTEER, GEOGRAPHY & NOTABLE LOCATIONS

> *The map is small. The world is large. The campaign is local.*

### §8.1 — Hollowmere (The Starting Location)

- **Population:** ~900 villagers (340 adults, 110 children, 380 elderly, 70 transient traders/refugees).
- **Layout:** One central square (cobblestones, a stone well); 60 homes on six radiating lanes; one temple to the Mother Who Burned (Alexiel's cult, three centuries old); one chapel to the First Empress (Sariel's cult, two centuries old); one tavern ("The Last Bell"); one herbalist's shop (Mira's); one blacksmith (Varn's, now buying blanks from Alexiel); one training yard (the Arcanus Fencing School); **one smithy-and-enchanter's workshop behind the fencing school (Alexiel's, eleven months old).**
- **Climate:** Cold summers (snow still on the Frostspine peaks in July), bitter winters (10-ft drifts common), short growing season.
- **Surroundings:** Old Wood 8 leagues to the East; Frostmere Garrison (small; 30 soldiers + 1 Inquisitor in disguise) 2 days' ride to the West; the Frostspine Mountains 1 league to the North (impassable in winter).
- **Notable features:** **The Hollow Stone** — a rock outcropping 1 mile from the village center where, every spring equinox, a faint blue light is seen at dawn for about 7 minutes. The villagers believe it is the Mother's blessing. **This is a surviving fragment of the Arcanus leyline.** It is where Alexiel woke up. The Hollow Stone is *also* what gave the town its name.

### §8.2 — The Arcanus Reaches (Capital Heartland)

- **Distance:** ~3,000 leagues East. Not reachable on foot within the campaign's normal Act span.
- **Cultural note:** All central roads lead to Zenith Spire. All roads are watched. All travelers are reported.

### §8.3 — Zenith Spire (The Hollow Capital)

- **Status:** Standing. The Silent Throne is intact, in the north wing. The Empress lives there with 800 Courtiers and ~2,000 servants. The Spire itself has been rebuilt three times.
- **Notable NPCs at court:** **Lord Chancellor Vesh, Master of Mirrors** — Vaelara XI's chief Inquisitor; aware of *some* unregistered flares in the periphery; currently focused on a peasant revolt in the South (which gives Alexiel time). **Archivist-General Iri** — Keeper of the Arcanus Archives; would recognize the Mortal Queen's name if Alexiel ever spoke it. **High Commander Titus val Raziel** — title passed down eleven generations; the current holder is an old soldier, great-great-grandson of your friend, who has been kept alive on life-support magics for forty years because the Imperium does not know how to retire him.

### §8.4 — Old Wood (The Forest Next Door)

- **Distance:** 8 leagues from Hollowmere.
- **Inhabitants:** Dragon-houses (the Saliar line of Aurum's descendants), bandit kingdoms (Bran the Red's domain), refugee settlements, hermits.
- **Notable figures:**
  - **Saliar the Younger** (gold dragon) — Aurum's great-grandson; 200 years old; interested in Alexiel because *the Arcanus line owes the gold dragon-blood a debt from the Sacrifice Fields*. He has been circling the village.
  - **Bran the Red** (bandit-king) — human; ~40 years old; lost his brother to Imperial conscription; willing to fight the Empress if given a reason.
  - **The Silver Nun** (hermit) — *the false-claimant* from Section 5; lives in a cave at the Old Wood's heart; performs "miracles" that look like Nullification Field effects but are actually just astrological timing + herbalism + suggestion.

### §8.5 — The Sundering Wastes (Where Alexiel Died)

- **Distance:** ~1,500 leagues East.
- **Status:** Demon-haunted. The ground itself remembers her death. Returning here would trigger **Echo of the Battle** — a narrative mode where past events replay around her.

## §9 — CAMPAIGN CONTINUITY HOOKS (NO CANONICAL ENDINGS)

> *The Hollow Queen's quiet war never ends. It widens. Pick up any thread between Acts; the world keeps moving.*

### Hook 1 — *Cael's Path*

Cael is starting to suspect. He is going to do one of three things: ask again, work harder, or quietly send a letter to a friend in the Imperial Legions asking whether his baby sister is "something he should know about." If Alexiel plays this well, he can be made into an ally. If she plays this badly, he becomes the family's wedge. **The added wrinkle in this chassis:** Cael has also noticed that Alexiel is *forging* — not just fencing. He does not understand why his baby sister, who two years ago could barely hold a hammer, is now making blades Varn cannot replicate. He has not connected the two skills (fencing + smithing) yet. When he does, his suspicion will double.

### Hook 2 — *The Letter from the Magister*

The sealed letter in Alexiel's satchel. She has not opened it. When she does, the campaign enters a different register. The letter is from Alexiel's past self, written the night before the Sacrifice Fields, addressed to "my daughter, Sariel." It contains three paragraphs: a confession that she knew Mordan's trap would kill her, an apology for not telling Sariel what she was, and a single sentence at the end — *If I come back, I will not be a queen. I will be a mother. Find me.* Sariel never received this letter. It survived in a belt-pouch on Alexiel's body when she died. The pouch was not recovered. Someone — not Sariel, not the imperial archive — preserved it. The letter will speak to Alexiel's Foresight the moment she opens it. **Paranoia +5 if she opens it in front of anyone.**

### Hook 3 — *The Inquisitor's Question*

Brother Aldous (false name; **Inquisitor-Legate Halric**) has been in Hollowmere for three months. He is patient. He is thorough. He has noticed two things: the Aura, and that Varn's blanks have been getting suspiciously good. He will, eventually, ask the Baron whether he has noticed anything unusual about his fifth daughter. The Baron will say no. Halric will not believe him. Halric will request a private audience with Alexiel. **When he does, the Aura will respond — and if she has her forge-halo lit, he will recognize it as unregistered forge-magic, not as the Mother Who Burned. He will file a report. The report will reach the Silent Throne. The Empress will send a *registered* forge-priest to "audit" the village.** **Paranoia +3 if the meeting occurs. Paranoia +5 if the Nullification Field flares during the meeting.**

### Hook 4 — *The Mortal Queen's Crown*

Across the campaign, the player will collect or hear about seven pieces of the Mortal Queen's Crown. When the seventh piece is found, the Crown is reassembled — and Alexiel's Hidden Apex stat forces to 0, the Field awakens, and the **L25 Divine Ascension Track** opens as the optional capstone campaign content path (user-locked Rhaenyra override). The seven pieces are:
  1. *The blade's hidden notch in the pommel of Cael's practice sword* (already owned).
  2. The Hollow Stone's buried third piece (in Section 8's "notable features").
  3. A mirror shard in the Silver Nun's cave.
  4. An amulet on a Frostmere garrison deserter.
  5. A tooth on Saliar the Younger's hoard.
  6. The Emperor's own crown-setting (in Zenith Spire, behind the Courtier Registry).
  7. *The exact location of the seventh piece is a freeform choice the player makes.*

### Hook 5 — *Maeve's Question*

Maeve (age 9) is the family's baby. She has not noticed Alexiel is unusual. If Alexiel uses the forge-halo or the Bladesong in front of Maeve, Maeve will ask about it, casually, the way children ask about everything, and will not think twice about it. **Maeve is the canary in the mine.** If Maeve asks a strange question, the household knows.

### Hook 6 — *The Wolves Out of Season*

Saliar the Younger is *not* the only dragon who has noticed. A shadow-dragon — descendant of **Umbrax** from the Dragon Knight canon — has been circling the Frostspine for a season. Her name is **Vessara**. She has not landed. She is *waiting for something.* (Hoard of Hook 4 piece 5 is in her territory too.)

### Hook 7 — *The Empress's Sleep*

Vaelara XI has been having dreams she does not understand. A woman with silver hair. A smith's apron. A child with a hammer in the air. The dreams are not psychic — they are leaking *up* the bloodline from Lumiel's embedded memory of Alexiel. The Empress does not know what they mean. **She will send a second Inquisitor to investigate, then a third, then a fourth. Eventually she will send the Courtiers themselves.** This is the late-game Loom escalation: the village becomes visible regardless of the player's choices.

### Hook 8 — *The Mirror Shatters*

In the campaign's *interstitial* — between Acts II and III, during a long rest, after a moment of perfect quiet — a single line of dialogue appears in a scene where it should not:

> *"Mother?"*

The voice is old. The voice is female. The voice has not been heard in three hundred and eighty-two years.

Sariel is dead. Sariel is also the First Empress, who is worshipped as a god. The line is a god-voice, bleeding through the Hollow Stone, reaching for the only person in ten generations who would recognize it.

**Paranoia +5 if Alexiel responds. Paranoia +10 if anyone else hears it.**

### Hook 9 — *The Forge-Halo Test*

A merchant from the Free Cities arrives in Hollowmere with a wagon of *unregistered* enchanted blades — clearly forged with divine-magic, clearly not blessed by any registered forge-priest, clearly illegal under the Stability Mandate. The merchant is looking for a local enchanter to "improve" the blades. He has heard there is a young woman in Hollowmere who can put divine fire into steel. **If Alexiel helps him, she will be visible to every Courtier who inspects the merchant's cargo at the next garrison checkpoint. If she refuses, the merchant moves on to the next village — and the next enchanter he finds might be less careful than she is.**

### Hook 10 — *The Quiet War Widens*

If Alexiel does nothing for the next five years, the Hollow Peace breaks. The Courtiers rebel against Vaelara XI. The Imperium fractures into civil war. The Empress sends her armies into the Arcanus Reaches to "restore order." Hollowmere is on the road between the Old Wood and the garrison. Hollowmere is *on* the road. The Baron's fencing school will be conscripted. The Baron's fifth daughter will be conscripted.

She will not be able to hide behind an anvil forever.

## §10 — SETUP NOTES FOR THE AI DM

### Reading Order

1. **Read §0 (frontmatter) first.** Canon-priority rules, open-ended system notice, canon-correction note, chassis-deviation note. These four rules are non-negotiable.
2. **Read §2 (canon) second.** Without canon inlined, you will fabricate. The canon-correction in §0 exists because you will fabricate Sariel's parentage.
3. **Read §3 (mechanics) third.** Wisdom-fusion is a chassis deviation. Do not silently swap to Intelligence for Wizard spells.
4. **Read §5 (starting scene) fourth.** This is the player's first beat. Read aloud.
5. **§1, §4, §6, §7, §8 are reference.** Re-read as the player triggers hooks.
6. **§9 is the live thread list.** Use the Quad-Pillar Stats (§7.3) to adjudicate player choices.

### Voice Rules

- **Alexiel is INTJ, exhausted, 1000 years old in a 16-year-old body.** She does not sound like a teenager. She sounds like a tired grandmother who happens to be sixteen. Her dialogue should be sparse, precise, and quietly funny. She does not whine. She does not monologue. She does not angst in front of other people.
- **The household is rural, cold, and honest.** Baron Aldric speaks like a retired soldier. Lady Sera speaks like a merchant's daughter who married up. Cael speaks like an eighteen-year-old who thinks he is the smartest person in the room. Edric speaks like a seventeen-year-old who actually is the smartest person in the room. Lina speaks like a fifteen-year-old artist. Maeve speaks like a nine-year-old tomboy who wants to be a swordsmith.
- **The Inquisitor is polite, patient, and absolutely not your enemy.** Brother Aldous files reports. He does not interrogate. He does not torture. He is a functionary. He will report what he finds. Treat him with the same respect the player would treat a tax collector.
- **The dragons do not speak unless spoken to.** Saliar circles. Vessara waits. They are not here for the player — they are here because the Arcanus bloodline has reactivated and they want to see what happens.
- **The Empress is sixty, attenuated, and dreaming.** Vaelara XI has been on the throne for thirty years. She is tired in a way that mirrors Alexiel's tiredness. She does not know who Alexiel is. She is not the antagonist. The *system* she runs is the antagonist.

### The Four Questions the Player Will Eventually Ask

1. *"Who am I, really?"* — You are Alexiel Arcanus, the Mother Who Burned, the Magister of the Watch, the Hollow Queen. You are sixteen. You are tired. You are hiding.
2. *"Where is Sariel?"* — Sariel died of old age in Year ~10,038, at age ~647, by Mirror Magic. Her body rests in the Deep Archives beneath Zenith Spire. Her god-voice bleeds through the Hollow Stone. She is a god now. Gods do not rest quietly.
3. *"What is the Empress going to do?"* — She is going to keep enforcing the Stability Mandate until it breaks. She is going to send Inquisitors. She is going to send forge-priests. Eventually she is going to send armies. The Hollow Peace is a slow war, not a peace.
4. *"Can I just stay at the anvil?"* — No. The Hollow War is widening. The Inquisitor has noticed you. The dragons are circling. The Empress is dreaming. You can delay. You cannot hide.

### Final Note

This bible ships as a *system*. There is no canonical ending. There is no Final Verdict. There is no Ending Determinator matrix. The player's choices are the campaign. The player will eventually choose whether to awaken the Nullification Field, whether to register with the Artisan-Priest Registry, whether to open the Magister's letter, whether to answer Sariel's god-voice, whether to confront the Inquisitor, whether to rally the dragons, whether to march on Zenith Spire.

*Any* of those choices is a valid campaign. *None* of them is the campaign. The campaign is what the player does next.
