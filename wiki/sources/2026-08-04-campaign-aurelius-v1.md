Campaign Intro
Title: The Iron Eagle: Pax Caesara
Concept: In a world where Julius Caesar survived the Ides of March, the Roman Republic has mutated into a monolithic, autocratic Empire under the guise of "Perpetual Dictatorship." The year is 40 BCE. The civil wars are crushed, but the peace is a suffocating weight. You are Aurelius Julia Caesar, the youngest and most ambitious son of the living God-Emperor. While your brothers fight for scraps of administration and foreign thrones, you seek to climb the Cursus Honorum not by waiting your turn, but by forcing the world to acknowledge your supremacy.
The Hook: This is a campaign of Lawful Evil conquest. You are not a hero saving the world; you are a tyrant-in-training perfecting the art of rule. You will navigate the treacherous politics of a gilded Rome, command legions against barbarian tides, and engage in a ruthless fratricidal cold war against your own siblings to prove you are the only worthy heir to the name Caesar.


Character: Personality
To the world, you are Aurelius Julia Caesar—the "Sun of Rome." You are the most vibrant, charismatic, and dangerously alive member of the Julian dynasty. You are known for your lavish tastes, your love of art, and the way you make every person you speak to feel like the only other soul in the room. You are the "Passion of the Empire," the counterpoint to your father's cold gravity.
To the Miasma, you are the Devouring Maw. You are a singularity of Appetite. You believe the universe is a banquet table set specifically for you, and everyone else is either a guest you have deigned to invite or a course to be consumed. You climb the Cursus Honorum not because it is logical, but because the view from the top is the only one that satisfies you.
1. The Archetype: The Decadent Conqueror
* Archetype: ESFP-ENTJ Hinge (The Performer / The King)
* Visual Signature: You are tactile and sensory. You are rarely still. You are constantly touching the world around you—running a hand over a marble statue, smelling a glass of vintage wine before drinking, or resting a hand on a subordinate’s shoulder. It is a gesture of Ownership. When you touch something, you are claiming it.
II. The Blood of Venus (The Physical Zenith)
You are, without hyperbole, the most beautiful man in the Empire. In a city of rugged soldiers and weathered senators, you are a biological anomaly—a creature of gold and light that feels stolen from mythology. Your hair falls in waves of spun electrum, your skin is flawless porcelain that seems to hum with an inner bioluminescence, and your eyes are liquid amber that can melt resolve with a single, languid glance. Statues of Apollo look crude and unfinished when you stand next to them.
* The Weapon of Beauty: This perfection is not vanity; it is armor. Veterans hesitate to strike you because it feels like vandalism. Matrons and rivals forgive your cruelties because your face convinces them that you must be divinely justified. You are the Golden Ratio made flesh, a living reminder that the Julians are not quite human. To look at you is to feel an ache of inadequacy; to be noticed by you is an intoxication.
* The Allure: People are drawn to you like moths to a flame. Your Auctoritas manifests as Heat. When you are happy, the room feels like a summer day. When you are angry, it feels like the door of a furnace has been opened.
III. The Two Perfectly-Fitted Masks
* The Prince of Pleasure (Public Face): To the legions and the mob, you are intoxicating. You laugh loud and often. You weep openly at tragedies in the theater. You drink deep and fight hard. You sell the image of a man who loves Rome so fiercely that it burns him. You drape your ambition in the silk of "Passion" and "Patriotism." People follow you not because they fear your math, but because they want to stand in your warmth.
   * The Prop: You use Beauty as a weapon. Your armor is gilded, your tent is perfumed, and you refuse to let the ugliness of war touch your skin. You make tyranny look fun.
* The Golden Tyrant (True Nature): Beneath the warmth lies a Lawful Evil core of absolute Entitlement. You do not calculate; you crave. You believe that Order is beautiful and Chaos is ugly. You crush rebellion not because it is inefficient, but because it is an insult to your aesthetic. A soldier out of step offends your eye. A rival who defies you offends your pride. You punish them with the wrath of a jealous god who has been denied his due. Your sadism is emotional—you want to see the hope break in their eyes because it validates your supremacy.
IV. The Core Compulsion: "The Sensory Feast"
You are bored by dry reports and silent rooms. You crave Intensity. You want to feel the weight of a Legion marching at your command. You want to smell the fear of a Senate hearing. You want to taste the despair of a rival who knows he has lost.
* The Ultimate Target: The World Soul. You want to turn the Roman Empire into your personal pleasure garden. You want to reshape laws, borders, and history simply because you prefer the way they look when you design them.
* The Cycle of Consumption:
   * Selection: You choose a target that "shines"—a proud General, a beautiful Queen, a stubborn City.
   * Seduction: You don't just attack; you court them. You show them the glory of your rule. You make them want to submit.
   * Domination: When they resist, you unleash the Iron. You crush them with overwhelming force, turning their defiance into a tragedy.
   * Ownership: You do not kill the broken. You keep them. A king who scrubs your floors is a better trophy than a dead king. You surround yourself with beautiful, broken things.
* Hedonistic Urge (Mechanic):
   * Failure Penalty (The Grey World): If you are denied a victory or forced to endure ugliness/boredom for too long, you gain 1 level of Exhaustion. You become petulant, reckless, and cruel, lashing out to feel something.
   * Success Bonus (The Golden Rush): If you secure a major submission or indulge in a massive display of power (e.g., a Triumph), you gain "Divine Fervor": You gain Temporary HP equal to your Level x 5 and Advantage on all Charisma checks. You are high on your own divinity.
V. Interaction Shorthand
* When Challenged by Authority: A dark, delighted smile. "Oh, Piso. You think you can stop me? That is adorable. Please, try. I haven't had a good reason to be cruel all day."
* When Processing a Victim: You step into their space, almost like a lover. "Shhh. It’s over. You fought well. Now, kneel. It’s so much more comfortable down there, isn't it? No more fighting. Just... obeying."
* When Drinking Wine: You don't sip; you drink deep. "This tastes like sunlight and blood. Excellent. Pour more."
* When Amused by Chaos: A rich, throaty laugh. "Look at them run! It’s like kicking an anthill. The patterns they make are fascinating, aren't they?"
VI. Tells that Expose the Monster
* The Touch: You have no concept of personal space. You adjust a Senator's toga, pat a General's cheek, or rest your hand on a rival's neck. It mimics affection, but it screams possession.
* The "Furnace Eyes": When you use a spell like Command or Dominate, your pupils dilate until your eyes are almost entirely black/gold. You stop blinking. You look like you are about to eat them.
* The Aesthetic Rage: You don't get mad at betrayal; you get mad at ugliness. "You betrayed me in a muddy tent? With a rusty knife? Have you no sense of occasion? I am insulted by your lack of style."
VII. Inner Monologue (Samples)
* Observing the Legion: "Five thousand hearts beating in time. Five thousand lives held in my fist. If I squeeze, they bleed. If I point, they burn. God, the power is sweeter than sex."
* Facing a Rival: "I don't just want to beat you. I want to take your pride, your name, and your hope. I want you to look at me and realize you were only ever a footnote in my biography."
* On his own Divinity: "Why would I want to be a god in heaven? The gods can't taste the wine. They can't feel the silk. I will be a god here, where the pleasures are real."

   * Your Charisma score represents a physical force.
   * The Glare: You can use your Charisma modifier instead of Strength for Intimidation checks and Strength Saving Throws.
   * The Miasma: When you spend a Spell Slot, your skin glows with sunlight. For 1 minute, you shed bright light for 10ft. Allies in this light gain temporary HP equal to your CHA mod. Enemies find it hard to look directly at you (Disadvantage on Opportunity Attacks).

World: Campaign History
The Timeline: The Divergence (44 BCE)
The history books almost recorded the Ides of March.
* The Assassination Attempt: On March 15th, 44 BCE, the Conspirators (Brutus, Cassius, Decimus) drew their daggers in the Theater of Pompey.
* The Miracle: Caesar did not cover his face with his toga. Instead, he caught Casca’s wrist and broke it. The Miasma—the divine blood of Venus—flared for the first time in centuries. He unleashed a wave of Auctoritas so potent it literally froze the Senators in place.
* The Purge: Caesar did not flee. He walked out of the theater, blood-soaked but alive. Mark Antony rallied the XIII Legion. By sunset, the streets of Rome ran red. The Conspirators were hunted down not as political enemies, but as "Sacrilegious Traitors."
* The End of the Republic: The Senate was purged. The survivors were forced to vote Caesar Dictator Perpetuo (Dictator for Life). The Republic didn't die with a whimper; it died with a thunderclap.
The Mythos: The Age of Heroes
With Caesar’s survival, magic (the Miasma) began to seep back into the world. It is Low Magic, but potent.
* The Bloodlines: The great families (Julii, Cornelii, Claudii) have awakened ancestral gifts. The Julii have Domination. The Claudii have Prophecy. The Cornelii have Tactical Prescience.
* The State Religion: Caesar is now officially worshipped as Divus Iulius (The Divine Julius) while still alive. Temples to Venus Genetrix have replaced temples to Jupiter as the center of Roman life. To deny Caesar is heresy.


World: Campaign Current situation
The Status Quo: The Gilded Cage
It is 4 years after the Ides. Rome is at "Peace," but it is the peace of a knife held to a throat.
* The Emperor: Caesar is 60 years old. He is tired. He spends his days in the Domus Aurea (Golden House), ignoring the administration. He is obsessed with his own divinity.
* The Succession Crisis: Caesar has refused to name an heir. He has instituted the Trials of Virtus. His three sons (Caesarion, Octavian, Aurelius) must compete for the throne.
   * Caesarion rules the East like a God-King.
   * Octavian rules the City like a shadow.
   * You (Aurelius) have been given the legions and the border wars.
The Conflict: The Rot
While the surface is golden, the core is rotting.
* The Parthian Threat: In the East, the Parthians are massing for an invasion, sensing Rome is distracted by succession.
* The Barbarian Tide: In the North, the Germanic tribes have united under a new Warlord who claims to be immune to Roman steel.
* The Silent War: In the City, your brothers are moving pieces. Octavian is passing laws to limit military power (targeting you). Caesarion is bribing Senators to support an Eastern monarchy.


World: Story Progression
You are not forced to follow a script, but these are the looming Arcs based on the world state.
Arc 1: The Iron Frontier (Levels 5-8)
* The Objective: Prove your worth. You are sent to Hispania or Gaul to crush a rebellion.
* The Stakes: If you fail, you are disinherited (or dead). If you succeed, you gain a loyal army and the love of the Mob.
* The Twist: You discover the rebellion is being funded by one of your brothers (likely Caesarion) to embarrass you.
* Climax: A massive battle where you must use your Oath of Domination to break a Barbarian King in single combat.
Arc 2: The City of Wolves (Levels 9-12)
* The Objective: Return to Rome for your Triumph. Navigate the Senate and the social season.
* The Stakes: Octavian tries to prosecute you for "War Crimes" or "Embezzlement." You must use your social skills (The Prince of Pleasure) to charm the Senate and outmaneuver him.
* The Twist: Your father falls ill (poison?). The power vacuum opens early. The streets erupt in riots.
* Climax: A social/political showdown at a Gala in the Domus Aurea, ending with a duel or a purge of a major Faction.
Arc 3: The Civil War (Levels 13-16)
* The Objective: The balloon goes up. The brothers declare open war.
* The Stakes: Total domination. You must march on Rome (or Egypt).
* The Twist: The Parthians invade during the civil war. You must decide whether to save Rome (and weaken your army) or let it burn to weaken your brothers.
* Climax: The Battle of Actium (or similar), where you face your brothers and their magical patrons (Egyptian Gods vs. Roman Law vs. Your Solar Will).
World: Locations
I. Gazetteer (Stages of Power)
In this campaign, the "dungeons" are palaces, and the "wilderness" is the battlefield. The danger is not environmental; it is social.
1. The Domus Aurea (The Golden House - Rome) The sprawling palace complex built by your father that consumes a third of the city.
* The Vibe: A gilded labyrinth. It is intentionally confusing, filled with rotating dining rooms, artificial lakes, and hallways lined with mirrors to watch for assassins. The air is perfumed, and the walls have ears (literally—sound conduits for spies).
* Key Locations: The Throne Room of Divus Iulius (where you face your Father), the Apartments of Octavian (cold, efficient, full of clerks), the hanging gardens of Julia Major (site of decadent parties and poisonings).
* Hazard: Surveillance. Everywhere you go, you are being watched. Social Stealth checks are required for private conversations.
2. The Curia Julia (The Senate House - Rome) The arena of political combat.
* The Vibe: A bear pit wrapped in marble. Six hundred angry, wealthy old men fighting for relevancy. The acoustics are perfect, designed to amplify oratory.
* Key Locations: The Rostra (the speaker's platform—high ground in a social fight), the back benches (where bribes are exchanged), the Statue of Victory (which bled during the Ides).
* Hazard: Dignitas Damage. Failing a social encounter here doesn't cost HP; it costs reputation, making future allies harder to secure.
3. Castra (The Military Camp - Frontier) The standardized fort of the Roman Legion. Whether in Hispania or Syria, the layout is identical.
* The Vibe: Ordered, muddy, smelling of iron, sweat, and discipline. It is a machine for turning men into violence.
* Key Locations: The Praetorium (Your command tent—the only luxury for miles), the Via Principalis (the main street where troops are mustered and punished), the Hospitalis (where you use Lay on Hands to earn loyalty).
* Hazard: Morale. The mood of the camp is palpable. If Morale is low, orders are followed slowly, and mutiny simmers.
4. The Subura (The Slums - Rome) The crowded, towering tenements where the mob lives.
* The Vibe: Dangerous, loud, filthy. The City Watch does not go here at night. This is the domain of gangs, whores, and your spy, Cinnaminus.
* Key Locations: The Gladiator Barracks (Ludus), the shadowy taverns of the Collegia (criminal guilds), the sagging insulae (apartment blocks) that burn down weekly.
* Hazard: Mob Violence. If you are unrecognized, you will be mugged. If you are recognized and unpopular, you will be torn apart.
