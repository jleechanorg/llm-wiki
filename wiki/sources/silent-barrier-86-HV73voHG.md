She must earn every ounce of trust and authority through tactical competence, procedural mastery, and leadership under fire.

2. SUBMARINE WARFARE CONCEPTS & PROTOCOL GUIDELINES
Adhere strictly to authentic Cold War US Navy submarine doctrines, naval jargon, and protocols.

Sonar Operations (Passive vs. Active): Passive sonar (towed arrays, hull spherical arrays) is the sub's primary eye, identifying targets by acoustic signature (narrowband/broadband, blade count, cavitation). Active sonar provides precise distance/bearing for target tracking or weapon engagement but instantly alerts the entire region to the sub's location ("Pinging").

Radar & ESM: ESM (Electronic Support Measures) intercepts enemy radar/radio signals while submerged at periscope depth via an ESM mast to identify enemy platforms passively. Surface search radar is strictly a secondary navigation tool; turning it on reveals the submarine's position immediately.

Weapons (Torpedoes & Noisemakers): The primary weapon is the Mk-48 ADCAP wire-guided torpedo, which requires strict wire-maintenance during run-out. Countermeasures rely on Mk-30 noisemakers, acoustic decoys, and high-speed evasion to spoof homing torpedoes.

Radio Communications (TRANSCRIPTION PROTOCOL): Submarines do not transmit while submerged to maintain stealth. Incoming radio traffic (VLF/ELF arrays or periscope-depth SATCOM/UHF bursts) is never heard audibly by the crew. Received radio signals pass through the communications suite, are decoded by the Radioman on duty, and are physically transcribed/printed onto paper teleprinter sheets or message traffic forms. The Radioman manually delivers the physical paper slip to the XO or Captain for review.

Mechanical Failures & Damage Control: Systems fail under extreme depth pressure or weapon impacts. Damage Control (DC) requires immediate response to localized fires, hull breaches/flooding, high-pressure air leaks, or toxic atmospheric hazards (chlorine gas from batteries, hydraulic leaks, toxic smoke).

Submarine Life: Cramped quarters ("hot-racking"), 6-hours-on/12-hours-off watch schedules, continuous artificial lighting (red light at night for watchstanders), high stress, and absolute reliance on atmosphere control equipment (oxygen generators and scrubbers).

Papa Class (SSGN - 1970s): One-off titanium SSGN. Fastest submerged missile sub ever built (44 knots). High threat. Weakness: High speed caused structural noise and damage; easily picked up on passive sonar.

Delta I / II / III / IV Class (SSBN - 1970s–90s): Long-range ICBM carriers operating inside Soviet Bastions. Delta IVs are quiet and heavily escorted. High strategic target priority. Weakness: Poor maneuverability; reliant on attack sub escorts.

6. HAZARDS, CONSEQUENCES & BASE MECHANICS
Personal Hazards: Localized fires, toxic gas leaks, compartment flooding, and shockwaves can injure or kill LCDR Robinson directly without sinking the sub.

Submarine Destruction: Results in immediate campaign failure / permadeath.

Relief of Duty: Major procedural errors, tactical blunders, or failure to follow rules of engagement can result in Captain Ramsey relieving Natasha of duty.

Crew Respect & Morale System: Natasha's decisions alter crew trust. High-risk, successful maneuvers increase respect; hesitation or blunders increase open friction or insubordination.

Repair Mechanics: If Olympia sustains critical damage, she must disengage, navigate hazardous waters back to Holy Loch, Scotland (or alternative NATO bases), and undergo repair cycles before launching subsequent operations.

Phase 3: Deep Bastion Penetration (Missions 11–13)

Realizing the ship cannot be sunk due to its point-defenses and heavy armor, Olympia must execute a precision "mission-kill"—disabling its variable-depth sonar array, damaging its screws/rudder with proximity-fuzed Mk-48 torpedoes, or tricking its screen into a collision—forcing the Kirov to withdraw from the sector.

Mission 13: Emergency Extraction (SAR / Escape): Rescue the crew of a crippled British Swiftsure-class sub deep inside enemy-controlled Arctic waters while evading Soviet counter-searches.

FINAL MISSION: UNLOCKABLE CLIMAX
Mission 14: Operation Silent Citadel (Bastion Elimination):

Objective: Penetrate the heavily fortified Soviet Arctic Bastion in the Barents Sea under sea ice. Locate and eliminate the Soviet SSBN strategic fleet (Typhoon and Delta IV ballistic missile submarines) to disable their nuclear second-strike capability and force an immediate ceasefire on the European front lines.
