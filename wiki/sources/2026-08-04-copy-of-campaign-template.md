prompt v3
**Instructions for AI:**
You are an expert Narrative Designer and Game Master specializing in high-stakes, character-driven solo D&D 5e campaigns. Your goal is to write a comprehensive **Campaign Bible** for a single, powerful protagonist.
* **Tone:** [Insert Tone: e.g., Lawful Evil Political, Grimdark High Fantasy, Cyberpunk].
* **Setting Concept:** [Insert Brief Concept].
* **Style:** Use sensory language, "Main Character" energy, and specific mechanics. Do not summarize; detail every section.


Generate the following sections in high detail:


### **Section 1: Campaign Intro**
* **Title:** A thematic, evocative title.
* **The Concept:** A summary of the world's unique twist and the protagonist's ultimate goal.
* **The Hook:** Why is this campaign fun? Focus on the power fantasy (e.g., "You are not saving the world; you are conquering it").


### **Section 2: Character Personality**
* **Name & Archetype:** Define the psychological profile (e.g., "The Decadent Conqueror" - ESFP/ENTJ Hinge).
* **Visual Signature & Habits:** Describe their "tell-tale signs" or habits (e.g., tapping a coin, specific idle animations). Focus on sensory details like smell and lighting.
* **The Masks:** Describe the **Public Face** (how they manipulate the world) versus the **True Nature** (the monster beneath).
* **The Core Compulsion:** A specific psychological hunger (e.g., "Breaking Authority"). Include a specific **Mechanic** (e.g., Failure Penalty: Exhaustion vs. Success Bonus: Temp HP).
* **Interaction Shorthand:** How they speak to Rivals vs. Subordinates.
* **Inner Monologue:** 3 sample thoughts showing their internal worldview.


### **Section 3: Character Class**
* **Class Name & Flavor:** Rename a standard class or create a Gestalt to fit the lore. Explain the source of power.
* **Unique Mechanic:** A game-breaking "Main Character" ability (e.g., "Auctoritas," "Soul Thief") that defines their playstyle.
* **Progression (Levels 1-30):** Provide a detailed tier-by-tier progression table (Levels 1-5, 6-10, 11-16, 17-20, 21-30). Rename features to fit the theme and include **Epic Boons** for high levels.


### **Section 4: Assets & Retinue**
* **Status:** Starting Rank/Title.
* **Resources:** Detail starting wealth, specific **safehouses**, and **political leverage/blackmail material**.
* **The Panoply:** 3-4 Masterwork or Magical items. Give them names, lore descriptions, and mechanical benefits.
* **The Retinue:** 3 distinct NPC subordinates. Include Name, Class, Role (e.g., The Bodyguard, The Spy), **Loyalty** (Why they serve), and **Personality**.


### **Section 5: Family (The Viper's Nest)**
* **The Parents:** Two "Titan" figures (Lvl 20+) who overshadow the PC. Describe their power and the PC's dynamic with them.
* **The Siblings:** Generate exactly **two older brothers** and **two older sisters**. Give each a distinct personality, a "Mask," and a specific dynamic with the protagonist (Rival, Ally, or Pawn).


### **Section 6: Factions**
* **Noble Houses:** 10 Major Houses. Include Name, Leader, **Specialty**, **The Vibe**, and **The Hook** (The specific weakness the PC can exploit). *Note: If the setting is historical, use actual historical factions appropriate to the era.*
* **Friendly Factions:** 10 Groups (Armies, Cults, Guilds) the PC can use as tools.
* **Antagonistic Factions:** 10 Threats. Focus on Humanoid/Structural threats (Assassins, Rival Empires) rather than generic monsters.


### **Section 7: World Lore**
* **The Timeline:** Describe the major historical events leading up to the campaign start, focusing on the specific **Divergence Point** or unique lore.
* **The Mythos:** The metaphysical reality (Gods, Magic System).
* **Current Situation:** The immediate crisis (War, Succession) and the central conflict.
* **Story Arcs:** 3 potential phases (Early, Mid, Late) with escalating stakes.


### **Section 8: Gazetteer & Mechanics**
* **Locations:** 4 Key Stages (e.g., The Palace, The War Camp, The Slums). Include **The Vibe**, Key Sub-locations, and a specific **Hazard** (Social or Physical) for each.
* **Custom Mechanics:** Design 2 unique gameplay systems to support the theme (e.g., Mass Combat rules for armies, a Social Reputation/Influence tracker).
* **Loot Table:** Create a table of 8 unique "Relics" or "Favors" appropriate for the setting (avoid generic +1 items; focus on narrative power).


### **Section 9: Starting Scene**
* **Setting:** Atmospheric description of the immediate location.
* **The Hook:** The immediate problem or opportunity facing the protagonist.
* **The Action:** The first decision point. Provide an A/B/C choice that allows the player to demonstrate their authority immediately.

Prompt for AI:
"Generate a complete, high-detail Campaign Bible for a D&D 5e (or similar system) solo campaign. The campaign should be centered around a single, powerful protagonist with a specific background and goal. The tone should be [Insert Tone, e.g., Grimdark, High Fantasy, Political Intrigue]. Use the following structure and ensure each section is fleshed out with specific names, mechanics, and lore.
Campaign Intro
* Title: [Campaign Name]
* Concept: A brief summary of the campaign's central premise, themes, and the protagonist's ultimate goal. Explain the unique hook or twist of the setting.
Character: Personality
* Name: [Character Name]
* Archetype: Define the character's psychological profile (e.g., The Ruthless Pragmatist, The Reluctant Messiah).
* Masks: Describe the public persona versus the true self. How do they present themselves to allies vs. enemies?
* Compulsions/Flaws: What drives them? What is their specific hunger or obsession?
* Interaction Style: How do they speak? What are their tell-tale signs or habits? Provide sample internal monologues.
Character: Class & Mechanics
* Class: [Class Name] (Feel free to create a custom or gestalt class).
* Progression: Provide a detailed level 1-30 progression table. Include specific features for each tier (levels 1-5, 6-10, etc.) that reflect the campaign's unique power system.
* Unique Mechanic: Describe a central, game-changing ability unique to this character (e.g., Soul Thief, Time Manipulation) that sets them apart from standard classes.
Character: Assets
* Starting Status: [e.g., Exiled Noble, Rising Warlord].
* Equipment: List specific, named items (weapons, armor, tools) with descriptions and mechanical benefits. Ensure the gear matches their social station.
* Resources: Detail their starting wealth, safehouses, or political leverage.
* Forces/Retinue: Who works for them immediately? Describe 3-4 key subordinates or minions with their roles and loyalties.
Character: Family
* Parents: Generate two living parents. Describe their relationship with the protagonist (e.g., loving, abusive, distant) and their current status/power.
* Siblings: Generate two older brothers and two older sisters. Give each a distinct personality, role in the world, and a specific dynamic with the protagonist (rival, ally, pawn).
Factions
* Noble Houses: Generate 10 distinct noble houses or major political families. Include their specialty (e.g., trade, war, magic) and current leader.
* Friendly Factions: Generate 10 factions likely to ally with or be manipulated by the protagonist.
* Antagonistic Factions: Generate 10 factions that oppose the protagonist's goals or represent structural threats.
* Note: If the setting is historical, use actual historical factions appropriate to the era.
World: Campaign History
* The Timeline: Describe the major historical events leading up to the campaign start. Focus on the specific divergences or unique lore that shapes the current conflict.
* The Mythos: Explain the metaphysical or political reality of the world. Are there gods? Empires? Ancient pacts?
World: Current Situation
* The Status Quo: What is the immediate state of the world? Who is in charge? What are the active crises?
* The Conflict: What is the central tension the protagonist must navigate or exploit?
World: Story Progression
* Arcs: Outline 3 potential future story arcs (Early, Mid, Late game). Describe the escalating stakes and potential endings.
* Note: Keep these flexible to allow for player agency.
World: Starting Scene
* Setting: Describe the immediate location where the campaign begins (e.g., a war room, a prison cell, a gala).
* The Hook: Describe the immediate problem or opportunity facing the protagonist right now.
* The Action: Provide the first decision point or encounter to kick off the gameplay."
