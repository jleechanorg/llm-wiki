# Campaign Bible: The Black Company & The Sovereign's Hunger (v1)

**Source**: Campaign setup document "campaign aizen thay v1 - nocturne ancestor" — a v1 campaign bible that radically restructures the Aizen/Nocturne mythos into an **ancestor-patron sequel**. Where prior campaigns cast [[NocturneSosuke]] as a playable ascendant (see [[CrossPlatformAscensionPattern]]), this bible sets the clock **+100 years post-Ascension** into the "[[CenturyOfSilence]]" and makes Nocturne the sealed great-grandmother patron of a new protagonist: [[SosukeAizen|Aizen Sosuke]], "the Spare" of ruined [[house-sosuke|House Sosuke]], playing a custom **[[AbyssKnight]]** class rather than a traditional Warlock.

## Part I: The Codex of the Sovereign

### The pact — the Mirror and the Moth

The pact is a physical object, the **Obsidian Mirror** ([[ObsidianMirrorPact]]): a shard of black glass framed in thorned iron that reflects *her* face, not Aizen's — a two-way psychic window into the [[ShatteredRose]], Nocturne's private Abyssal sub-plane. The relationship is defined as **[[WeaponizedDesire]]**: she grants power out of boredom and hunger, treating Aizen as pet/toy/potential lover while he serves as her **Material Anchor** — the only tether to the world that rejected her. Crucially, the scrutiny runs both ways: Aizen studies her rule of "a kingdom of dead gods and enslaved demons" so he can one day replicate it — the mastermind-apprentice inversion of the usual patron dynamic, consistent with the [[PowerlessMastermindBuild]] pattern.

### The mythos — the rise of the Black Diamond

The world believes the Crisis of the Absolute was solved by heroes; the truth was "a hostile takeover." Nocturne's canonical history in this timeline:

- **Origin (1492 DR)**: a House Sosuke scion "sold for profit" — ruined by the [[CassalanterFamily]] and trafficked into [[BaldursGate]]'s Upper City velvet cages. At sixteen she derived her core law: "Love is a weapon, and desire is a leash." When the Nautiloid crashed she **seduced the illithid parasite** rather than fleeing it.
- **The Audit of the Hells**: she "audited" the Blood War instead of fighting it — used the internalized [[CrownOfKarsus]] (absorbed into her blood) to rewrite [[Zariel]]'s loyalty, consumed her solar fire, and hollowed out [[Asmodeus]] into "a porcelain puppet speaking with her voice." A direct continuation of the [[Divine-Portfolio-Consumption]] / [[DivineAscensionByConsumption]] mechanics from earlier campaign versions.
- **Sovereignty of the Abyss**: enslaved Orcus, Graz'zt, and Demogorgon at a summit via soul-fueled Mass Dominate; industrialized the 666 layers into "Breeding Paradises" — soul-production factories. Reached "Level 60 capability," outpacing the gods.

### The Hard Reset — Ao's intervention

Her miscalculation: assuming [[Ao]] didn't care. When she prepared to invade Mount Celestia and "Invoice the Heavens," Ao enacted the **[[GeometricFirewall]]** — not destruction (killing her would collapse the Hells and Abyss into a black hole, so deeply had she woven herself into the Lower Planes) but **isolation**: Nocturne sealed inside the Shattered Rose with her dominion intact, severed from the Material Plane. History calls it "the Sovereign's Vanishing." Her Material-Plane empire collapsed instantly; House Sosuke was hunted to extinction by fearful gods and rival lords. Aizen is the debris of that collapse, born in exile carrying the accursed name.

## Notable structural features

- **Sequel-as-prequel-inversion**: earlier campaigns built Aizen up toward ascension; this one starts *after* an ascension already happened, failed, and was quarantined — the [[PostVictoryDarkTimeline]] pattern applied to the player's own dynasty rather than to BG3 companions.
- **Ao as balance-enforcer, not combatant**: the Geometric Firewall extends [[DivineDetectionTiers]]/[[DivineImposition]] thinking — the overgod acts only when a player becomes a Singularity who is "deleting the board" rather than playing the Great Game.
- The document truncates mid-sentence ("she cannot physically touc[h]..."), so the mechanical consequences of the seal for gameplay are only partially specified in v1.
