# Campaign Bible: Blades of the High and Low (campaign veleria iseki reborn)

**Source**: Campaign setup document "campaign veleria iseki reborn" (self-titled *Spellblade Valeria Campaign — Updated Input Artifact*). A grounded martial-fantasy progression campaign starring [[ValeriaVex]], a fifteen-year-old border baron's daughter and [[RoyalKnightAcademy]] squire who is secretly the reincarnation of the legendary [[WarriorQueen]]. Starting level 6, class [[SpellbladeGestalt]] (Fighter + Bladesinger Wizard as one integrated style).

## Premise

One thousand years ago the [[WarriorQueen]] unified the surface kingdoms through martial perfection and [[AetherPrimordialEnergy|Aether]] mastery, then vanished. Her advisors converted fragments of her research into floating plateau-cities, birthing the [[Highlanders]] — an elite that now monopolizes high magic, advanced craftsmanship, and political authority over the lowland kingdoms (the [[HighlanderLowlanderDivide]]). Lowlanders are legally and bloodline-restricted to half-caster or martial traditions; Valeria's Aether bypasses those restrictions entirely.

## Design pillars

- **Grounded martial fantasy**: duels, tactical combat, training, rivalries, Aether mastery, discovery, and *choosing which challenges are worth accepting*. Politics and family pressure generate situations, but Valeria is explicitly NOT required to become a ruler, administrator, or warrior queen again.
- **No mindless monsters**: every threat is a person — knights, duelists, mage-hunters, inquisitors, rival nobles, commanders, spies, mercenaries, Highlander elites.
- **Powerful but not apex**: Valeria is far more capable than her public identity suggests, but not automatically the strongest person in every meaningful situation. Challenge comes from opponent preparation, objectives, terrain, time pressure, and counterplay — never from secretly inflating enemy numbers.

## Valeria's character

The reincarnation *rejects* her former life: her previous existence was consumed by administration, decrees, and the weight of governing. This life is about freedom, mastery, hard fights, and deciding for herself what her power means. She fears stagnation, not responsibility — but refuses responsibility that becomes permanent ownership of other people's lives. Personality: calm under pressure, direct, impatient with ceremony, animated about swordplay and tactics, morally serious (no casual acceptance of preventable civilian deaths). Habits: tests the balance point of utensils while thinking, reads posture/grip/escape-routes instinctively, trains before dawn, deliberately underperforms to avoid obligations.

## Core mechanic: witnessed reveals

The [[WitnessedRevealMechanic]] governs hidden power throughout play: (1) Valeria appears limited or merely exceptional → (2) opponent assumes based on what they've seen → (3) Valeria chooses to keep hiding, reveal a portion, or fully escalate → (4) a witnessed reveal creates surprise → (5) observers become intimidated/cautious/respectful/counter-planning → (6) later encounters reflect what each *individual* actually witnessed. Explicit rule: a reveal must never become a global suspicion meter — one witness may be intimidated while an unseen rival still treats her as an ordinary squire.

## Gestalt integration rules

The [[SpellbladeGestalt]] must feel like one style, not two class lists: weapon attacks create openings for Aether techniques; defensive reactions enable ripostes/repositioning; concentration and action economy force real choices; Aether escalation may reveal more than intended; mundane mastery lets her win while preserving the squire disguise. The GM is instructed NOT to arbitrarily reduce her to an ordinary level-6 character.

## Relation to other campaigns

Third 2026-08-04 campaign bible in the hidden-power lineage: [[SaitaAethelgard]]'s herald bluff (undercover royal in Baldur's Gate) and the shy lich Ains ([[MaskOfFragility]] deception DC) share the concealed-apex-identity premise, but Valeria's version is distinctive for its *per-witness knowledge tracking* and its anti-ruler stance — the power fantasy is mastery and freedom, explicitly not governance.
