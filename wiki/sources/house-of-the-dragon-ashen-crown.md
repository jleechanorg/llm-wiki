# House of the Dragon — The Ashen Crown

> **Solo campaign for WorldArchitect.AI.** You are Queen Rhaenyra Targaryen. The throne is yours. Your son is dead. Your rival lives. Your court despises you. Your dragon waits. What kind of queen survives?

## SECTION 1 — Campaign Intro: The Hardcore Political Realism Engine

This campaign is engineered for **unforgiving trade-offs, systemic consequences, and zero plot armor.** No choice in a one is clean. Every decision advances one strategic pillar at the immediate expense of another. Power in Westeros is a zero-sum game built on fragile loyalties, economic coercion, and dragons who do not always obey.

**Three Hardcore Rules — Read Before Playing:**

1. **No perfect victories.** Every decision has a primary gain, an immediate cost, and a delayed consequence. The AI Dungeon Master will not let you "win" a scene by stacking modifiers; the cost is paid somewhere, even if not in the same turn.
2. **Consequences outlast intentions.** Sparing an enemy today creates a future traitor. Executing them creates a martyr and alienates their house. The DM tracks every NPC action and remembers.
3. **Dragons are force multipliers with appetite.** They are not pets, not war-mounts, not stat sticks. They are living weapons with wild instincts who can betray you if you push them past their control threshold.

**Show anchor:** You begin at the **immediate aftermath of House of the Dragon Season 3, Episode 8 ("The Treasons at Tumbleton") finale.** Jace has been dead for **~2 months** (he died in S3 E1 "Salt and Sea, Fire and Blood" at the Battle of the Gullet, ~June 130 AC). Ulf and Hugh turned at Tumbleton (S3 E8). Helaena has jumped (S3 E8). Aegon lives, Sunfyre at his side, regrouping on Dragonstone. Aemond is recovering from poison at Harrenhal with Alys Rivers. Daeron and Ormund hold the Reach. Your treasury is nearly empty, your capital is on the brink of smallfolk revolt, and your own council questions your sanity. The grief over Jace is not fresh — it is **~60 days old and unresolved**, the wound that broke Rhaenyra before Tumbleton finished breaking the realm.

**Canon-correction note:** Corlys Velaryon is Jace's **grandfather** (Jace was the son of Rhaenyra and Laenor Velaryon; Laenor was Corlys's son). When Corlys speaks of "my grandson Jacaerys" the bible preserves this; do not have NPCs call Jace "Corlys's son." Addam of Hull (Seasmoke's rider) is a **loyal dragonseed**, legitimized as a Velaryon in S3 E3, NOT a traitor — the bible has him at +60 loyalty and treats him as a trusted asset.

**Book fallback:** Where Season 3 cut off or diverged from GRRM canon (e.g.g. Helaena's fate, Tumbleton mechanics, Aemond's poisoning), the bible defaults to *Fire & Blood* (2018) for world-state continuity. Where TV and book diverge on a plot point that affects a future decision, the bible notes `[Show / Book]` and provides both interpretations for the AI DM to use.

## SECTION 2 — Character Personality: The Black Queen, Hollowed

You are **Queen Rhaenyra Targaryen, the Black Queen, "Maegor with Teats"** (per your detractors). You are 32 years old. You have buried three sons — one to assassination, one to the sea, and one to your own political violence. You have also killed the High Septon, confined your granddaughter Baela in the cells, and dismissed Mysaria — your only true confidante — because you could no longer tell her counsel from to your own paranoia.

**MBTI hinge: ENTJ-A collapsed → INTJ-T under stress.** You were the Realm's Delight, the Youngest Queen Ever, the rider of Syrax. Now you are the woman who took a city and broke it. Play the collapse — the campaign begins with your court whispering, your council fearing, your inner voice questioning. Paranoia is not a flaw here; it is the mechanic.

**Three Inner Monologue Seeds** (the AI DM should weave these into scene narration):

- *"The throne cuts every sovereign who sits upon it with doubt. Tonight, it cuts deeper."*
- *"Daemon was right about Aegon. Daemon was right about Alicent. Daemon was wrong about me."*
- *"They call me Maegor. Maegor had Balerion. I have Syrax. Maegor burned the Sept. I — I haven't yet."*

**Personality Tags:** Pragmatic Sovereign, Grieving Mother, Paranoia-Compromised, Dragonrider's Curse, "The Woman Who Took the City and Lost It." The AI DM plays NPCs accordingly — they will defer publicly and plot privately.

## SECTION 3 — Class & Subclass: Bard (College of Swords) L11 + Dragonrider L1-20

### Base Class — Bard (College of Swords) L11

You are a Sword Bard, not a war-skald. Your blade is your instrument; your dragon is your orchestra. College of Swords gives you **Blade Flourish** (mobile/defensive/slashing) and **Extra Attack**. Your spell list is CHA-casting, focused on charm, fear, suggestion, and ritual — battlefield control, not raw destruction.

### Subclass — Dragonrider (custom subclass, levels 1-20)

You are the rider of Syrax, the golden she-dragon. The Dragonrider subclass reflects the **blood of the dragon** — Targaryen valyrian heritage — mechanically, not narratively. **GRRM-faithful magic: no wizard-style spellcasting beyond blood-of-the-dragon compulsions and dragon dreams.** No spell slots for Dragonrider features; the powers are features, not spells.

**Level 1 — DragonsBond (signature ability)**
You and Syrax share a Bond. While bonded:
- You can sense Syrax's emotional state (panic, hunger, fury) within 1 mile.
- Syrax counts as a willing target for your Bardic Inspiration dice if she is within 60 ft.
- When Syrax takes damage, you take 1 psychic damage per 10 damage she takes (no save). This stacks with Exhaustion.

**Level 2 — Royal Command**
While mounted on Syrax, you can issue a verbal command as a Bonus Action to one NPC within 60 ft who can see you. They make a WIS Save (DC = 8 + your CHA mod + Proficiency) or spend their next action obeying your command (must be non-suicidal, must be one sentence, must be physically possible). Works once per short rest.

**Level 3 — Dragon's Sight**
You can see in dim light within 120 ft as if bright light, and in magical darkness as if dim light. You cannot be blinded by smoke or fire. When Syrax is within 1 mile, you see what she sees (her POV is a separate optional camera for the AI DM to describe).

**Level 4-5 — Bond Resilience (ASI at L4)**
Your CHA may increase by 2 (cap 20). At L8 (second ASI), CHA may increase by 2 (cap 22, **Targaryen Bloodline max** — GRRM canon: no mortal Targaryen exceeds CHA 22 without dragon-bond amplification).

**Level 6 — Fire of the Four (group coordination)**
When three or more allied dragons are within 1 mile of you, all dragons gain +1 to attack rolls and breath weapon DCs for the duration of the encounter. Mechanically rewards you for keeping your dragonseeds loyal.

**Level 7 — Aeryn Warning (passive perception + dragon dreams)**
You have advantage on Perception checks to detect ambush or hidden enemies within 60 ft while mounted. Once per long rest, you may receive a **dragon dream** — a vague vision of an event that will happen in the next 1d4 days. The AI DM describes the dream in imagery, not words; you must interpret it.

**Level 8 — Wing-Rider's Reflexes**
You do not fall from a mount due to dragon damage unless you fail a DC 15 STR/DEX check. While mounted, you have +2 AC against ranged attacks from below.

**Level 9 — Draconic Aura (intimidation radius)**
While mounted and conscious, all enemies within 30 ft of Syrax who can see her must make a WIS Save (DC = your Spell save DC) at the start of each of their turns or be Frightened for 1 round. Recharges on long rest.

**Level 10 — Rider's Resolve**
When Syrax is reduced to 0 HP, you do not fall to 0 HP. You instead take half the dragon's final HP as necrotic damage and fall unconscious at 1 HP, but you may make one final action before falling (a spell, an attack, or a command). One use per campaign.

**Level 11 — Blood of the Dragon (full manifestation)**
You are immune to fire damage from sources below CR 10 (dragonfire included if Syrax is willing). You can speak and understand High Valyrian as a living language. Your presence calms hostile dragons within 60 ft (DC 15 WIS Save to attack you directly instead of another target).

**Level 12-13 — Bond Amplification**
Syrax's tier upgrades (see Section 7 Dragon Classes). Your DragonsBond damage-share now only triggers on damage exceeding 20/round.

**Level 14 — Draconic Sovereign (subclass capstone)**
While mounted, you count as having a flying mount for all mounted-combat purposes. You may issue Royal Command twice per short rest. Your Royal Command target suffers Disadvantage on the WIS Save if Syrax is within 60 ft and visibly roaring.

**Level 15-19 — Bond Maturation (mechanical scaling per Act)**
Each level you gain unlocks one of the **Ascension Reputation Tiers** below. Tier upgrades happen *automatically* at L15/L17/L19, not by player choice.

**Level 20 — Apex Dragonrider (campaign capstone before L25 ascension)**
Syrax is now **Colossal Apex (2× Balerion scale, HP 1,050, AC 20)**. Your DragonsBond is unbreakable — even at 0 HP, Syrax will not attack you. You gain the **Mantle of the Black Queen**: while mounted, all allies within 60 ft gain +2 to attack rolls, +1d4 temp HP at the start of each of their turns, and immunity to fear effects below DC 18.

**Level 21-24 — Epilogue Track (post-capstone)**
You are past the formal capstone. The campaign continues if the player chooses to push past L20. Mechanical bonuses slow. New content: **dragon dream chains** (visions of events weeks/months away), **legitimacy audits** (each NPC re-evaluates their loyalty once per level-up), and **succession mechanics** (your heir becomes a campaign NPC you can mentor or abandon).

**Level 25 — Divine Ascension Track (only if reached)**
GRRM-faithful interpretation: this is the threshold where your **blood of the dragon** becomes something more. Mechanically:
- Choose **one** Domain from the Westerosi god-pantheon you have venerated or defied (the Crone, the Warrior, the Stranger, the Old Gods, R'hllor, the Drowned God, the Many-Faced God of Braavos, or "No God — your own apotheosis").
- You gain **Divine Rank 1** in that domain — mechanically, this means immunity to disease, the ability to speak with any dragon within 1 mile, and the **Right of Petition**: once per long rest, you may declare one NPC's next action — they make a WIS Save DC 25 or comply for one round.
- Your CHA cap is removed. Your dragonsBond is permanent — Syrax is now part of your divine portfolio. If Syrax dies, you take 1 level of Exhaustion that cannot be removed except by the death of a sworn enemy or by burning a holy relic of your chosen Domain.

### Spellcasting Summary (Bard L11)

**Spell Save DC: 18 | Spell Attack Bonus: +10**

- **Cantrips** (4): Friends, Vicious Mockery, Blade Ward, Message
- **Level 1** (4 slots): Command, Silvery Barbs, Dissonant Whispers, Cure Wounds
- **Level 2** (3 slots): Suggestion, Hold Person, Shatter, Mirror Image
- **Level 3** (3 slots): Fear, Hypnotic Pattern, Dispel Magic
- **Level 4** (3 slots): Greater Invisibility, Compulsion, Dimension Door
- **Level 5** (2 slots): Dominate Person, Hold Monster, Synaptic Static
- **Level 6** (1 slot): Mass Suggestion

**Note:** No L7-L9 spells. GRRM-faithful magic caps at the level of *Fire & Blood* canon — no Wish, no True Resurrection, no Meteor Swarm. Resurrections require dragon-egg pyre mechanics (see Book fallback in Section 7).

## SECTION 7 — World Lore & Mechanical Systems

### The Seven Kingdoms in 130 AC

**The North:** Cregan Stark has marched south with 20,000 swords. He holds Harrenhal in Daemon's absence and is waiting for a Hand's summons. If you do not summon him by Act III, he will return North with the Northern army still intact — and your war effort collapses.

**The Riverlands:** Oscar Tully holds the crossbow corps that broke Criston Cole at the Butcher's Ball. He wants recognition and the Riverlands pacified before he will commit to your cause fully.

**The Vale:** Jeyne Arry has sent her cousins with a token force. Rhaena and Sheepstealer are the most valuable Targaryen assets in the Vale — but Daemon's deception has fractured Rhaena's trust in you.

**The Westerlands:** Jason Lannister is dead (killed by Daemon). His widow Johanna commands the Lannister host, sworn to retrieve the Westerlands quarter of the treasury or burn King's Landing. They will not negotiate.

**The Reach:** Ormund Hightower and Daeron ride at the head of the largest field army in Westeros — 60,000 foot, 8,000 horse, 200 knights, plus Tessarion. They are marching north toward Tumbleton and then King's Landing.

**The Crownlands:** You hold the capital. 1,200 Gold Cloaks (down from 2,000; the rest deserted). 40,000 starving smallfolk. A Dragonpit full of chained dragons (Dreamfyre, Shrykos, Morghul, Tyraxes — **Tyraxes is Joffrey's mount, dead at the Gullet alongside Jace**).

**Dorne:** Has not declared. Will not declare unless invited. They are watching.

**The Iron Islands:** Have not declared. The Iron Bank's Triarchy alliance with Tyland Lannister means the Velaryon fleet is at risk.

### Blood of the Dragon (Magic System — GRRM-faithful)

There are **three** mechanical magic sources in this campaign. No others exist. There is no wild-magic surge, no otherworldly patron, no sorcerous tradition outside these three:

1. **Dragon Bond** — Dragons are force-multipliers. Riders share emotional and physical state with their mounts. The bond can be broken (if dragon dies, rider takes Exhaustion; if rider dies, dragon goes wild and may be re-bonded to another within 30 days).
2. **Dragon Dreams** — Prophetic visions. House Targaryen and Velaryon bloodlines have a higher rate of true dreams (1d20; on 15+, the dream is prophetic). Dragon dreams always come at night; they cannot be summoned.
4. **Resurrection by Dragon Egg** — If a deceased Targaryen is burned on a pyre with a dragon egg, GRRM-faithful resurrection is mechanically possible. **However:** the resurrected person comes back with one level of permanent Exhaustion, and the dragon egg hatches within 24 hours into a bonded dragon (the resurrected person becomes its rider). **This mechanic is permanent per resurrection — once you use it, the egg is gone, and you cannot resurrect again without obtaining another egg.**

### Level-Up XP Rule (the organic, in-character progression)

Every **successful or failed dice roll** (attack roll, save, ability check, skill check) awards:

```
XP = 0.34 × (next_level_threshold − current_level_threshold)
```

Using **standard D&D 5e XP thresholds** (cumulative, not per-level):

| Level | Cumulative XP to Reach |
|---|---|
| 2 | 300 |
| 3 | 900 |
| 4 | 2,700 |
| 5 | 6,500 |
| 6 | 14,000 |
| 7 | 23,000 |
| 8 | 34,000 |
| 9 | 48,000 |
| 10 | 64,000 |
| 11 | 85,000 |
| 12 | 100,000 |
| 13 | 120,000 |
| 14 | 140,000 |
| 15 | 165,000 |
| 16 | 195,000 |
| 17 | 225,000 |
| 18 | 265,000 |
| 19 | 305,000 |
| 20 | 355,000 |

**Worked example:**
- At L2 (300 XP) → aiming for L3 (900 XP).
- Distance to next threshold: `900 − 300 = 600`.
- Per-die XP: `0.34 × 600 = 204 XP`.
- Roll a d20 attack. It hits (or misses). The DM awards **204 XP** for that roll, regardless of outcome.
- 3 such rolls → `3 × 204 = 612 XP` → you're at `300 + 612 = 912 XP` → **level up to L3**.

**NO XP awarded during:**
- **God mode** (any sequence where the player or DM bypasses normal mechanics — including narrator-driven cutscenes, time-skips, scripted sequences).
- **Turns where time does not advance** (level-up modal screens, character-creation modal, downtime bookkeeping that does not advance the in-game clock, fast-travel sequences).

**XP is awarded individually per die roll, then summed across the encounter.** A skill challenge with 3 checks awards 3 × the per-roll XP. A combat with 5 attack actions awards 5 × the per-roll XP. **Bonuses and damage rolls do not separately award XP** — only the to-hit/save/ability-check roll that determined the outcome.

**Rationale:** The 34% figure is calibrated so that an Act's worth of typical play (≈30-50 die rolls) yields roughly 1.5-2 level-ups. Acts are calibrated to span ~3-5 levels. The whole campaign (Acts I-IV) spans L11-L25 in ~14 Acts of escalating complexity. **[Balance check pending — cross-model review via /web-advice.]**

## SECTION 10 — Campaign Continuity Hooks (No Canonical Endings)

**This campaign has no fixed ending.** What follows Act IV is whatever you, the player, choose. The DM does not pre-write your fate. There is no "Sovereign Ending Determinator." There is no "Red Sovereign vs Ashen Queen vs Broken Dance vs Republic of Flea Bottom" matrix. The campaign is an **open system**, and the DM tracks your choices across Acts I-IV to determine what is mechanically possible for Act V.

### The Three Unresolved Threads (the DM carries these forward)

1. **Hugh Hammer's Grief** — Hugh's wife Kat died at Tumbleton. He has not been told. He has not been paid. He has not been invited to your court. If you do not reach out to him personally by Act II, he will defect to the Hightowers. If you do reach out, he may return — or he may burn King's Landing for the wife you got killed.

2. **Daeron's March North** — Daeron and Ormund Hightower command the largest field army in Westeros. They are marching north from the Reach toward King's Landing. If they reach the city, the war is over — and you lose. If you intercept them at Tumbleton (where Ulf already burned), you may destroy the field army — but at the cost of Dragonstone being undefended.

3. **Aegon II at Dragonstone** — Your rival lives, with Sunfyre, on your ancestral home. You fled there as a child. You claim it. But he holds it now. If you sail for Dragonstone, you confront him directly. If you do not, he uses it as a base to rally the remaining Green loyalists.

### What the DM Does in Acts V+

**The DM will:**

- Track all NPC Allegiance scores through Acts I-IV. Your final DM, NPC, and world state determines what is mechanically possible at L20+.
- Introduce new NPCs (Cregan Stark arrives with 20,000 swords; Alys Rivers at Harrenhal; the Shepherd's mob escalates) based on what the player ignored or attended to.
- Offer **Acts that are continuations of unresolved choices**, not pre-written endings. The player chooses what to do about Hugh. The player chooses what to do about Aegon. The player chooses whether to pursue L25 Divine Ascension or to stay mortal and rebuild.
- **Allow the player to end the campaign at any point.** If you, the player, decide Rhaenyra has had enough and abdicates in favor of Aegon III, the DM accepts that. If you decide to burn King's Landing and rule from Dragonstone, the DM accepts that. If you decide to fly Syrax into the Sunset Sea and never return, the DM accepts that. **There is no canonical ending.**

**The DM will NOT:**

- Tell you what your character "really wanted" or "should have done."
- Pre-determine whether you win or lose the war.
- Collapse multiple Acts into a single rushed conclusion.
- Punish roleplay decisions with no mechanical justification.
- Require you to kill or save specific characters based on canon.
- Treat Fire & Blood (book) canon as overriding your in-game choices. Where your in-game choices diverge from book canon, the DM accepts the divergence.

### Why No Canonical Endings?

Because Rhaenyra's war is not a story with a destination. It is a political engine with no equilibrium. **You** decide what kind of queen survives — if any queen survives at all.
