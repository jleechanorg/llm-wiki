# The Eagle's Brood: The Serpent & The Sun — A Complete Campaign Guide

**Source**: Campaign world document "world rome - The Pax Julia - Gaia julia" — the alternate-history setting bible for the Rome campaign line (cf. [[GaiaJuliaCaesar]], [[AureliusJuliaCaesar]]). Point of divergence: the Ides of March, 44 BCE, when the assassination of [[GaiusJuliusCaesar]] FAILS and he remakes Rome as a Roman-Egyptian god-empire.

## Core Premise

Caesar is haunted by the [[FatedMiasma]] — a hereditary connection to the chaos of destiny itself (prophetic visions of alternate timelines). His ambition is not mere power but a *stable reality*: a single timeline free from the maddening whispers of what could be. To achieve it he shatters Republican tradition, embraces divinity, and fuses Roman iron with Egyptian mysticism, founding the Julian-Ptolemaic dynasty of philosopher-kings and warrior-queens.

## Timeline: The Julian-Ptolemaic Century (44 BCE – 56 AD)

### First Generation — Age of the God-King (44 BCE – 14 BCE)
- **44 BCE — [[DayOfAscension]]**: Guided by a vision, Caesar preemptively exposes the conspiracy in the Senate; conspirators arrested; Republic suspended under emergency powers.
- **43 BCE — The Legitimization**: Caesar divorces Calpurnia, marries [[CleopatraVII]], and passes the [[LexDeFilioAegypto]] legitimizing [[Caesarion]] (renamed Gaius Julius Caesarion) as Roman citizen and undisputed heir. Outrages the aristocracy; wildly popular with plebeians; Ptolemaic gold floods Rome's treasury.
- **42 BCE — Assassination of Octavius**: [[GaiusOctavius]] becomes the Republican-remnant figurehead; Caesar's agents (likely under [[MarkAntony]]) arrange his "unfortunate accident," extinguishing the old guard's last hope.
- **40–30 BCE — Wars of Unification**: Caesar and Antony crush Pompeian remnants in Spain, then triumph over Parthia (avenging Crassus). Cleopatra rules as regent in Rome, seeding Egyptian culture and religion in the capital.
- **29 BCE — The Divine Triumph**: Caesar deified as *Divus Julius*; temples to Venus Genetrix and Isis; Rome becomes a cosmopolitan Hellenistic capital.
- **14 BCE — Passing of the Eagle**: Caesar dies of old age after three decades as a living god. Primogeniture established; throne AND the full burden of the [[FatedMiasma]] pass to Caesarion.

### Second Generation — Age of the Sun-Prince (14 BCE – 37 AD)
- **14 BCE — Accession of Caesarion**: Imperator Caesarion Augustus, man of two worlds (Roman general's training, Egyptian queen's blood), charming, multilingual patron of arts and sciences — but afflicted more deeply by the Miasma than his father; Dowager-Empress Cleopatra remains his most trusted advisor.
- **14 BCE – 10 AD — The Golden Age**: Egyptian wealth funds massive public works; [[Alexandria]] becomes a second capital rivaling Rome as a global center of learning; the syncretic [[CultOfSerapisJupiter]] becomes de facto state religion.
